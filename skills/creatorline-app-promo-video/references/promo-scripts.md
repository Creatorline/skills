# Promo scripts

How to write the spoken script of an app or website promo, tie every beat to a real screenshot,
and turn it into the brief for the take. The general brief rules are in video-briefs.md; this
file is what is particular to apps and websites.

## 1. The shape

Three parts, and the voice never stops between them:

| Part | Share of the runtime | Job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 8 to 12 % (at most about 2 s) | One bold, believable claim | The face, the hook plate |
| Body | about 70 % | The site solves it: one beat per screenshot | A card per beat, the face between them |
| Closer | about 15 % | A concrete result, then a short verdict | The face, the call to action text |

| Length | Hook | Body | Closer | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 s | about 1 s | about 7 s | about 1.5 s | 3 to 4 |
| 15 s (recommended) | 1.2 to 1.8 s | about 10.5 s | about 2.3 s | about 6 |
| 30 s | about 2 s | about 21 s | about 4.5 s | up to 10 |
| 45 s | about 2 s | about 31 s | about 7 s | up to 10 |

Up to 30 s is one take. 45 s is one call too: the server films joined takes, and each join can
shift the face a little, so the Framing and Setting lines must stay word for word the same
(section 10).

## 2. How many words

The take speaks at 2.4 to 2.7 words a second. Aim for the middle; an overfull script is the
main reason a take comes back talking far too fast.

| Length | Words | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| 10 s | 22 to 26 | 26 |
| 11 to 12 s | 28 to 33 | 33 |
| 13 to 15 s | 35 to 40 | 40 |
| 30 s | 70 to 80 | 80 |
| 45 s | 105 to 120 | 120 |

Count before you price. Over the ceiling: cut a beat, never squeeze the lines.

## 3. The hook

- **One clause, at most 8 words, about 2 seconds.** A bold claim that is still believable.
- **It carries a hook token in its first breath:** a number, a price, a percentage, a time, a
  contrast, or a named result. A fact about the app comes from a screenshot, the page or the
  person; a personal result comes from the persona's own use and is welcome (section 4).
- **The site's name is a separate beat.** The hook names the brand only inside its claim
  ("Fernly is free and sorts itself"); the first body beat names it properly.
- **Never open with a pointing intro** ("This is", "Here's", "Meet", "I found", "Let me show
  you", "Say hello to") **or a filler** ("OK", "So", "Wait", "Um", "Well", "Like").
- It says more than the picture: a face talking is not news, the claim is.
- The avatar's face is at its most lively here.
- **The hook plate** is the same hook in 6 words or fewer, in capitals, with no emoji. Shorten
  the spoken hook for it when it runs longer.

## 4. Hook angles

Pick one and write your own line. A number about the app comes from the page, the screenshots
or the person; a number from the persona's own use is its own.

| Angle | Fits when | Shape of a line |
|---|---|---|
| A personal result | The persona's own use makes the point: time back, a habit, money kept | "This app gave me back 10 hours last month." |
| Price contrast | The page prints a low price or a free tier | "A full budget app for zero dollars." |
| Looks premium | The design is the selling point and the price is printed | "Looks this good for 9 dollars." |
| A number people trust | The page prints a count: users, reviews, templates | "2,000 reviews and still 4.8 stars." |
| Stop overpaying | The page says what it replaces | "Stop paying for three planning apps." |
| The result | A screenshot shows the finished result | "A week of dinners, planned in one tap." |
| Speed | The page states the time or the screenshots show how few steps | "Booked in three taps, start to finish." |

A personal result ("this site saved me 10 hours") is a proven hook: the persona has a past and
its results are welcome (house-rules.md, section 7). Make it specific: a number, a time, what it
replaced. A line that tells the viewer to look at something needs the card that shows it, and
the hook has no card, so a "look at this" line belongs in the body.

## 5. The body: one beat per card

- **The first body beat names the site,** and the hero card (the screenshot with the logo and
  the navigation bar) comes in on it: "Ok so, it's called Fernly."
- **Then one beat per card, in card order** (screen-layouts.md, section 6): what the product
  is, the app at work, the reviews, the specs, the price.
- **Each beat talks about the card that is up.** Reviews while the reviews card shows; the
  price while the pricing card shows. A number is said only while the card that prints it is
  on screen.
- **Never promise a screen you do not have.** A feature with no screenshot is left out.
- **Skip the filler sections:** the footer, the logo strip of press or partners, the cookie
  banner.
- At 15 s a body beat is about 4 to 7 words. More cards come from more screenshots and more
  beats, never from longer beats.
- Say what it does, not how it feels: "Purchases land in categories on their own," not "it's
  so easy". The persona's own use can ride along ("I check it every morning").

## 6. The closer

- **A concrete result,** what the viewer ends up with ("Your whole week, sorted before
  Monday.") or what the persona ended up with ("My whole month, sorted before payday."), then
  **a short verdict** with eye contact ("Honestly? The first budget I'd keep
  open."). No new number.
- **No card** during the closer: it is the face.
- **The action:** an online store with a product photo from the person: she lifts the
  product into frame now, and only now. Anything else (an app, a service): a small natural
  gesture, a nod or a point down toward the call to action.
- **The call to action is on-screen text** over the closer and a line in the post caption.
  Our writer turns a spoken call to action into a verdict, so it is never one of the lines.

## 7. Making it sound alive

- **Contractions,** always.
- **One or two connectors** from: "ok so", "honestly", "no joke", "and get this", "here's the
  thing". Never as the hook's first words.
- **Mix short lines (2 to 4 words) with longer ones.**
- **Exactly one live, imperfect moment:** a self-correction or a quick aside ("this part's my
  favourite").
- **Change the speed inside the take:** quicker through connecting lines, slower on the
  number, the price and the last line. An even pace, fast or slow, sounds like a robot. Say it
  in the Delivery line of the brief.
- **Perform the peak:** the key word (usually the number or the result) is drawn out a little,
  said in the Delivery line, never respelled in the line. At most one or two words in capitals
  per line. One sentence that breaks off at the hardest beat ("It just... sorts itself."). One
  swing from a near whisper to a spike. Over the take: flat, then a spike, then settled. A
  person's size, never an infomercial voice. At most one deliberate pause, in the hook or the
  closer.
- **Two or three small emphasis moments** spread across the take (a small laugh, an
  "honestly", a lean toward the lens), not all at the start.
- **Register:** natural by default. Hyped only when the ask says hype; calm when it says
  premium, clinical or luxury.
- Skip filler the writer removes anyway ("literally", "game changer", "obsessed").

## 8. Claims for apps and websites

The brand is responsible for its claims, and the avatar is a living persona (house-rules.md,
section 7).

| Welcome | The app's facts: from the page, a screenshot or the person, as given | Never |
|---|---|---|
| What the screenshots show, while they show it | Ratings, review, download or user counts | A rating badge, review, notification or chat drawn into the picture (it renders garbled: a real screenshot or Video Studio text instead) |
| What the app's page states | Rankings, awards, "number one" | A screen of another company's app or an unrelated page |
| How it works ("you tap, it books") | Comparisons with a named competitor | A drawn or generated screen shown as the real app |
| Opinions ("honestly, it's so clean") | Launch offers and discount codes | Guaranteed income or a return; a promise that it cures, treats or prevents a disease |
| The persona's own use: how long, how often, what it got from it ("I've used it every day since March", "it saved me 10 hours last month") | The price | |

- An approved claims list is an option, never a condition (product-intake.md, section 4). When
  the person gives one, keep to it: word for word, never stronger, never joined. Before you
  price, read every line, the hook plate and the call to action against it, and delete what is
  not on it. Never put a new claim in the place of one you deleted.
- Bold and specific beats vague: every claim carries one concrete thing (a number, a time, the
  card that shows it). "It changed my life" is weak because it is vague: say what changed.
- Finance, health and money-making apps get the persona like any other (its routine, its own
  result), with the one legal line: no promise of guaranteed income or a return, and no promise
  that it cures, treats or prevents a disease (house-rules.md, section 7).

## 9. The script-to-card map

Write it before the price, keep it for the edit:

| Part | Line | Cue word | Card | Seconds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | "..." | none | none (face + hook plate) | 0 to about 1.6 |
| Body 1 | "Ok so, it's called <App>." | the app's name | c1: the hero, logo and navigation bar | 1.4 |
| Body 2 | "..." | the feature noun | c2: the feature | 1.4 |
| Body 3 | "..." | ... | c3: the app at work | 1.4 |
| Body 4 | "..." | ... | c4: the reviews or the price | 1.4 |
| Closer | "..." | none | none (face + call to action) | the last 15 % |

More screenshots than beats: keep the hero, then the strongest, in page order. Tell the person
which you left out.

## 10. The brief

Plain sentences in this order. Name only what is in the picture; the app's name lives in the
quoted lines and the Product line.

```
Idea: @Avatar1 talks to camera about <App>, <what it does> for <who>, in one continuous take, like talking to a friend, not like a narrator or a voiceover.
Framing: a medium shot from mid-torso up, @Avatar1 about two thirds of the frame, head centred left to right and top to bottom, eyes into the lens, hands gesturing in the upper part of the frame.
Setting: the phone propped on a stand on <a lived-in couch / a desk / a kitchen counter>, <natural window light / a warm lamp>, the same place all the way through.
Delivery: natural hand gestures, <a natural American accent>, quicker through the connecting lines, slower on <the number> and the last line, most energy on the hook. Her hands are empty and free.
Hook: "<the hook>"
Lines (say these as written, in this order; the edit shows what each one names): "<hook>" "<body 1>" "<body 2>" ... "<closer>"
Takeaway: <the concrete thing the app does>
Product: <App>, <one line>; its real screens are added in the edit. Page: <url>
Ending: <the closer's action>, a calm verdict with eye contact.
Language: <spoken language>
Sound: her voice and the room's own quiet sound, close and slightly compressed like a phone's front microphone; voice and room sound only.
```

- **No product in the picture before the closer.** A product named in an action with no photo
  for it gets invented, so the actions never mention or hold it. An online store's closer adds
  the person's product photo in `objects`, and the Ending line says: "@Object1 out of frame
  until the last line; she lifts it into frame."
- **No screen in the picture, ever.** A website, an app, a phone, a laptop, a monitor or a
  browser filmed by the video model comes out with garbled text. Keep those words out of the
  Idea, Framing, Setting, Delivery and Ending lines; say what is there instead ("a couch, a
  lamp, a plain wall").
- **Accent:** for English lines, "a natural American accent" unless the person or the brand's
  country asks for another. A requested accent is described in words, never spelled out, and
  written a notch stronger than asked ("slight Southern" becomes "a clear Southern accent").
- **Language:** the brand's `language`. It is the person's own choice for this brand, so it
  stands even though English lip-syncs best; another language only on request.
- `params`: `ugc_style: "Auto"`, `duration` (10, 15, 30 or 45), `aspect_ratio: "9:16"`,
  `resolution: "720p"`. `Talking head` only when the person wants a tighter selfie that
  pushes in each sentence; it fights the medium framing the cards need.

## 11. Setting by site type

| Site type | Setting |
|---|---|
| SaaS, service, portfolio, content site | A home desk or a living room, someone who works from there |
| Online store, marketplace | The room its products belong in: the bathroom shelf for skincare, the kitchen for food, the bedroom for fashion |

- Premium by default: a tidy, well-kept room. An everyday, mass-market store: an ordinary
  lived-in room.
- One setting for the whole video, in the same words in every brief.

## 12. Check before you price

- **Hook:** at most 8 words, a token in the first breath, no pointing intro or filler first,
  says more than the picture. Hook plate at most 6 words.
- **Script:** inside the word band; contractions; one or two connectors; short and long lines
  mixed; one imperfect moment; a performed peak; each body beat matched to a card.
- **Brief:** no cut words ("cut", "shot 2", "panel"); no product in an action before the
  closer; no screen, phone, laptop, browser, website or app in the visual lines; Framing,
  Setting, Delivery (accent and pace) and the phone-microphone Sound line are there.
- **Claims:** section 8, with the approved list when there is one.

## 13. A full example

The person: "Make a promo for our budgeting app Fernly. Site: fernly.example." Five
screenshots after `preview_assets`: c1 the home page with the Fernly logo and the navigation
bar, c2 purchases sorted into categories, c3 "left to spend this week" in large type, c4 the
reviews section printing "4.8 stars from 2,000 reviews", c5 the pricing table with a free tier.
15 s, Both.

The lines (39 words):

1. Hook: "A free budget that sorts itself."
2. Body 1 (c1): "Ok so, it's called Fernly."
3. Body 2 (c2): "Purchases land in categories on their own."
4. Body 3 (c3): "Then, this part's my favourite... what's left to spend."
5. Body 4 (c4): "4.8 stars from 2,000 reviews."
6. Closer: "Honestly? The FIRST budget I'd keep open."

Cue words: "Fernly" (c1), "categories" (c2), "left to spend" (c3), "4.8" (c4). c5 is left out:
the hook already says it is free, which the page prints.

The brief (`prompt`), with `ugc_style: "Auto"`, `duration: 15`:

```
Idea: @Avatar1 talks to camera about Fernly, a budgeting app for people who gave up on spreadsheets, in one continuous take, like talking to a friend, not like a narrator or a voiceover.
Framing: a medium shot from mid-torso up, @Avatar1 about two thirds of the frame, head centred left to right and top to bottom, eyes into the lens, hands gesturing in the upper part of the frame.
Setting: the phone propped on a stand on a tidy home desk by a window, natural daylight, the same place all the way through.
Delivery: natural hand gestures, a natural American accent, quicker through "Ok so, it's called Fernly", slower on "4.8 stars" and the last line, the word "free" drawn out a little, most energy on the hook. Her hands are empty and free.
Hook: "A free budget that sorts itself."
Lines (say these as written, in this order; the edit shows what each one names): "A free budget that sorts itself." "Ok so, it's called Fernly." "Purchases land in categories on their own." "Then, this part's my favourite... what's left to spend." "4.8 stars from 2,000 reviews." "Honestly? The FIRST budget I'd keep open."
Takeaway: Fernly sorts every purchase into categories and shows what is left to spend this week.
Product: Fernly, a budgeting app; its real screens are added in the edit. Page: https://fernly.example
Ending: a small nod toward the bottom of the frame, a calm verdict with eye contact.
Language: English.
Sound: her voice and the room's own quiet sound, close and slightly compressed like a phone's front microphone; voice and room sound only.
```

Hook plate: `A FREE BUDGET\nTHAT SORTS ITSELF`. Call to action text: `Fernly: link in bio`.

## 14. Showing the script to the person

In the price message, after the one-line plan: the lines, numbered, with the card in brackets
after the line it appears on, then the prices. Example:

> A 15-second promo: Mia at her desk talks about Fernly; four of your screenshots pop up as she
> names them; captions on, with the hook at the top.
> 1. "A free budget that sorts itself." (hook, on screen as A FREE BUDGET THAT SORTS ITSELF)
> 2. "Ok so, it's called Fernly." [home]
> 3. "Purchases land in categories on their own." [categories]
> 4. "Then, this part's my favourite... what's left to spend." [left to spend]
> 5. "4.8 stars from 2,000 reviews." [reviews]
> 6. "Honestly? The FIRST budget I'd keep open." On screen: "Fernly: link in bio"
>
> The take <N1> credits, captions <N2>, export <N3>: <total> in all; you have <M>. Go ahead,
> or change a line first?

A change to a line before the yes is free. After the take, a new line means a new take.
