# Promo scripts

How to write the four spoken lines of an app promo, tie each to a real screen, and turn them
into the brief for the take. The general brief rules are in video-briefs.md; this file is what
is particular to apps and websites.

## 1. The four beats

| Beat | Job | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| Problem (the hook) | The pain the viewer knows, or the result, in the first 3 s | The face, plus the hook text |
| App moment | Name the app and show the one thing it does about the problem | The screens, one per feature line |
| Result | What the finished screen shows: the plan made, the total, the booking confirmed | The result screen |
| Verdict | A short opinion about the app, with eye contact | The face, plus the call to action text |

Write the lines as speech: contractions, short sentences, one idea each. Give them to the
writer as "use these lines as written", so the words, and so the cue words that time the
screens, come back unchanged. The writer softens a fact it cannot see on camera into an
opinion, and the screens are added only in the edit, so say in the brief that the edit shows
what each line names (section 8 has the wording).

## 2. Beats by length

People speak about 2.5 words a second (video-briefs.md, section 7). Aim for the middle of the
range.

| Length | Words | Problem | App moment | Result | Verdict | Screens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 s | 22 to 30 | 0 to 3 s | 3 to 7 s | with the moment | 7 to 10 s | 1 to 2 |
| 15 s (default) | 30 to 45 | 0 to 3 s | 3 to 9 s | 9 to 12.5 s | 12.5 to 15 s | 2 to 4 |
| 20 s | 45 to 60 | 0 to 3 s | 3 to 13 s | 13 to 17 s | 17 to 20 s | 3 to 5 |
| 30 s | 65 to 90 | 0 to 3 s, with a second small hook near 15 s | two parts | 24 to 27 s | 27 to 30 s | 4 to 6 |

One take holds up to 30 s on the default model, so every length here is one take: no joins,
no face drift between parts.

## 3. Hooks for apps

The hook names the pain or shows the outcome, mid-sentence, in under 3 seconds. Never a
greeting, never the app's name as the first word, never "So I found this app".

| Angle | Works when | Example line (write your own) |
|---|---|---|
| The everyday pain | The app removes a chore everyone knows | "Your grocery list lives in four different group chats." |
| Old way, new way | There is an obvious before | "Spreadsheet budgets die in February. This one fills itself in." |
| The result first | The result screen is striking | "A whole week of dinners, planned from what's already in the fridge." |
| Who it is for | A clear niche | "If you run a shop on your own, look at this." |
| A fact the page prints | The page states it (a free tier, a number of templates) | "It's free, and it has three hundred workout plans." |
| The question | A real gap in how people do it | "Why does booking a haircut still take five messages?" |

- A line like "fills itself in" or "planned from the fridge" must match a screen the person
  sent. If no screen shows it, pick another angle.
- The hook text on screen adds to the spoken hook in 3 to 6 words; it does not repeat it.

## 4. The app moment and the result

- Name the app once, in the first line of the app moment: "This is Fernly."
- One feature per line, in the present tense, saying what the screen shows: "You snap the
  receipt and it fills in the total." Not "it's so easy to use".
- Each feature line carries its cue word, the word that times its screen (screen-layouts.md,
  section 3).
- The result is shown, not claimed: "And here's the week, every bill in its slot," over the
  screen that shows exactly that.
- Never promise a feature, a platform or a price the screens or the page do not show.

## 5. The verdict and the call to action

- The verdict is an opinion about the app, in a few words: "Honestly, the first budget I'd
  keep open." It adds no new fact and no number.
- The call to action is on-screen text over the last 2 to 3 seconds and a line in the post
  caption: the app's name and where to get it.
- A spoken call to action ("download it now") is turned into a verdict by the writer. When the
  person insists on one, add their words to the given lines, tell them it may come back as a
  verdict, and keep it on screen either way.

## 6. Claims for apps and websites

| Allowed | Only from the person, word for word | Never |
|---|---|---|
| What the screens show | Ratings and review counts | A made-up rating badge, review, notification or chat |
| What the app's page states | Download or user counts | "I've used it for months", "it changed my life" |
| Present-tense workflow ("you tap, it books") | Rankings, awards, "number one" | Money or time "saved", income, health results |
| Opinions ("honestly, it's so clean") | Comparisons with a named competitor | A screen of another company's app |
| The price, when the page prints it | Launch offers and discount codes | A drawn or generated screen shown as the real app |

Finance, health and money-making apps: only what the page states, no promises of income,
savings, returns or health results (house-rules.md, section 7).

## 7. The script-to-screen map

Write it before the price, keep it for the edit:

| Beat | Line | Cue word | Screen | Layout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | "..." | none | none (face) | hook text |
| App moment | "This is <App>. ..." | the feature noun | s1: home with the name | card |
| App moment | "..." | ... | s2: the feature | card |
| Result | "..." | ... | s3: the finished result | card or cutaway |
| Verdict | "..." | none | none (face) | call to action text |

More screens than lines: keep the best ones. One that shows the app's name, the clearest
feature, the result. Tell the person which you left out.

## 8. A full example

The person: "Make a promo for our budgeting app Fernly. Screenshots: <3 image links>. Site:
fernly.example."

Screens after `preview_assets`: s1 the home screen with the Fernly name, s2 purchases sorted
into categories, s3 "left to spend this week" in large type.

The four lines (41 words, 15 s):

1. "Budget in a spreadsheet you never open? Look at this."
2. "This is Fernly. Link your bank once and every purchase sorts itself into categories."
3. "Then it shows what's left to spend this week."
4. "Honestly, the first budget I'd actually keep open."

Cue words: "Fernly" (s1), "categories" (s2), "left to spend" (s3).

The brief (`prompt`), with `ugc_style: "Talking head"`, `duration: 15`:

```
Idea: @Avatar1 talks to camera, hands free, about Fernly, a budgeting app for people who
stopped keeping a budget, sitting at a home desk.
Hook: "Budget in a spreadsheet you never open? Look at this."
Lines (use these lines as written; the edit shows what each one names):
"Budget in a spreadsheet you never open? Look at this."
"This is Fernly. Link your bank once and every purchase sorts itself into categories."
"Then it shows what's left to spend this week."
"Honestly, the first budget I'd actually keep open."
Takeaway: Fernly sorts every purchase into categories and shows what is left to spend this
week.
Product: Fernly, a budgeting app. Page: https://fernly.example
Setting: a tidy home desk by a window, daylight.
Ending: a calm verdict with eye contact.
Language: English.
Sound: voice and room sound only.
```

The hook text: "Budget you'll\nactually open". The call to action text: "Fernly: link in bio".

"Link your bank once" stays only because s2's screen or the page shows a bank connection;
otherwise cut it and say only what s2 shows.

## 9. Showing the script to the person

In the price message, after the one-line plan: the four lines, numbered, with the screen in
brackets after the line it appears on, then the prices. Example:

> A 15-second promo: Mia at her desk talks about Fernly; your three screens come in as she
> names them; captions on.
> 1. "Budget in a spreadsheet you never open? Look at this."
> 2. "This is Fernly. Link your bank once and every purchase sorts itself into categories."
>    [home, then categories]
> 3. "Then it shows what's left to spend this week." [left to spend]
> 4. "Honestly, the first budget I'd actually keep open." On screen: "Fernly: link in bio"
>
> The take <N1> credits, captions <N2>, export <N3>: <total> in all; you have <M>. Go ahead,
> or change a line first?

A change to a line before the yes is free. After the take, a new line means a new take.
