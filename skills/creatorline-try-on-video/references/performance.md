# Performance: reaction, words and voice

How the avatar moves, reacts and speaks in a try-on. These details make it read as a person
trying on something they like, not a model posing. Write them into the brief as plain staging
and quoted lines.

## 1. Register: set by words

Choose it only from explicit words: the person's ask, or the brand's tone in `get_brand`
(`brand_voice`). Never from the item's category alone.

| Register | Words that set it | How it plays |
|---|---|---|
| Natural (default) | Nothing said | Human-sized delight, one real peak on the Wearing beat, never staged screaming |
| Hyped | hyped, energetic, explosive, high energy, viral energy | Bigger, faster, a peak on more than one beat |
| Calm | goth, deadpan, clinical, refined, quiet luxury, minimal, runway-cool, quiet, GRWM, routine | Slow moves, few words, small expressions, no goofy beats |

## 2. Expressions are body mechanics

- Write what the brows, eyes, mouth, shoulders and hands do, never a mood word: "her brows lift,
  a laugh breaks through her nose, shoulders drop", not "she is thrilled".
- Never the same expression twice in one video.
- Name at least two small beats in every shot (a glance down at the hem, a lip press, a weight
  shift, a breath, a head tilt) and one change inside the shot; the writer adds more around
  them. None of them adds a second job for the hands.

## 3. Peaks

A peak is a body event the item causes:

- a hand flies to the chest;
- both hands frame the face;
- the head thrown back mid-laugh;
- a double-take at the outfit.

One or two per video, each taking the place of a small beat, never stacked on one. The first is
on the Wearing beat. In a seated verdict section, only seated ones (a hand to the chest, a slow
breath out).

## 4. Small human moments

- **One unguarded moment** per video: an eye flick away and back, a stumble mid-thought she
  recovers from.
- **Small goofy beats** in at least three different on-camera shots (in a short video, as many
  on-camera shots as it has): a quick tongue flash, an eyebrow waggle, a mock chef's kiss. Never
  in a detail shot, and none in the calm register.
- **One sound from the place, optional:** something that belongs there (a kettle clicking off,
  a dog's tags, a door down the hall), named exactly in the Sound line. She glances toward it
  and is back to the lens within about 1.5 s, with no new words.

## 5. Micro-beats and sound

- Name the body part and the object for every small move: "her left hand smooths the hem at
  her hip", not "she adjusts it".
- One movement at a time; moves written as happening together render as a glitch.
- Put small moves between spoken phrases, never on a key word.
- Every sound to be heard (the bag's paper, a laugh, a breath, footsteps on wood) is named in
  the Sound line. A sound only in the shot text may come out silent.
- Music only when asked; then one line naming its feel, with no lyrics.

## 6. The hook

- Frame one is already moving: the hand already on the bag's handle, the grin already breaking.
  The first word, or the first sound, lands within the first 0.4 s. The only allowed delay is a
  deliberate freeze of up to 0.7 s.
- The first section may open on one to three small sounds before the first word: a soft gasp,
  a small bright laugh, a sharp breath in. Describe them in the shot; never write them inside
  quoted lines, where they would be read out. Later sections open on no sound.
- Later sections never greet and never name the item again.

## 7. The lines

- **The first section** opens on the format's proven hook, a reason this piece matters now: an
  occasion ("This is the dress for every summer wedding"), a gap it fills ("Every outfit needs
  one pair like this"), what the frame shows.
- **Later sections** continue mid-thought and say more specific things in the same number of
  words. A seated verdict can carry the fit the frame shows ("Sits right at the hip") or an
  opinion ("the one I keep reaching for").
- **Word bands:** 12-20 words up to 10 s, 20-28 for 11-12 s, 28-35 for 13-15 s, about 2 words a
  second. A shot with a move or a twirl holds fewer words; the detail shots take the most
  (try-on-arc.md, section 8).
- Cut AI-sounding openers, empty praise, repeats and any call to action. The item's facts
  (fabric, price, sizes offered) come from the person, the label or the page; an approved-claims
  list, when the person gives one, is kept word for word (product-intake.md, section 4).
- **Every claim carries one concrete:** a place on the body ("cropped right at the waist"), a
  time or occasion ("for a summer wedding"). Never compared with another brand by name.

## 8. Words never used

**As the first word of any section:** OK, okay, so, alright, yeah so, um, well, like, wait,
hold on, OMG, hey guys, guys, story time, so basically, stop scrolling.

**Anywhere:** obsessed (any form), you have to try this, game changer, 10/10, 100%, "literally"
as filler, holy grail, changed my life, hits different, elevate, seamless, effortless, the
"this is X, not Y" construction, and empty praise (amazing, so good, mind-blowing).
("Seamless" as a fact about a garment's construction, from the item card, is fine.)

## 9. Delivery marks in quoted lines

When you write the lines in quotes, mark the delivery sparingly:

- at most two stretched vowels in the whole video ("sooo good on");
- one or two words in CAPS per shot's line, at most;
- one sentence that breaks off at the peak, written with an ellipsis;
- at most one whisper that rises to a spike;
- breaths named in the shot and in the Sound line;
- in the wordiest on-camera shot, one moment with the lips closed (a smile, a look down at the
  hem): lip sync is where renders break, and the closed-mouth beat gives it a rest.

## 10. Accent, persona and a quirk

- **Accent** only when asked, or when the person mentions where the avatar is from. Describe it
  by its qualities ("a strong Texan drawl, long vowels"), never with phonetic spelling. Write
  it two steps stronger than asked, never "slight", "subtle" or "light", and add "a strong
  accent in every line". Copy the Accent line word for word into every take, because
  separately filmed takes drift apart. English lines with no accent asked: an American accent,
  unless the brand's `country` says otherwise.
- **The avatar's persona and face** come with it. Never describe its face, age or ethnicity in
  a brief; name it as `@Avatar1`.
- **Age-blind words:** never girl, boy, child, kid, young or teen. Write the avatar's name,
  `@Avatar1`, "she", "he" or "the woman".
- **A physical quirk** (a habit, a mannerism, an unusual energy) only when the person mentions
  one: one shot per section shows it, staged about a third bigger than life so it reads on a
  phone. A charming habit, never a condition, never about appearance, body or age.
