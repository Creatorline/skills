# The deliverable

What the person gets, part by part, and how each part is written: the layout, the beats, the
production cues, the on-screen text, the caption and hashtags, the retention map, scripts for
other speakers, parts over 30 seconds, checking a script the person wrote, and the hand-off to
filming.

## 1. The layout

One message, in this order. Write it in the person's language around the script; the lines,
texts and caption are in the brand's `language` unless the person asked for another.

```
**<Working title>**: <length> <genre>, <lane> · <who says it> · <language>
Defaults: <genre, and the one it beat in a few words; anything else they can change>

**Hooks** (the script opens with 1)
1. <Type> (recommended, <reason in a few words>)
   Says: "<line>"
   On screen: <text>
   First frame: <what we see>
2. <Type>
   Says: "<line>"
   On screen: <text>
3. <Type>
   ...

**Script**

**0-3 s · Hook**
Says: "<line>"
On screen: <text>
We see: <one picture>

**3-7 s · <block name>**
Says: "<line>"
On screen: <text, or nothing>
We see: <one picture>
[B-ROLL] <a cue, only when the beat needs one>

**Part 2** (only over 30 s with the avatar on camera, section 8)

**<n-m> s · <block name>**
...

**Caption**
<first line>
<one to three short lines>
<the ask>
#tag #tag #tag

**Retention map**
| Time | Device |
|---|---|
| ... | ... |

**To check before filming** (only when there is something)
- [VERIFY] <the fact and where it came from>
Sources: <links>

What next?
1. <film it, in plain words: "Film it with Mia (I'll show the credits first)">
2. <next step>
3. <next step>
```

- No preamble before it and no summary after it; one line at most.
- No ids, no tool or skill names, no word counts (except when checking the person's script).
- No praise of your own script and no claims about its pacing or its compliance in the title or
  the headings: the retention map is the only commentary on it.
- The person may copy any part, so every part reads on its own.

## 2. Beats

- **Times** are whole seconds, start at 0, follow on with no gaps or overlaps, and add up
  exactly to the length. The hook is always 0-3 s.
- **A beat is 3 to 5 seconds** when the avatar films it; a longer block holds several beats.
- **Says:** the exact words in quotes, said aloud as written. Two speakers: a label per line
  (section 7). No stage directions inside the quotes.
- **A verdict, a punch line or an aphorism gets its own beat**, or at least its own sentence at
  the end of one, so an editor can find it.
- **On screen:** the words, or "nothing" (section 4).
- **We see:** one picture in plain words: who, the one action, what is in frame. The product is
  in frame from the beat's first second, label to the lens. No camera words (dolly, drone, slow
  motion, studio light) unless the person asked for a polished film. No split screen or text
  in this line: those are edits, so they are cues.
- **A cue**, when needed, on its own line in brackets (section 3).
- Each block's name comes from the genre's map ([genres.md](genres.md)), so the person sees the
  shape.

## 3. Production cues

A cue in brackets, on its own line under the beat, tells whoever makes the video what else is
on screen. Use only the cues the beat needs, and only ones Creatorline can make:

| Cue | Means | How it gets made |
|---|---|---|
| `[ON-SCREEN]` | Words on screen beyond the beat's text line | Video Studio text, after the take |
| `[B-ROLL]` | A cutaway picture | A clip from the brand's library, the stock library, or a new photo or clip, in Video Studio |
| `[DEMO]` | The product doing its thing | In the take itself (the filming skill's brief) |
| `[CLIP]` | The artifact being discussed | The person's video or screenshot as a split screen or a layer in Video Studio, only what they may show |
| `[OVERLAY]` | A picture over the speaker | A Video Studio layer |
| `[CHART]`, `[MAP]`, `[ANIMATION]` | A drawn graphic | A new picture used as a layer, with no numbers drawn in it; the numbers as text |
| `[COUNTER]`, `[SCORE CARD]` | A running number or a score | Video Studio text, the same look each time |
| `[PHOTO]`, `[ARCHIVAL]`, `[FOOTAGE]` | Real records | Only material the person supplies and may use, as a layer or a clip; never a real person's face unless they agreed |
| `[ACT-OUT]`, `[RE-ENACTMENT]` | The speaker plays a part, or a scene is staged | In the take; a staged scene of real events uses anonymous figures, never a real face |

Not available, so never written: a music sting or a sound effect at a moment (only one track
from the brand's library can sit under the whole video).

## 4. On-screen text

Text is for the viewer with the sound off, and for the eye while the ear listens.

- **Size:** one or two lines of about 26 characters each. A few big words, never a sentence.
- **Where:** the hook text at the top in the first 3 seconds; labels for steps, items, "Myth"
  and "Fact" at the top or the middle; the call to action over the last beat. Never the bottom
  row: speech captions sit there (or in the middle when the video has a split screen).
- **How long:** its whole beat, and never under 2 seconds.
- **What:** something the viewer needs muted (the promise, the number, the step), not the
  spoken line word for word: captions do that.
- **Matching:** texts of one kind (all step labels, all item numbers) share one look and size.
- **Numbers as digits** on screen ("4 g sugar"), even when they are said as words.
- **Exact words come from the edit**, never from the filming brief: the texts are added in Video
  Studio after the take, where they are sharp and editable.
- Plain characters, the brand's language, no em dashes.

## 5. The caption and hashtags

**The shape:** a first line, one to three short lines, the ask, then the hashtags.

- **First line:** a second hook that adds something the video did not say. It shows before the
  "more" cut, so it works alone. Never the spoken hook word for word.
- **Body:** the takeaway in the words someone would search for: what it is, the problem it
  solves, the place or the routine. Product facts from the label, the page or the person (an
  approved list, when given, word for word).
- **The ask:** `brand_voice.call_to_action` when the brand has one. Otherwise one that fits the
  video: save it for later, the link in bio, the next part of a series. A question with a real
  answer is welcome ("Which one do you grab?"); "comment yes" is not.
- **Hashtags:** 3 to 5. One or two about the exact topic, one for the niche, the brand's own tag
  when its past captions use one. Nothing unrelated. Shown with `#`; passed to `create_post`
  later as bare words in `hashtags`.
- **Length,** hashtags included: TikTok and Instagram 2,200 characters, YouTube 5,000, Telegram
  1,024. A good short-form caption is far shorter.
- **YouTube:** when the brand posts there (`list_connections`), add a title: the hook as a plain
  statement, short. The publish skill puts it in the post's YouTube settings.
- **Voice:** the brand's tone, do's and don'ts. Emojis only when the brand's past captions use
  them. No em dashes.
- **The persona:** an avatar's caption may speak in its voice and with its own history, like
  the lines ("three weeks of desk lunches, still my snack"); "my honest review" is fine.
- **Disclosure:** AI disclosure is the post's `made_with_ai` setting, which the publish skill
  sets; it is not words in the caption. A post someone other than the account's owner paid for
  opens its caption with `#ad`. Both are defaults the person may switch off (house rules,
  section 7).

## 6. The retention map

One line per device the script really uses, with its time. It shows the person why the script
holds attention, so they can keep it when they edit.

| Device | What it is | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| Loop | The hook's open question | Opens at 0 s, paid near the end; one per short script |
| Deferral | A named thing promised for later ("the third one is the strangest") | Paid within the video, by name |
| Pivot | A "but here's the thing" turn | The turn, or the middle of a 60-second script |
| Counter | "2 of 3", step and item numbers, a running score or count | Each item, step or rung |
| Callback | A late line returns to the hook's words or a planted line | Only to a line that really exists in this script |
| Held reveal | The named thing shown late | At the payoff |
| Loop-close | The last line runs back into the first | The last second |

- Three to five lines for 15 and 30 seconds, up to seven for 60.
- Only devices that are in the script, each with its time. No scores, no grades, no predictions
  ("this will go viral").

## 7. Other speakers

**A narrator** (no face on camera, or a voice track for footage):

- One flowing sentence beats three clipped ones: a synthetic voice pauses at every full stop.
- Numbers written as they are said ("four grams", "twenty twenty-six").
- Feelings are marked only where they change, with the tags the Video Studio voiceover reads:
  `[excited]`, `[cheerfully]`, `[laughs]`, `[whispers]`, `[curious]`, `[sad]`, `[sighs]`,
  `[pauses]`. A performed tag (a laugh, a sigh, a pause) takes about a second: count it.
- At most about 2.9 words a second, whatever the genre's pace.
- The whole narration stays under 2,000 characters (one voiceover holds that much).
- "We see" becomes the picture list: one photo or clip per beat, changing every 3 to 4 seconds.

**The person themself, on their own camera or recording their own voice:**

- Write it the way they talk. If you cannot tell, ask for one sentence they would say.
- Their own experience goes in their words: what they told you is written in, and what you do
  not know (their number, their timeline) is a marked blank they fill, "[your result]".
- Delivery cues in parentheses, sparingly: (to the lens), (hold it up), (pause).
- No part breaks; they film however they like.

**Two speakers:**

- On camera (a podcast clip, a street interview): label every line with the speaker's role,
  "Host:" and "Guest:", one speaker per beat. A second avatar is the filming skill's job.
- Voice only (a two-voice dialogue): label the lines "A:" and "B:", one turn per line.

## 8. Over 30 seconds

- With the avatar on camera, these scripts film with `creatorline-ai-host`: parts of 12 to 25
  seconds, one take each, in one set, joined in Video Studio. A cut at a complete thought keeps
  every join clean, and a flawed part is filmed again on its own.
- Mark each break as its own line, **Part 2**, **Part 3**, between two beats: one break in a
  45-second script, two in a 60-second one. Never split a sentence.
- The last line before a break is a one-line bridge, a small deferral ("And the second one is
  worse."). The first line after it pays it at once ("The second one:"). It is not a second
  loop.
- Same place, same look across the parts: one continuous video, not scenes.
- A narrator's script or the person's own has no breaks.

## 9. Hooks only, caption only, checking a script

- **Hooks only:** the layout's title line, then the hooks grouped by type or family as
  [hooks.md](hooks.md), section 6 says, the three to test first marked, then "What next?" (a
  script for the winner first).
- **Caption only:** the caption in its shape (section 5), two other first lines to choose from,
  a YouTube title when the brand posts there, then "What next?".
- **Checking the person's script** (a check, a fit or a fix):
  1. Time it: count the spoken words against the length at the genre's pace and put each line
     on the seconds it would take.
  2. Route it ([router.md](router.md)): which genre is it trying to be?
  3. Find the single weakest point, checking in this order: a fixed-line or facts problem
     (house rules, section 7; [router.md](router.md), section 7); an invented plug; no hook in
     the first 3 seconds; a hook the body never pays; a false callback; over or under the
     budget; genres blended; no concrete takeaway; a spoken call to action as the ending; a
     beat that adds nothing; for a story, the story check ([genres.md](genres.md), section 9).
  4. Show:

```
**What works:** <one line>
**Weakest point:** <one line; with the count when it is the budget: "62 words for 15 s, about 20 over">
**Fix 1, smallest change:** <their script with only that point changed>
**Fix 2, rewritten:** <your version, as beats in the layout of section 1>
```

- Never one big rewrite they did not ask for: Fix 1 keeps their words.
- A fixed-line or facts problem is fixed in both versions, with the reason in one line.
- Their beat order and words stay unless a rule breaks; say what changed and why.

## 10. Handing it to filming

When the person says "film it", follow the filming skill and carry the script into it:

| Part of the script | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| The spoken lines | The filming brief, in quotes, in order, with "use these lines as written" (video briefs, sections 2 and 3). The filming skill counts them again against its length |
| The beat times | Not sent: the server's writer times the shots itself (video briefs, section 1) |
| The length | The filming run's `duration`, in whole seconds |
| The part breaks | `creatorline-ai-host` films one part per break |
| Who says it | The avatar the filming skill uses, or a narrator's voice |
| "We see" | The brief's Idea and Setting lines, in plain words |
| The genre and lane | The filming skill's template: talking head for advice, commentary and explainers on camera, product ad for a review, storytime for a scene |
| The cues and on-screen texts | The edit after the take (`creatorline-video-studio`, or the format skill's own text step) |
| Speech captions | `creatorline-captions` |
| Narration | `creatorline-faceless-video`, or `creatorline-voiceover` for footage they have |
| New lines over a trending video | `creatorline-reshoot`: the new words go in quotes in its Direction |
| The caption, hashtags, YouTube title | `creatorline-publish`, once the video is made |
| `[VERIFY]` items | Resolved with the person BEFORE filming; never filmed unchecked |

The filming skill runs its own intake (a product photo, for example) and shows the credits
before anything runs. Never start a paid run from this skill.

## 11. A worked example

The person: "Write a 15 second TikTok script for our Peanut Crunch bar. Label says 20 g protein,
4 g sugar." The brand's default avatar is Mia; `brand_voice.tone` is "friendly, matter of fact";
its call to action is "Link in bio". The engine is a verdict and the spine her own use:
Product and tech review, in the Hands-on lane. Mia is a living persona, so her three weeks with
the bar are her story to tell; the numbers are the label's.

```
**Peanut Crunch, three weeks in**: 15 s product review, hands-on · Mia on camera · English
Defaults: product review (not explainer: it ends on a verdict, not a how), Mia's three weeks
of desk lunches as her story, for TikTok and Reels, lines in your brand's voice. Say if you
want any changed.

**Hooks** (the script opens with 1)
1. Verdict-first tease (recommended, her three weeks are the proof)
   Says: "Three weeks in, and this bar wins. One catch, though."
   On screen: 3 weeks in. One catch.
   First frame: Mia holds the bar to the lens, the front of the wrapper showing.
2. Contrarian flip
   Says: "Everyone reads the protein. Read the sugar."
   On screen: Read the sugar
3. Countdown bomb
   Says: "Two things I like, and one I don't. Bad news last."
   On screen: 2 good, 1 bad

**Script**

**0-3 s · Hook**
Says: "Three weeks in, and this bar wins. One catch, though."
On screen: 3 weeks in. One catch.
We see: Mia holds the bar up, the front of the wrapper to the lens.

**3-6 s · Proof 1**
Says: "Four grams of sugar. Twenty of protein."
On screen: 4 g sugar · 20 g protein
We see: She turns the bar over, her finger on the sugar line, label to the lens.

**6-9 s · Proof 2**
Says: "And it still crunches."
On screen: nothing
We see: She snaps the bar in half, the peanut pieces to the lens.

**9-12 s · The catch**
Says: "It's dense. Half at ten, half at three."
On screen: 1 bar = 2 snacks
We see: She holds up the two halves, one in each hand.

**12-15 s · Who it's for**
Says: "For the desk-lunch crowd. Not for chocolate people."
On screen: Peanut Crunch, link in bio
We see: She sets one half down by a laptop, a small nod.

**Caption**
Everyone checks the protein. Flip it over for the number that matters.
Peanut Crunch: 20 g protein, 4 g sugar, and one bar is two snacks.
Link in bio.
#proteinbar #highprotein #snackideas #peanutbutter

**Retention map**
| Time | Device |
|---|---|
| 0 s | Loop: "one catch, though", paid at 9 s |
| 3 s | Counter: the two numbers on screen |
| 6 s | Held reveal: the inside of the bar |
| 9 s | Pivot: the catch |

What next?
1. Film it with Mia as a 15-second talking video (I'll show the credits first)
2. Two more hooks from other types, to test later
3. A 30-second version with a third proof
```

**Why it works:** 37 spoken words for 15 seconds, each beat inside its band with the bar in
hand. The verdict comes first, with her three weeks behind it, and the one loop (the catch) is
paid at 9 to 12 seconds. The numbers are the label's; the three weeks and the half-at-ten habit
are Mia's own, the persona carrying the review. All three hooks lead into the same second beat.
The call to action is on screen and in the caption, not spoken.
