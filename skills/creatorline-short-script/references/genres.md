# Genres

The six spoken-word genres in their short-form mode, then the UGC scene formats. Each genre
has its gate (in [router.md](router.md)), its lanes, its map at 15, 30 and 60 seconds, its
register, what holds attention, what it never does, what each speaker may say, and the skill
that films it. Its hook types are in [hooks.md](hooks.md), section 2.

## 1. How to read the maps

**The short-form rules every genre keeps** (anything of 4 minutes or less is short-form, so
everything Creatorline makes):

- The first line IS the hook. The key thing (the image, the product, the artifact, the number)
  is on screen within the first 2 seconds, and the hook works with the sound off: assume
  on-screen text carries it.
- One loop only, opened at the top and paid near the end.
- The long arc folds into: hook, one mechanism or escalation, payoff, a one-line kicker, a hard
  stop. The outro is one plain line, or nothing.
- Consider a loop-close: a last line that hands back into the first.

**The maps.**

- A map lists the blocks and their seconds. A block longer than 5 seconds holds several beats:
  one spoken line and one picture each, 3 to 5 seconds. "-" means the block is not in that
  length.
- The seconds come from the genre's proven percentages, rounded to 3-second beats when the
  avatar films it (a beat under 3 seconds is merged into its neighbour).
- **Other lengths.** 10 s: hook 0-3, the middle 3-7, the ending 7-10. 20 s: the 15-second map
  with one more beat in the middle. 45 s: the 60-second map with its middle blocks shortened.
  Keep the hook at 0-3 and an ending of at least 3 seconds.
- **The pivot** (60-second maps, near 30 s): a "but here's the thing" line that turns the
  middle without opening a second loop.
- **Part breaks** (over 30 seconds with the avatar on camera): the avatar is filmed in parts of
  12 to 25 seconds that are joined, so mark a break after a complete thought: one in 45 s, two
  in 60 s. The line before a break is a short bridge; the line after pays it
  ([deliverable.md](deliverable.md), section 8). A narrator or the person needs no breaks.

**Pace by genre.** Words = seconds x the pace. The avatar on camera stays inside the speech
budget of [video-briefs.md](video-briefs.md), section 7, whatever the genre.

| Genre | Words per second | Per minute |
|---|---|---|
| Business, Commentary, Explainer | 2.3 to 2.5 | 140 to 150 |
| Commentary with the artifact played (a reaction) | 1.9 to 2.3 over the whole video, leaving air for the clips | about 115 to 140 |
| Documentary story, True crime | about 2.4 | about 145 |
| Product and tech review, on camera | 2.7 to 3.3 (the avatar: to the top of its budget) | 160 to 200 |
| Product and tech review, a dense voiceover | 3.3 to 3.9, the person's own voice only; a narrator voice stays at 2.9 at most | 200 to 235 |

## 2. Business and self-improvement

**Lanes.** Tips: one lesson, one receipt, one command. Listicle: a counted promise of 3 or
fewer (more only when the person asks). How-to steps: the Steps map below.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: a specific number or a named contradiction | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-6 |
| Credibility: one flex and one confession, a line each | - | 3-6 | 6-12 |
| The mechanism, with its receipt | 3-9 | 6-22 | 12-45 |
| The command, or an if-then | 9-12 | 22-27 | 45-54 |
| A one-line verdict or aphorism, then a hard stop | 12-15 | 27-30 | 54-60 |

**Listicle items.** Each item is numbered aloud ("number two"), named (a proper name when it is
a framework), then its mechanism, one example, one figure, and a one-line verdict. Items get
shorter as you go; the heaviest or most personal comes last, flagged ("save the last one").
When the person asks for more than 3:

| Length | Items | Seconds per item |
|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 4 or 5 | 4 to 5 |
| 45 s | 5 or 6 | about 6 |
| 60 s | 6 or 7 | 6 to 7, the pivot before the last two |

**The Steps map** (how-to, also the "taught through use" lane of a product review):

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the result first | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| What you need, in one line | - | 3-6 | 3-8 |
| Steps, one action each, in the imperative | 3-12 (3 steps) | 6-21 (3 or 4) | 8-45 (5 or 6, the pivot "the step everyone skips" near 30) |
| The one mistake to avoid | - | 21-26 | 45-52 |
| The result again, and the verdict | 12-15 | 26-30 | 52-60 |

Never ask the avatar to count on its fingers: the step label on screen counts.

**Register.**
- First person into second person, present tense, contractions.
- One long explaining sentence, then a short verdict worth a screenshot, on its own line.
- One original aphorism per script.
- No claim goes unproven past its own beat: the receipt (a specific number, the speaker's own
  result, a named source, a demo) sits in the same or the next beat. On camera, claim then
  proof; in a narrator's voice, proof then claim.
- Say "number two" at every joint of a list, and mark the last one.
- Voice the viewer's objection and answer it at once.
- Commands without hedges; the hedges ("not financial advice") in one short beat of their own.
- The cost of doing nothing, dated and vivid: "by March, that's another four hundred dollars".
- Give the viewer the exact words to say: "Say: 'I'll need that in writing.'"
- Money numbers exact and non-round, per unit of time, in before-and-after pairs; a range when
  inexact. Hold the breakdown of the hook's number to the last third.
- No guru posture: say the real cost and the real time.

**Holds attention.** The hook's number explained late; the counter on screen; the objection
answered.
**On screen.** The hook's number; "1.", "2." labels in one look and size; the command in a few
words over its beat.
**Never.** Throat-clearing; vague or round numbers; a stat or quote with no source; get-rich
promises; the same pace throughout; a tease never paid; corporate words.
**Speakers.** The avatar: in its own voice, written specific; data about the wider world names
its source. The person: their own numbers, or a marked blank they fill. **Films with**
`creatorline-talking-video` (`Talking head`), `creatorline-tutorial-video` (steps), or a
narrator in `creatorline-faceless-video`.

## 3. Product and tech review

**The contract.** The speaker does work the viewer cannot or will not (buys, tests, breaks,
reads the fine print) and reports back, skeptical by default, excited only when the product
changes their mind on camera.

**Lanes.**
- **Hands-on**: the speaker bought it, lived with it and reports back: what they bought, how
  long they used it, what changed, what broke.
- **Demo**: the product working now, no history needed: a first look, or a clip where the
  product doing its job is the whole story.
- **Ranking**: several products; the sort rule announced in the second sentence (price going
  up, a countdown, tiers), a score for each, a running tally, the total revealed at the end.
- **News**: a recency hook, a one-line primer, the findings strongest first, a mini-verdict with
  its caveat after each (never a separate list of limits), a closing calibration line.
- **Taught through use**: the Steps map (section 2), the product as the tool.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the verdict first, with a number; hands-on adds an effort verb with its time ("thirty days in"). The product does something on screen within 2 s. Tease the catch to open the loop | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-5 |
| Proof 1, the strongest | 3-6 | 3-8 | 5-16 |
| Proof 2 | 6-9 | 8-13 | 16-27 |
| Proof 3 | - | 13-18 | 27-38 |
| The one catch (pays the loop) | 9-12 | 18-23 | 38-46 |
| Who it is for, and the hedge, in one line | 12-15 | 23-30 | 46-55 |
| An original sign-off (optional) | - | - | 55-60 |

**Register.**
- Translate every spec: "that means...", a multiple ("three times the old one"), or the number
  on screen while the voice says what it means.
- Show it before judging it. Own every opinion ("for me", "I'd"). Say your limits.
- Every negative gets a fairness beat and a way out: who it still suits, or what would fix it.
  Aim at the decision, never at the people who bought it.
- Every hype claim gets a comparison or a check. Claims printed on the box are shown working or
  hedged ("the box says ten hours; that's the box talking").
- Hedges come in three kinds: it may change with updates; one unit, one sample; what nobody
  knows yet.
- At speed: flat one-idea sentences, and an analogy within 5 seconds of any hard idea.

**Holds attention.** The verdict first, then a "but here's the thing" pivot; a running game (a
score, a price check); the hook's loop closed in the hook's own words.
**On screen.** The verdict in a few words; each proof's number as digits ("4 g sugar"); scores
in a ranking; the product's name and the call to action over the last beat.
**Never.** Spec-sheet order (use the order a normal person's questions come in, or the order of
use); an unowned opinion; a superlative with no comparison; a verdict with no why; press-release
voice; apologizing for length; asking for likes.
**Speakers.** The avatar: any lane, in its own voice. The person: Hands-on with their own
story; what they do not tell you is a marked blank. Rankings compare the brand's own products,
or products by type and price ("the twenty-dollar one"), never another brand by name (house
rules, section 7). **Films with** `creatorline-talking-video` (`Product ad`),
`creatorline-product-video` (product as the hero, voice off screen).

## 4. Commentary

**Lanes.**
- **Hot take**: a stance on a claim, a trend or a practice; the claim is the artifact. One honest
  concession ("to be fair...") late, before the close.
- **Reaction**: a supplied artifact shown on screen (a split screen or a layer in the edit),
  only something the person may show. Each piece of it plays 8 seconds or less, and the
  reaction runs at least as long as the piece it answers.
- **Deadpan satire**: a textbook definition, an announced scaffold of tiers or stages,
  setup-twist units of about 15 to 20 seconds (one or two in 30 s, three in 60 s) where the
  twist is the last words of a calm sentence, never signposted; the top tier turns on the
  speaker or the viewer; then a dead stop, no outro.
- **Close reading**: an earnest look at one work. Count something concrete first (words, shots,
  seconds of pause), name the technique with a quick definition, check it against something
  outside, and only then interpret.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the artifact AND the stance in sentence one | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| Cycle 1: the moment shown, said literally, questioned the way the viewer would or acted out, then a tag | 3-11 | 3-13 | 3-20 |
| Cycle 2, more absurd, after a labelled escalation ("and somehow it gets worse") | - | 13-24 | 20-36 |
| Cycle 3, the most absurd | - | - | 36-50 |
| The sincere point, one line: irony never closes the video | 11-15 | 24-30 | 50-60 |

**Register.**
- A peer, never a lecturer. Narrate the artifact in the present tense.
- Act out the confusion before resolving it.
- Self-deprecation at three points: the hook, just before punching at something, and the close.
  It earns the right to mock.
- Check before you punch: the harm is real, you side with the people it hurts, and you aim at
  institutions, output and behavior, never at looks or fans.
- Escalate in threes: the literal contradiction, an imagined scene, the world-sized consequence.
- Plant the strangest line early and bring it back unannounced; label only the last callback.
- A misdirection list: two or three sincere items and one absurd one, in the same grammar.
- Joke density: in satire, a deadpan reversal every 15 to 25 seconds; in comic commentary, a big
  laugh every 30 to 60 seconds with small ones between; in a close reading, one moment of
  delight (a count, a replay, "did you see that?").

**Holds attention.** The cycles, each more absurd than the last; the planted line coming back.
**On screen.** The take in four or five words at the top; the artifact itself, beside or behind
the speaker; nothing else unless a number needs showing.
**Never.** "In today's video"; a neutral recap; hype voice; teases that never pay; a superlative
the video does not earn; explaining a joke; a real creator's markers (router.md, section 9).
**Speakers.** The avatar holds a stance in its own voice, and may say how the artifact reached
it ("six of you sent me this"). No real person's face or name in the artifact: a claim, a
trend, a practice, a kind of ad, or something the person owns.
**Films with** `creatorline-talking-video` (`Talking head`, `Reaction`); the artifact beside it
in `creatorline-video-studio`.

## 5. Documentary story

**The law.** The premise is said in the first 3 seconds (the title's promise, almost word for
word), and the answer to the title's question is held to 60 to 80 percent of the runtime, given
out in partial reveals: who, then how, then where.

**The narrator's register**, one or the other, never between: a **present companion** (first
person, reacting, showing the seams: the person telling their own true story, or the avatar
reacting in the present, "and this is where it gets strange") or a **composed narrator** (third
person, invisible until the verdict: the faceless narrator).

**Lanes.** A story of what happened (the default). **Scale ladder**: one question, steps that
grow in one direction, a comparison at every step, a payoff every 10 to 20 seconds, ending on
humility. **Economic argument**: thesis, the proof with its number, the history, the mechanism,
the counterpoint, a kicker, and no cold open. **Comedy retelling**: escalating farce, a joke
every 5 to 15 seconds.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: a cold open (an exact date and place, an ordinary act, one wrong detail) or another type | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-4 |
| The frame: the premise said plainly, and where the facts come from | 3-6 | 3-6 | 4-8 |
| The rewind to the start of the chronology | - | 6-10 | 8-15 |
| Escalation: every step ends unsettled; partial reveals | 6-9 | 10-18 | 15-36 |
| The reveal: the cold open replayed with its full context | 9-12 | 18-24 | 36-48 |
| A hedged verdict, then a kicker that gives the meaning; stop | 12-15 | 24-30 | 48-60 |

**Register.**
- Tense is a camera: the present puts the viewer inside the scene; the past is for analysis.
- Punch sentences of 4 to 8 words alternate with longer explaining ones; never two long ones in
  a row.
- No naked number: every number gets a comparison. Every term gets a quick gloss. Every actor
  gets a role on first mention ("the harbor master, Jens").
- Say the core question again, word for word, once more in a 60-second script.
- A counter the viewer can track: days, dollars, meters.
- Stretch the one big reveal: slow it down, read it out, "did you catch that?".
- One honest caveat on the biggest claim.
- Every setup is paid within a third of the video's length.
- Opinion only in the last 10 to 15 percent, flagged as opinion. Moral framing at most once.

**Holds attention.** The cold open is the loop; partial reveals; the counter; in a ladder, a
payoff every 10 to 20 seconds.
**On screen.** The date and place over the cold open; the counter; a source's name when a big
number is said.
**Never.** Throat-clearing; an early verdict; overclaiming; a recap; neutral transitions
("next..."); moralizing mid-story; invented dialogue.
**Speakers.** Facts from sources, named in the notes: the events and the real people in them
are never invented. The avatar tells it in the third person or as a present companion, and may
say how it came to the story ("I lost a weekend to this logbook"). A real person is named only
as a matter of record, never shown. **Films with** `creatorline-faceless-video` (a narrator),
`creatorline-talking-video` (the avatar tells it), `creatorline-ai-host` (an episode).

## 6. True crime

**The reveal-ordering law.** Run the mystery the title did NOT answer. The title names who did
it: run how they were caught. The title hides them: run who and how. The footage shows them: run
why the lie falls apart.

**The ethics, kept whole in short-form.**
- The victim is a person before they are in danger: a sentence or two of who they were.
- No invented inner thoughts at the moment of death; rebuild only from confessions, testimony
  and records, and say so.
- Speculation is fenced: "allegedly" before a verdict, "it's believed", "according to
  <source>", the level of certainty said out loud.
- What is left out is named, with the reason.
- The person responsible is pathetic, never mythic. No blaming the victim. Documented failures of
  institutions are named.
- Violence is named briefly, then the script cuts to its consequence.
- A warning, when needed, is one clause that says where the hard part is ("the call at the end
  is hard to hear").

**Lanes.** Oral storytelling (the default). Footage analysis, only with real interrogation,
bodycam or court footage the person may show: explain each tactic just before the footage shows
it working. Conversational: one commentary beat in the middle.

| Block | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|
| Hook, with the warning as one clause when needed | 0-3 | 0-4 |
| Before: who they were, an ordinary day | 3-8 | 4-12 |
| The anomaly and the search, seen by those who found it; the wrong theory said sincerely before it breaks | 8-15 | 12-27 |
| Escalation: each step ends on a contradiction, a partial reveal or "it would turn out" | 15-21 | 27-45 |
| The payoff: how it was solved, beat by beat; time slows down only here | 21-27 | 45-54 |
| Fates and sentences, fast and cold, with one exact number; no recap | 27-30 | 54-60 |

At least 30 seconds: the victim's sentence needs its room. A 15-second version is only a teaser
for a longer one.

**Register.** Spoken, not written: contractions, "and so... and then", dialogue paraphrased.
Past tense for the background, present at the moment of discovery. Talk to the viewer once
(brief them, quiz them, hand them a choice). Foreshadow with a time and a shift ("then, in
March, everything changed"). Keep identities behind labels ("the neighbor") until the reveal.
Set exact numbers early and let the lie collide with them late. Every promise names where it
will be paid, and is. Never place one atrocity next to another.
**On screen.** The date, time and place stamps; "allegedly" where it is said; nothing graphic.
**Never.** An ending that resolves nothing (an unsolved case says what is known); a mystery
about what the title already answered; an ask in the climb to the payoff; a recap at the end;
fake experts; all-knowing dramatization; jokes inside the crime.
**Speakers.** A narrator by default; the avatar in the third person with sources, and its own
part only as a reader of the record ("I read the whole transcript"): never an interview, a
visit or a lead in the case itself, which would put events on real people. Names are matters of
record; no real person is ever shown.
**Films with** `creatorline-faceless-video`, `creatorline-talking-video`.

## 7. Explainer

**The law.** The viewer is the hero, in the second person: the subject of the experiment, the
one deciding. First person only as a fellow learner one step ahead ("most of us were taught
this"). Every hook plants a concrete image AND opens a question the video closes. Hooks are
confident; hedges come mid-video. Sharp numbers, never round ones.

**Lanes.** On camera (personality-led). A narrator's essay: a chain of causes, one domino per
sentence, the twist on the last domino. An inquiry: the evidence in the order it was found,
with "but how do we know that?". **Myth vs fact**: the misconception flip, where line one states
the wrong belief and line two breaks it.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: a concrete image and the question (the misconception flip is the best short hook) | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-6 |
| The analogy carries the mechanism | 3-9 | 3-18 | 6-36 |
| The turn the hook promised | 9-12 | 18-25 | 36-51 |
| The payoff, then one sentence that zooms out to the viewer's life | 12-15 | 25-30 | 51-60 |

**The analogy, every time a concept is abstract.** Flag it ("think of it like..."), run the
familiar thing's own logic for 2 to 4 plain sentences (1 or 2 in 15 to 30 seconds), come back
with the technical name attached, reuse the analogy as shorthand afterwards, and say where it
breaks: that honesty buys trust for the next one.

**Register.**
- Present tense, even for history: set the date and narrate it live.
- A run of building clauses lands on a blunt verdict of 3 to 6 words, the aha, on its own line.
- Jargon: show it first, name it second, defuse it within one sentence with a household image
  or a joke.
- Every beat moves: a reveal, a reversal or a laugh; no abstraction without an anchor, a
  number made human or a joke.
- Invisible agents get verbs of feeling ("the virus is starving"). Every big number gets a
  household ruler (buses, marathons, a wallet). Calibrate a sensation: name a familiar one, then
  correct it.
- Stage a test instead of citing a claim when you can.
- The misconception is reasonable before it is corrected: never mock the viewer for holding it.

**Holds attention.** The question opened by the hook; the turn; the zoom-out.
**On screen.** The question at the top; "Myth:" over the hook and "Fact:" over the break, in two
looks; the household ruler's number ("as long as 3 buses").
**Never.** A definition as the opening; announcing the topic ("today we're talking about");
lecture register; unbroken abstraction; teases never paid; moralizing before the mechanism;
round numbers; mocking the misconception.
**Speakers.** A fact about the world needs a source, the label, or common knowledge the viewer
can check. Health, supplements and money: no promise that it cures, treats or prevents a
disease, or guarantees income or a return.
**Films with** `creatorline-talking-video`, `creatorline-faceless-video`.

## 8. UGC scene formats

A scene carries these, not a spoken argument. Their hooks come from the seven families of
[hooks.md](hooks.md), section 3, and the short-form rules of section 1 still hold.

**POV.** The viewer should think "that is me". Default 15 s. Filmed as `Storytime` (a scene)
or `Reaction` (the punch is the face) by `creatorline-talking-video`. Over 30 seconds it
becomes a storytime.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| Hook: the "POV:" text, the situation already happening | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| It gets more specific | 3-8 | 3-10 |
| A second escalation | - | 10-16 |
| **Payoff**: the twist, or the line that names the feeling | 8-12 | 16-22 |
| The reaction, held | 12-15 | 22-26 |
| Kicker, or a loop back to the hook | - | 26-30 |

The text is the setup ("POV: your manager says 'quick call'"); the avatar plays the scene in
the present, often as the other person, talking to "you". Words at the bottom of the band: the
face carries it. On screen: the "POV:" line for the first 3 to 5 seconds, then nothing. A
situation the viewer is in; no real company or person named. Run the story check.

**Storytime as a scene** (the persona's own story, fiction, a skit, a POV story; a true story
about real events is section 5). The viewer
should stay to the last line. Default 30 s. Filmed as `Storytime` by
`creatorline-talking-video` (45 and 60 s: `creatorline-ai-host`); told by a narrator:
`creatorline-faceless-video`.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: a tease of the turn, mid-moment | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| Setup: who, where, what they want | 3-6 | 3-7 | 3-12 |
| The obstacle | 6-9 | 7-13 | 12-24 |
| It gets worse | - | 13-18 | 24-30 |
| The pivot | - | - | 30-33 |
| The attempt that fails | - | - | 33-40 |
| **Payoff**: the turn | 9-12 | 18-24 | 40-48 |
| What it means now | - | - | 48-55 |
| Kicker: the reaction, or the line that lands | 12-15 | 24-30 | 55-60 |

One held pause just before the turn. The avatar tells the story as its own
("Last spring I showed up a day early..."), as a POV ("POV: you show up a day early"), in the
second person ("You walk in and..."), or as something happening now ("I'm in the parking lot
and I just realized..."). The person tells theirs. A product may be in the scene, never the
reason it ends well. Run the story check first.

**Other formats**, with skills or templates of their own. Write the script here when only words
are asked for:

| Format | Shape | Default | Films with |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRWM chat | One point made while getting ready; lines about two words shorter | 30 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`GRWM`) |
| Day in the life | 4 to 6 moments, one line each, one thread tying them | 30 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Day in the life`) |
| Before and after | A look, a room or a setup changed on camera, or two takes cut together in Video Studio | 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Before & after`) |
| Street interview, podcast clip | Two speakers, lines labelled by role, one speaker per beat | 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Street interview`, `Podcast clip`) |
| Unboxing, haul | Package, open, reveal, reaction | 15 to 20 s | `creatorline-unboxing-video` |
| Try-on, fit check | The look, the details, the movement, the verdict | 15 s | `creatorline-try-on-video` |
| App or website promo | The avatar talks while the person's screenshots show it | 20 to 30 s | `creatorline-app-promo-video` |
| A trending video's structure with new lines | The source's beats, the person's words | the source's length | `creatorline-reshoot` |

A comparison of two products is the Ranking lane of a product review (section 3); a reaction to
a supplied video is Commentary (section 4).

## 9. The story check

Run it on POV, storytime-as-a-scene, skit and true-story scripts before writing lines. A flat
story films as well as a good one; the flaw only shows once it is posted, so catch it on paper.

1. **One want.** What does the main character want in this video? One thing.
2. **One obstacle** that threatens it, not one that only slows it down.
3. **One turn** against expectation: their own move backfires, something hidden is revealed,
   or the situation flips.
4. **The verdict changes.** Name how the viewer sees the character before the turn and after
   it ("careful" to "the one who saw it coming"). A turn that moves the plot but not that
   verdict is the most common flat story.
5. **The turn is visible**: a look, a held object, a line. What only happens inside a head does
   not film.

When the person's own story fails a step, fix only that step first and say so; offer a fuller
rewrite as the second option ([deliverable.md](deliverable.md), section 9).
