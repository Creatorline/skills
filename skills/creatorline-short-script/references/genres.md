# Genres

The short-form genres a script is written in, each with its aim, its block map at 15, 30 and 60
seconds (other lengths are fitted from these), its on-screen text pattern, its claim-floor trap
and the skill that films it.

## 1. How to read the maps

- A map lists the **blocks** of the arc and their seconds. A block longer than 5 seconds holds
  several **beats**: one spoken line and one picture each, 3 to 5 seconds.
- "-" means the block is not in that length.
- **Payoff** marks the block that answers the hook. It sits at about half to three quarters
  of the way in (7-11 s of 15, 14-22 s of 30).
- **Other lengths.** 10 s: hook 0-3, payoff 3-7, verdict 7-10. 20 s: the 15-second map with
  one more beat before the payoff. 45 s: the 60-second map with 15 seconds taken out of its
  middle blocks, some before the re-hook and some after it, so the re-hook lands near the
  middle. Keep the hook at 0-3 and an ending of at least 3 seconds.
- **Re-hook** (the 60-second maps, at 30-33 s): a small new hook where viewers decide again
  ("And this is the part nobody checks."). It works for every speaker.
- **Part breaks** (over 30 seconds with the avatar on camera): the avatar is filmed in parts of
  12 to 25 seconds that are joined, so mark a break after a complete thought: one in 45 s
  (at the re-hook near the middle), two in 60 s (near 20 s and near 40 s). The line before a
  break is a short bridge; the line after pays it ([deliverable](deliverable.md), section 7).
  A narrator or the person themself needs no breaks.
- "Filmed as" is the template the filming skill will most likely pick (`ugc_style`). The
  filming skill decides; you only write the script.

## 2. Review

**The viewer should** stop wondering whether it works. **Default** 15 s. **Filmed as**
`Product ad` by `creatorline-talking-video`; product as the hero with the voice off screen:
`creatorline-product-video`.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the product in hand, the most specific line | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| What it is and one label fact | 3-7 | 3-8 | 3-10 |
| The detail that builds toward the answer | - | 8-14 | 10-22 |
| A second detail: how it is used | - | - | 22-30 |
| Re-hook: a small new hook | - | - | 30-33 |
| **Payoff**: the one thing it does, shown on camera | 7-11 | 14-22 | 33-45 |
| The honest downside, or who it is not for | - | 22-26 | 45-52 |
| Verdict, and who it is for | 11-15 | 26-30 | 52-60 |

- The product is in frame from the first second, label to camera. The payoff is something the
  camera shows (it pours, it snaps, it folds), not an adjective.
- **On screen:** the hook text, the label fact as a number ("4 g sugar"), the product's name
  and the call to action over the verdict.
- **Claim floor:** a demonstration, never a testimonial. Facts from the label or the approved
  list only. No "I've used it for months", no result on the avatar's body.

## 3. Hot take

**The viewer should** agree, or argue. **Default** 15 s. **Filmed as** `Talking head` by
`creatorline-talking-video`.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the take at full strength | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| What most people believe, said fairly | - | 3-7 | 3-9 |
| Reason one, with a concrete example | 3-8 | 7-14 | 9-20 |
| Reason two | - | - | 20-30 |
| Re-hook: a small new hook | - | - | 30-33 |
| **Payoff**: the sharpest proof, or what to believe instead | 8-12 | 14-22 | 33-45 |
| To be fair: the one honest concession | - | 22-26 | 45-52 |
| Verdict, sharper than the hook | 12-15 | 26-30 | 52-60 |

- One take, stated without hedging; the concession comes once, late, and makes the verdict
  stronger.
- A loop works well: the verdict's last words lead back into the hook.
- **On screen:** the take in four or five words at the top; nothing else unless a number needs
  showing.
- **Claim floor:** no rival brand or real person by name ("the big-name tub", "most apps"); no
  health or money negation the video cannot back; an opinion is said as an opinion.

## 4. Storytime

**The viewer should** stay to the last line. **Default** 30 s. **Filmed as** `Storytime` by
`creatorline-talking-video` (45 and 60 s: `creatorline-ai-host`); told by a narrator:
`creatorline-faceless-video`.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: a tease of the turn, mid-moment | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| Setup: who, where, what they want | 3-6 | 3-7 | 3-12 |
| The obstacle | 6-9 | 7-13 | 12-24 |
| It gets worse | - | 13-18 | 24-30 |
| Re-hook: a small new hook | - | - | 30-33 |
| The attempt that fails | - | - | 33-40 |
| **Payoff**: the turn | 9-12 | 18-24 | 40-48 |
| What it means now | - | - | 48-55 |
| Kicker: the reaction, or the line that lands | 12-15 | 24-30 | 55-60 |

- Run the story check ([section 10](#10-the-story-check)) before writing lines.
- One held pause just before the turn: the pause is the tension. Words sit in the middle of
  the band.
- **On screen:** the hook text only, or one line that sets the scene ("Day 3 of the new job").
  Speech captions carry the rest.
- **Claim floor:** the avatar has no past. Tell it as a POV ("POV: you show up a day early"),
  in the second person ("You walk in and..."), or as something happening right now ("I'm in
  the parking lot and I just realized..."). Only the person on their own camera tells a story
  as their own, and only a true one. A product may be in the scene, never the reason it ends
  well.

## 5. Tutorial

**The viewer should** do it today. **Default** 30 s. **Filmed by** `creatorline-tutorial-video`,
which labels every step on screen.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook: the result first | 0-3 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| What you need, in one line | - | 3-6 | 3-8 |
| Step 1 | 3-6 | 6-11 | 8-15 |
| Step 2 | 6-9 | 11-16 | 15-22 |
| Step 3 | 9-12 | 16-21 | 22-30 |
| Re-hook: "the step everyone skips" | - | - | 30-33 |
| **Payoff**: the key step (steps 4 and 5) | - | - | 33-45 |
| The one mistake to avoid | - | 21-26 | 45-52 |
| The result again, and the verdict | 12-15 | 26-30 | 52-60 |

- At 15 and 30 s the key step is step 3, the payoff. Up to 3 steps in 15 s, 4 in 30 s (the
  mistake block becomes step 4), 5 or 6 in 60 s.
- One action per step, said in the imperative ("Fold it in thirds."). A step that needs two
  actions needs more seconds or becomes two steps.
- Never ask the avatar to count on its fingers: the label on screen does the counting.
- **On screen:** "1. Fold in thirds", "2. ..." with the same look on every label, plus the
  hook text and the result's name at the end.
- **Claim floor:** the result shown is a made thing (a fold, a dish, a setup), never a body or
  skin result.

## 6. Listicle

**The viewer should** save it for later. **Default** 30 s. **Filmed as** `Talking head` by
`creatorline-talking-video`, or narrated over pictures by `creatorline-faceless-video`.

| Length | Items | Seconds per item | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 3 | 3 | Hook 0-3, items 3-12, verdict 12-15 |
| 30 s | 4 or 5 | 4 to 5 | Hook 0-3, items to 26 or 27, verdict for the last 3 or 4 s |
| 45 s | 5 or 6 | about 6 | Hook 0-3, items, a re-hook before the last two items, verdict for the last 3 or 4 s |
| 60 s | 6 or 7 | 6 to 7 | Hook 0-3, items to 30, re-hook 30-33, items to 55, verdict 55-60 |

- The count is in the hook ("3 things...") and every number is on screen. The counter is the
  loop: each number is a small payoff, so the 50 to 75 percent rule bends here.
- Order: a strong first item (it keeps the viewer past three seconds), the weakest in the
  middle, the best last, or the best flagged at the top ("number one is the one I'd keep").
- Every item gets its name and one concrete reason or detail. A name alone is a list, not a
  video.
- **On screen:** "1. Granola" style labels, same look and size every time; the title over the
  first beat.
- **Claim floor:** every reason is a fact, a visible detail or an opinion; no item about a
  rival by name.

## 7. Myth vs fact

**The viewer should** update a belief. **Default** 15 s. **Filmed as** `Talking head` by
`creatorline-talking-video`.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| Hook: the myth, said as if it were true | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| Why people believe it, said fairly | 3-7 | 3-9 |
| The crack: one detail that does not fit | - | 9-15 |
| **Payoff**: the fact | 7-11 | 15-21 |
| The proof they can check | - | 21-26 |
| What to do instead | 11-15 | 26-30 |

- **30 s, two myths:** two quick cycles (myth, fact, what to do) of 13 to 14 seconds, then a
  3-second verdict.
- **45 and 60 s:** two or three cycles of 15 to 20 seconds each (myth, crack, fact), the most
  surprising last, a 5-second verdict. Part breaks fall between two myths, and each next myth
  is its own small hook.
- The myth is reasonable to believe; never mock the viewer for holding it.
- **On screen:** "Myth:" over the hook, "Fact:" over the payoff, in two different looks.
- **Claim floor:** the fact needs a source the person gave, the label, or common knowledge
  they can check. Health, supplements and money: label facts and approved claims only.

## 8. POV

**The viewer should** think "that is me". **Default** 15 s. **Filmed as** `Storytime` (a
scene) or `Reaction` (when the punch is the face) by `creatorline-talking-video`. Over 30
seconds it becomes a storytime.

| Block | 15 s | 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| Hook: the "POV:" text, the situation already happening | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| It gets more specific | 3-8 | 3-10 |
| A second escalation | - | 10-16 |
| **Payoff**: the twist, or the line that names the feeling | 8-12 | 16-22 |
| The reaction, held | 12-15 | 22-26 |
| Kicker, or a loop back to the hook | - | 26-30 |

- The text is the setup ("POV: your manager says 'quick call'"); the avatar plays the scene
  in the present, often as the other person, talking to "you".
- Words at the bottom of the band: the face carries it. Write reactions small (a look, a
  pause), they render.
- Run the story check ([section 10](#10-the-story-check)).
- **On screen:** the "POV:" line for the first 3 to 5 seconds, then nothing.
- **Claim floor:** a situation, never a result or a history; no real company or person named.

## 9. Other formats

These have skills or templates of their own. Write the script here when only words are asked
for, with the shape below.

| Format | Shape | Default | Films with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Comparison (this vs that) | The hook names both; one difference per beat, shown; the verdict picks one for a named situation | 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video`; side by side in `creatorline-video-studio` |
| GRWM chat | One point made while getting ready; the routine keeps the hands busy, lines about two words shorter | 30 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`GRWM`) |
| Day in the life | 4 to 6 moments, one line each, one thread that ties them together | 30 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Day in the life`) |
| Reaction | The thing reacted to is supplied and shown; the face; one line at the peak | 10 to 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Reaction`) |
| Before and after | A look, a room or a setup, changed on camera; never a body or skin result | 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Before & after`) |
| Street interview, podcast clip | Two speakers, lines labeled by role ("Host:", "Guest:"), one speaker per beat | 15 s | `creatorline-talking-video` (`Street interview`, `Podcast clip`) |
| Unboxing, haul | Package, open, reveal, reaction | 15 to 20 s | `creatorline-unboxing-video` |
| Try-on, fit check | The look, the details, the movement, the verdict | 15 s | `creatorline-try-on-video` |
| App or website promo | The avatar talks while the person's screenshots show the product | 20 to 30 s | `creatorline-app-promo-video` |
| A trending video's structure with new lines | The source's beats, the person's words | the source's length | `creatorline-reshoot` |

## 10. The story check

Run it on storytime, POV and skit scripts before writing lines. A flat story films as well as
a good one; the flaw only shows once it is posted, so catch it on paper.

1. **One want.** What does the main character want in this video? One thing.
2. **One obstacle** that threatens it, not one that only slows it down.
3. **One turn** against expectation: their own move backfires, something hidden is revealed,
   or the situation flips.
4. **The verdict changes.** Name how the viewer sees the character before the turn and after
   it ("careful" to "the one who saw it coming"). A turn that moves the plot but not that
   verdict is the most common flat story.
5. **The turn is visible**: a look, a held object, a line. What only happens inside a head does
   not film.

When the person's own story fails a step, fix only that step first and say so; offer a fuller
rewrite as the second option ([deliverable](deliverable.md), section 9).
