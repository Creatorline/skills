# Long-form maps

**Read this only for a script longer than 4 minutes.** Everything Creatorline makes is
short-form by that line (the Video Studio holds 180 seconds), so these maps never shape a
Creatorline video. Two cases send you here:

- A script over 60 seconds for a Creatorline video belongs to `creatorline-ai-host` (episodes of
  30 to 120 seconds, filmed in parts). It still uses the short-form maps in
  [genres.md](genres.md); nothing here applies.
- A long YouTube script (several minutes) asked for as plain text. Creatorline cannot film it;
  say so in one line, then write it with these maps if the person still wants it.

The router, proof by speaker, the facts and monetization policies, and the hook types are the
same as for short scripts ([router.md](router.md), [hooks.md](hooks.md)). Percentages are of
the whole runtime.

## 1. Format and pace

- A title line: the working title, the format or lane, and about how many minutes.
- "Hook options (pick one)": three hooks of 40 to 80 words (a tech review: three sentences, 15
  seconds at most), each labelled with its type.
- The script, with a time marker every 60 to 90 seconds; production cues in brackets on their own
  lines ([deliverable.md](deliverable.md), section 3); verdict and punch sentences on their own
  lines.
- A retention map: one line per device with its time, never a grade.
- Pace: 140 to 150 words a minute by default; documentary and true crime about 145; a tech review
  on camera 160 to 200, a dense tech voiceover 200 to 235. Write to the runtime; never describe
  it.

## 2. Business and self-improvement

**Personality listicle** (8 to 20 minutes, on camera):

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-4% | Hook and the counted promise |
| 4-10% | Credibility: one proof of standing and one costly confession within a minute of each other, the terms defined, an honest disclaimer against hype |
| 10-85% | The items: numbered, named, mechanism, a story or example, a specific money or time figure, a one-line verdict. Items get shorter as you go; an optional fixed scorecard of two or three measures for every item. At most one clean break after a finished item, near 20% or near 85% |
| 85-95% | The heaviest or most personal item last, flagged; or a recap of all of them |
| 95-100% | A free next action: the next video, a small homework task, a question for the comments with a real answer |

**Voiceover business essay** (10 to 28 minutes): a paradox hook (0-8%), then a pivot sentence
the channel repeats as its own (about 10%), history and context to 30%, the mechanism with unit
economics the viewer could work out themselves to 60%, a "dig deeper" complication or data turn
to 85%, the thesis pressed into a maxim, then a pointer to related videos. A comparison case: one
industry, three companies at three price points, each through audience, pricing, unit economics
and a verdict.

**Direct-response talk**: the counted promise up front, every item carried by a named story, the
count recapped at the middle and at the end, a live demonstration or a stack of value as the
climax, a soft pointer to what is offered.

**Long-form register extras**: one pattern break (a short sketch, a stunt, a fake-narrator gag)
about every 5 minutes; a story told in the first person at the point of greatest abstraction,
then back to the mechanism; the breakdown of the title's number held to the final third.

## 3. Product and tech review

**Single product** (10 to 25 minutes):

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-5% | Hook, the work done (what was bought, used, broken), the roadmap |
| 5-12% | What it is (the price now only if the price is the news) |
| 12-70% | The walkthrough, never in spec-sheet order: in the order a normal person's questions come, in a physical tour, or in the order of use. The most impressive thing pulled forward and flagged; three or more specific, named deferrals planted |
| 70-85% | The downsides, numbered |
| 85-95% | The bottom line, who should buy, the honest hedge on what was not tested |
| 95-100% | An original one-line sign-off and the next video by name |

**Ranking (gauntlet)**: the sort rule in sentence two is the spine (price going up, a countdown,
tiers); for each item a setup, a live demo, a score; a running tally and the total revealed at
the end. **News analysis**: a recency hook, a 30-second primer, numbered findings (strongest
first, or the wildest openly held back), a mini-verdict with its caveat after each, a final
calibration paragraph.

**Retention**: one named deferral about every 2 minutes; one "but here's the thing" pivot per
section; the biggest number held for the last 20%, teased once at the top; the hook's loop
closed in its own words at the end; running games (a price check, a poll, a score per item).

## 4. Commentary

**Comic commentary** (15 to 30 minutes):

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-3% | A cold-open bit, acted 10 to 20 seconds, then a snap to the normal voice |
| 3-10% | How the artifact found the host, and the premise. Any announcement 15 seconds at most, self-aware, cut off fast |
| about 10% | An explicit spoken chapter turn |
| 10-80% | The reaction loop: a clip of 20 seconds or less, a literal restatement, the questions the audience is thinking, an act-out of what is unsaid, a tag. 30 to 90 seconds a cycle, the reaction at least as long as the clip. Ordered by rising absurdity, joined by labelled escalations |
| 40-60% | One pivot: a real setback, a deeper thesis, or a twist in the format |
| 80-90% | The verdict and one sincere paragraph that zooms out |
| 90-100% | A persona outro under 20 seconds |

**Deadpan satirical explainer** (2 to 8 minutes): a definition opens; a scaffold of tiers or
stages is announced; 12 to 18 setup-twist units of 15 to 25 seconds, the twist the final clause
of a calm sentence; the top tier turns on the speaker or the viewer; then stop dead.

**Earnest video essay** (6 to 10 minutes): the artifact cold; a hedged "I think" thesis by 15%,
framed as fascination; an evidence ladder (count something concrete, name the technique, check it
outside, then interpret); the key moment replayed so the viewer feels their own perception
change; human stakes in the last 20%; a closer in the second person; a warm outro. Every
observation echoes another.

## 5. Documentary story

**The mortgage template**: the cold open is a debt the middle pays.

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-2% | Hook |
| 2-8% | The frame and the contract; for an investigation, the source's credibility up front |
| 8-15% | Rewind to the origins; the chronology begins |
| 25-35% | The first plateau: an act break with a partial payoff and an explicit re-hook that raises the stakes |
| 35-70% | Three to five escalating chapters, each ending unsettled (never "and that was that"); reveals dripped: the name, then the method, then the place; one twist or pattern interrupt per chapter |
| 70-85% | The cold-open scene replayed with its full context: the biggest reveal |
| 85-100% | A hedged verdict, a zoom-out to a universal insight or a kicker, then stop within seconds |

**Lanes**: a game or challenge (rules told like a sportscast, counters as the spine, ending on
the winner's reaction); a scale ladder (one question, steady physical progression, a comparison
at each step, ending on humility); an economic argument (thesis, proof, history, mechanism,
counterpoint, kicker; no cold open); a business epic (5 to 10% of "why you should care" first);
a comedy retelling (escalating farce, a joke every 5 to 15 seconds).

**Long-form retention**: every promise paid within 2 to 5 minutes; at most one loop older than 5
minutes (the cold open); the core question repeated word for word about three times; a pattern
interrupt whenever exposition runs past about 60 seconds; payoffs by lane (a ladder every 10 to
20 seconds, a challenge every 20 to 40, a statistical essay every 30 to 60, a procedural a
chapter payoff every 2 to 4 minutes with peaks near 45, 65 and 80%); the most payoffs in minutes
one to three and right after each act break; no setup unpaid past a third of the video.

## 6. True crime

**Single case** (20 to 60 minutes):

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0-2% | Hook and warning |
| 2-5% | A fixed, original opening ritual that ends on a clear boundary: the story starts now |
| 5-20% | Normal life; the victims are people before they are in danger (the person responsible first, if the title names them) |
| 20-45% | The anomaly and the investigation, strictly from the view of those who discovered it; a lead or a dead end every 2 to 4 minutes; the police's wrong theory stated sincerely before it breaks |
| 45-55% | One calm procedural break; nothing interrupts the story after it |
| 55-80% | The escalation ladder, each part ending on a contradiction, a partial reveal or "it would turn out" |
| 80-90% | THE payoff: a second telling from the inside, from confessions and testimony, or the capture; time slowed to beat-by-beat detail only here |
| 90-97% | Fates and sentences, fast and cold, with one exact number |
| 97-100% | The same closing ritual every time, no recap |

**Footage variant**: the crime and the person responsible revealed by 8%; escalating encounters
with the institution (statement, interrogation built on rapport, fatigue, confrontation, arrest);
each interrogation tactic taught just before the footage shows it working; trial excerpts with
one devastating cross-examination; a cold verdict card and no outro.

**Cadence by lane**: oral storytelling is a long fuse with small payoffs every 2 to 4 minutes and
one bomb at 80 to 90%; footage analysis climbs a rung every 5 to 10 minutes; conversational has a
commentary beat every 20 to 40 seconds.

## 7. Explainer

| Share | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:30 to 1:30 | The stakes in the viewer's own life, and about three explicit questions, answered in order (each an open loop with its payoff named) |
| 12-30% | The primer: the least mechanism needed, through ONE anchor analogy |
| 30-70% | The ladder: the same mechanism at a growing scale or across cases, each rung reusing the last one's words; a "but" at least every 90 seconds |
| 60-75% | The turn the hook promised, optionally after a fully scripted fake ending ("roll credits... except") |
| about 70% | The biggest factual payoff |
| 85-95% | The zoom-out to people, time or who the viewer is: where shares happen |
| last 5% | One optional line |

**Lanes**: a personality-led quest (the dream, the counted failed tries, the test day, the payoff;
the dense science block placed after the viewer is invested, and bargained for: "give me two
minutes"); an inquiry (the evidence in the order the investigator found it, with "but how do we
know that?"); a voiceover essay (a chain of causes, one domino per paragraph, the twist around the
fifth). **Long-form register**: no 90 seconds without a reveal, a reversal or a laugh; no
abstraction past about 30 seconds without an anchor, a household number or a joke; an optional
15-second montage of news clips after the hook.

## 8. Placing a requested plug

Only a plug the person asked for, never an invented one ([router.md](router.md), section 8): in a
review, outside any test or reveal; in commentary, outside the reaction loop and before the
sincere payoff; in a documentary, at a clean boundary outside a rising sequence; in true crime,
before the second half, at a clean act boundary.
