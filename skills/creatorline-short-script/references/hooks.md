# Hooks

The first three seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest. This file is how to write the
hook options of a script: what a hook is made of, the seven families to draw from, what each
one owes the rest of the video, and the rules every hook keeps.

## 1. Three layers

A hook is three things at the same moment. Write all three for the recommended option.

| Layer | What it is | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Says | The first spoken line | Starts at 0 s, the most specific line of the video. 7 to 11 words for a 3-second beat, about two fewer when the hands hold the product |
| Reads | The on-screen text | 2 to 6 words at the top of the frame. It carries the promise for a viewer with the sound off. Shorter than the line, never the line word for word |
| Sees | The first frame | The speaker already in shot and in motion, the product already in hand, the thing the hook is about already visible. Nothing walks in, nothing arrives |

A viewer with the sound off gets the hook from the text and the picture. A viewer with the
sound on gets a line the text did not spoil.

## 2. The seven families

Each family works on the viewer in its own way. Every form below names what the rest of the
video then owes: an unpaid hook is worse than none. "By the payoff" means no later than the
payoff beat, half to three quarters of the way in.

### Name the payoff

The opener says what the viewer gets.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value promise | The benefit in one clause, with a count, a time or a price that makes it credible | "Three settings that stop your phone photos looking flat." | Every item delivered; a list keeps its count on screen to the end | "This will change your life": the constraint does the work |
| Intent | What is about to happen, then straight into doing it | "I'm folding a fitted sheet in four moves." | Doing it on camera, in that order, no filler | A past project the avatar never did |
| Result first | The finished thing on screen at frame one; the line reframes it | (the finished shelf) "This takes eleven minutes." | How it was done | A body, skin, weight or earnings result: only a made thing |
| Offer up front | The real offer, said flat over the product | "Two bags for the price of one, until Sunday." | The terms and what the product is | Terms, prices or deadlines the person did not give |

### Hold one thing back

The opener names a gap and keeps exactly one thing back. Withholding everything ("wait for
it") signals nothing.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One missing piece | Everything concrete except one detail: the reason, the method or the number | "One number on this wrapper matters more than the protein." | That one thing named, by the payoff | A second tease; hiding the label to reveal it later |
| "This" first | An unnamed "this" tied to something real that just happened | "This is why your iced coffee tastes watery." | Name it and show it | An answer so ordinary the gap was not worth it |
| A real question | One answerable question with a number or a time in it | "What do you actually need for your first 5K?" | The answer, in the video | "Comment yes if..."; a question nobody answers |

### Tell them they are wrong

The opener says the viewer believes something that is not so. It works only when this
audience really holds the belief; it backfires when they do not.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat no | A short, calm sentence that denies a common belief | "Your fridge isn't too small. It's set up wrong." | The belief that replaces it | Health, money or results claims the video cannot back; strawmen |
| Stop doing it | "Stop" plus a practice people in this niche really follow | "Stop rinsing your pasta." | What to do instead, specific enough to try | A bare "stop" or "listen" with nothing after it |
| Hidden cost | What the viewer is losing without knowing | "You're paying for phone storage you already have." | The mistake named and the fix shown | A cost with no diagnosis; fear in health or money |

### Own up

The speaker concedes something. Only these two forms: a confession about a past or a result
is a story the avatar never lived.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Almost did not post | A line that overrides its own hesitation and names the subject in the same breath | "I wasn't going to post this, but the cheap one wins." | The thing and the demonstration, then a flat verdict | A second setup beat; any personal result |
| The honest downside | A real downside, said first | "Yes, it's expensive." | Why the downside is worth it | A fake downside ("almost too effective"); never resolving it |

### Call out the viewer

The opener sorts the audience: the right viewer thinks "that is me" or "that is my friend".

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The situation | The viewer's exact situation, in their words, not a label for them | "If you're four tabs deep comparing the same two blenders..." | The answer for that exact situation | A demographic ("hey moms") |
| The objection first | The doubt the viewer already has, said before they can | "You're going to ask if it works on curly hair." | An honest answer, including where it is partly no | A screenshot of a comment nobody wrote |
| The friend | Someone else the viewer knows, played rather than described | "Every group chat has the one who still pays full price for flights." | Something worth sending to that friend | "Tag someone"; a caricature nobody recognizes |

### Let the picture do it

The frame is the hook; the line follows it. Keep it to what renders: small changes in one
shot, never impacts, falls or things flying in.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One thing off | An ordinary frame held a moment, then exactly one odd detail in the same shot | (a tidy desk, one mug of pens upside down) "Every pen on this desk is upside down on purpose." | The odd detail explained or used | Several oddities at once; anything needing a collision |
| Just noticed | The speaker turns to something already there, and the face changes a little | (looks at the open fridge, a small head tilt) "Who organized this?" | What was noticed and why it matters | A big gasp; something arriving from off screen |

### Start mid-thought

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | The sound starts in the middle of the strongest sentence of the video | "...and that's the part of the label nobody reads." | A video that makes sense without the missing start | Opening on a question the missing start was meant to answer |

**Labels that are hooks.** Some genres open on their own label, and the label is the text
layer: "POV: ..." (POV), "Myth: ..." (myth vs fact), a count ("3 things...", listicles and
tutorials). Pair the label with a spoken line from a family above.

## 3. Rules for every hook

- **Specific beats general.** A number, a named thing, a time, a place: "four grams of sugar",
  not "less sugar".
- **The first word lands at 0 s.** No greeting, no name, no logo. Never open on "So", "Okay",
  "Alright", "Hey guys", "Wait for it", "You won't believe", "Stop scrolling", "Let me tell
  you" or "Story time". Later in a sentence these are fine.
- **Paid by the payoff.** Write the payoff before the hook, then the hook that earns it.
- **Never** a request for comments, tags or follows in place of the payoff ("comment yes",
  "tag someone", "follow for part two"), a comment, review or DM nobody supplied, a credential, a personal history for the
  avatar, a rival's name, or a real person.
- **Small faces render.** A look, a pause, a head tilt. Big gasps and shouted openers do not.
- **The brand's `donts` bind the hook too.**

## 4. Writing 3 to 5 options

1. Draw each option from a different family. Five options from one family are one option
   reworded.
2. Label each with its form ("One missing piece") and mark the recommended one, with a reason
   of a few words ("the label is the proof").
3. Give each option its spoken line and its on-screen text. Give the first frame for the
   recommended one; the others share it unless you say otherwise.
4. Test each option against the body: read it, then beat 2. If an option needs a different
   payoff, say which beat changes with it ("with hook 3, beat 3 says...").
5. The script opens with the recommended option. The others swap in for the first beat only.

## 5. Hooks only

When the person asks only for hooks ("give me 10 hooks for..."):

- Write the number they asked for (10 when they named none), two or three per family, grouped
  under the family's name.
- Each hook: the spoken line and its on-screen text.
- Mark the three you would test first and why in one line each.
- End with: a script for the winner, and a hook test once a video exists (the same video with
  several openings, `creatorline-ad-variations`).

## 6. Which families fit which genre

| Genre | Strongest | Also good | Weak here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review | Hold one thing back | The honest downside, the objection first, result first | Flat no on the product's own category |
| Hot take | Tell them they are wrong | Start mid-thought | Value promise (too polite) |
| Storytime | Start mid-thought | "This" first | Offer up front |
| Tutorial | Result first | Intent, value promise | Let the picture do it |
| Listicle | Value promise (the count) | The situation, hidden cost | Start mid-thought |
| Myth vs fact | Flat no | A real question | The honest downside |
| POV | The situation (as the POV label) | Just noticed, one thing off | Value promise |

## 7. Weak and strong

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "Hey guys, today I want to talk about protein bars." | Warm-up, no gap, no number | "One number on this wrapper matters more than the protein." |
| "You won't believe what this does!" | Withholds everything, says nothing | "This is the one setting your air fryer manual skips." |
| "This changed my life." | Vague, and a personal result | "Three drops, and the dressing stops splitting." |
| "Comment if you agree!" | An engagement prompt, no payoff | "Your fridge isn't too small. It's set up wrong." |
| "As a nutritionist, I..." | A credential the avatar does not have | "Read the back first. The front is marketing." |
