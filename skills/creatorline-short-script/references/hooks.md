# Hooks

The first three seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest. This file is how to write the hook
options of a script: the three layers a hook is made of, the hook types of each spoken-word
genre, the seven families for UGC scene formats, the rules every hook keeps, and how to present
the options.

## 1. Three layers

A hook is three things at the same moment. Write all three for the recommended option.

| Layer | What it is | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Says | The first spoken line | Starts at 0 s, the most specific line of the video. 7 to 11 words for a 3-second beat, about two fewer when the hands hold the product |
| Reads | The on-screen text | 2 to 6 words at the top of the frame. It carries the promise for a viewer with the sound off. Shorter than the line, never the line word for word |
| Sees | The first frame | The key thing already in shot within 2 seconds: the speaker in motion, the product in hand, the artifact, the number. Nothing walks in, nothing arrives |

A viewer with the sound off gets the hook from the text and the picture. A viewer with the
sound on gets a line the text did not spoil.

## 2. Hook types by genre

A spoken-word script draws its options from its own genre's types: three options, three
different types, each labelled. "With the avatar" says how the avatar takes the type: it is a
living persona with its own receipts, tests, purchases and confessions (house rules, section 7).
"Sourced" marks a fact about the real world, which needs a source; "supplied" marks material
only the person can give (a headline, a recording). When the person speaks as themselves, what
you do not know of theirs is a marked blank. Every example is an illustration, never a fact.

### Business and self-improvement

Every business hook holds a specific number or a named contradiction. Non-round numbers per unit
of time read as receipts.

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receipts | A non-round result in a timeframe, then "here's the short version" | "Four hundred and twelve orders in nineteen days. Here's the short version." | Yes, the persona's own numbers |
| Blueprint | The map to an outcome: N steps, and "step one is..." | "Three steps to a pantry that never runs out. Step one is boring." | Yes |
| Paradox | Fact A, then the fact that contradicts it: how? | "The cheapest airline earns the most per seat. How?" | Yes, sourced |
| Second-person mirror | The viewer's exact week, in the present tense | "You open the banking app, wince, and close it again." | Yes |
| Inversion tutorial | How to stay stuck, step one | "How to never finish a side project. Step one: announce it." | Yes |
| Gate | "If you're <painful state>, this is for you" | "If your inbox has four digits, this one's for you." | Yes |
| Answer-first | The whole answer at once, then the deeper why | "Pay yourself first, automatically, on payday. Here's why it works." | Yes |
| Story in progress | A story already costing something | "Day four, and I've already lost two clients." | Yes, its own story |
| Headlines, then thesis | Two real headlines on screen, then the claim | (two headlines the person supplies) "Both are true. Here's why." | Only with headlines the person supplies |
| Borrowed anecdote | A documented story about a known company that carries the thesis | "A famous toy maker nearly went bankrupt selling too many toys." | Yes, sourced, never a quote you cannot source |

### Product and tech review

A hands-on hook holds at least one number and a first-person effort verb ("thirty days in").
The avatar's persona carries its own: its purchase, its month of use, its test. The person's:
ask them, or leave a marked blank.

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stunt ledger | Spend a sum fast on the most extreme version, then: dream or scam? | "I spent two hundred dollars on the priciest pan I could find." | Yes, its own spend |
| Fresh intel | A recency stamp, the work done, and a promise to sort it | "It came out Tuesday. Two things the box doesn't tell you." | Yes |
| Verdict-first tease | The verdict at once, then "here's why" | "Skip the pro model. Here's why." | Yes, as an opinion |
| Disaster cold open | The failure, live, then "and I did everything right" | (the cheap bottle leaks on the desk) "Filled to the line. Closed tight." | Only a failure shown on camera, of a generic product |
| Forbidden object | Someone said no, and here it is anyway | "The store said it was sold out for months." | Yes |
| Countdown bomb | Tease number one at the top, name it only at the end | "Number one surprised me, but first, number three." | Yes |
| Contrarian flip | Everyone says X; for most people X is wrong | "Everyone says buy the big one. Most kitchens shouldn't." | Yes |
| Character sketch, twist | Describe a kind of buyer, then turn it | "This is for people who never read manuals. So, everyone." | Yes |
| Payoff montage | The results first, then "here's how" (tutorials) | (the finished shelf) "Eleven minutes, one tool." | Yes, a result the first frame can show |

### Commentary

The artifact AND the stance are known by the end of sentence one.

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold-open sketch | Three to five seconds of the scene acted, then a snap to normal voice | (as a cheerful ad voice) "Unlimited plan!" (normal) "It's not." | Yes |
| Artifact first | The odd thing shown or read raw, then the reaction | (reads the fine print aloud) "...Okay, let's talk about that." | Yes |
| Discovery story | "People kept sending me this" | "Six of you sent me this ad this week." | Yes |
| Experiment stakes | A costly, irreversible commitment | "I followed this advice for a month. Here's the bill." | Yes, its own month |
| Verdict first | The stance, flat | "This ad is lying to you, politely." | Yes, as an opinion |
| Deadpan definition | A textbook definition, undercut within seconds | "A free trial: a trial that is free. In theory." | Yes |
| Fake-service guide | A helpful guide that delivers the opposite | "Welcome to the guide to ruining a first date." | Yes |
| Superlative, then a count | The most extreme one, then count something about it | "The longest terms I've seen. Fourteen thousand words." | Yes, with a real count |
| Personal confession | Something the speaker admits | "I fell for this twice." | Yes |
| Pre-announcement loop | What the viewer will believe by the end | "By the end, you'll never trust a free trial again." | Yes |

### Documentary story

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prize, stakes, preview | What was at stake, the prize, a glimpse of the end | "A whole harbor bet on one ship. It came back empty." | Yes, sourced |
| Date-stamped cold open | An exact date and place, an ordinary act in the present tense, one wrong detail | "March third, eighteen eighty-one. The keeper lights the lamp. It's noon." | Yes, sourced |
| Prophecy bomb | Added to a cold open: the subject does not know what is coming | "He has no idea this is his last normal day." | Yes, sourced |
| Misconception flip, with a comparison | The belief, then the fact against it, with a comparison | "We picture it as huge. It was smaller than a bus." | Yes, sourced |
| Superlative object | "This is X", two number comparisons, then how? | "This is the oldest lighthouse still working. Older than the country around it." | Yes, sourced |
| Investigator's confession | What the teller went through to find it | "I read three hundred pages of court notes for this." | Yes; the facts it found, sourced |
| Archive, then thesis | Archive pictures first, then the claim | (old photos the person supplies) "None of this was an accident." | Only with material the person supplies |
| Contradiction autopsy | The accepted answer, then why it does not hold | "The official story is a storm. The logbook disagrees." | Yes, sourced |
| Question ladder, what-if | A question that leads to a bigger one | "What if the map was wrong on purpose?" | Yes |
| Impossible narrator | A comedy retelling told by an object or a place | "I'm the lighthouse. I saw the whole thing." | Yes |

### True crime

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ranked superlative | Of the cases covered, one of the most unsettling | "Of every case on this channel, this one stays with me." | Yes, once the channel has other cases a viewer can scroll to |
| Located warning | The hard part plays at the end, discretion advised: a warning that is also a promise | "The call at the end is hard to hear." | Yes |
| Artifact tease | "We'll play it near the end" | "There's a note. We'll read it at the end." | Only with a real record |
| Victim clock | An exact time and date, an ordinary act, and they never came back | "Nine forty, a Tuesday. She walks out for milk." | Yes, sourced |
| Uniqueness | Why this case is unlike any other | "It's the only case solved by a parking ticket." | Yes, sourced |
| Normalcy trap | A perfectly ordinary day | "The most ordinary street in town. Then the dog stops barking." | Yes, sourced |
| Cut at the peak | The scene cut at its worst moment, then a rewind | "The door was open. Six hours earlier..." | Yes |
| Raw audio first | A real emergency or jail call first, the voice named after | (the person's supplied recording) | Only with a real record the person may use |
| Personal connection | The teller's own link to the case | "This happened two streets from where I grew up." | Yes, from the persona's own past; the case facts sourced |
| Concept, applied | A known idea, then the case that proves it | "Investigators call it the second look. It solved this." | Yes, sourced |
| Damning stack | Fact on fact, ending on one absurd number | "No alibi. No car. One receipt, for eleven cents." | Yes, sourced |
| Discovery framing | How the case was found, or the detail that broke it | "A librarian noticed the book was returned twice." | Yes, sourced |

### Explainer

Every hook plants a concrete image AND opens a question the video closes. Confident, no hedges.

| Type | The move | Example | With the avatar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artifact | "This is X", a sharp number, "but..." | "This is a sixty-cent part. It grounds whole airlines." | Yes, sourced |
| Misconception flip | Line one, the wrong belief; line two breaks it | "Your fridge isn't too small. It's set up wrong." | Yes |
| What-if, walked back | An escalating what-if, then "well, almost" | "What if your phone charged in a minute? Well, almost." | Yes |
| Impossible object | Something that should not exist | "This bridge is made of ice. In summer." | Yes, sourced |
| In medias res | Mid-moment, then a rewind | "The coffee is already cold. Here's why it happened so fast." | Yes |
| Body-count anecdote | A year, a place, the losses, and nobody knew why | "Eighteen fifty-four, London. Hundreds sick, and nobody knew why." | Yes, sourced |
| Dare | Try this, right now | "Close one eye and point at the corner. Now switch." | Yes |
| Authority shock | A surprising line from a trusted body | "The safety board's advice for a fire: don't open that door." | Yes, sourced |

## 3. The seven families, for UGC scene formats

POV, storytime as a scene, GRWM, unboxing and the other scene formats draw from these. Hooks only
mode also uses them when no genre is set. Every form names what the rest of the video then
owes: an unpaid hook is worse than none.

### Name the payoff

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value promise | The benefit in one clause, with a count, a time or a price that makes it credible | "Three settings that stop your phone photos looking flat." | Every item delivered | "This will change your life" |
| Intent | What is about to happen, then straight into it | "I'm folding a fitted sheet in four moves." | Doing it on camera, in that order | An intent the clip never carries out |
| Result first | The finished thing at frame one; the line reframes it | (the finished shelf) "This takes eleven minutes." | How it was done | A number drawn into the frame: earnings or counts go in as Video Studio text |
| Offer up front | The real offer, said flat over the product | "Two bags for the price of one, until Sunday." | The terms and what the product is | Terms the person did not give |

### Hold one thing back

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One missing piece | Everything concrete except one detail | "One number on this wrapper matters more than the protein." | That one thing named, by the payoff | A second tease |
| "This" first | An unnamed "this" tied to something real | "This is why your iced coffee tastes watery." | Name it and show it | An answer not worth the gap |
| A real question | One answerable question with a number or a time | "What do you actually need for your first 5K?" | The answer, in the video | "Comment yes if..." |

### Tell them they are wrong

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat no | A short, calm sentence that denies a common belief | "Your fridge isn't too small. It's set up wrong." | The belief that replaces it | A no the rest of the video never backs up |
| Stop doing it | "Stop" plus a practice people really follow | "Stop rinsing your pasta." | What to do instead | A bare "stop" |
| Hidden cost | What the viewer loses without knowing | "You're paying for phone storage you already have." | The mistake named and the fix shown | Fear in health or money |

### Own up

The persona has a past, so its purchases, habits and results are its own to confess (house
rules, section 7).

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Almost did not post | A line that overrides its own hesitation and names the subject | "I wasn't going to post this, but the cheap one wins." | The proof, then a flat verdict | A hesitation the video never explains |
| The honest downside | A real downside, said first | "Yes, it's expensive." | Why it is worth it | A fake downside |
| Personal result | A result the speaker had, with its time | "Three weeks in, and my cheeks stopped going red by noon." | What they did, and what changed | A vague result ("it changed my life") |
| The purchase | Why they finally bought it, or waited for it | "I waited three months for this to come back in stock." | Whether it was worth it | A purchase with no reason |

### Call out the viewer

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The situation | The viewer's exact situation, in their words | "If you're four tabs deep comparing the same two blenders..." | The answer for that situation | A demographic ("hey moms") |
| The objection first | The doubt the viewer already has | "You're going to ask if it works on curly hair." | An honest answer | A doubt nobody has |
| The friend | Someone the viewer knows, played | "Every group chat has the one who still pays full price for flights." | Something worth sending | "Tag someone" |

### Let the picture do it

Small changes in one shot only, never impacts, falls or things flying in.

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One thing off | An ordinary frame, then exactly one odd detail | (a tidy desk, one mug of pens upside down) "Every pen here is upside down on purpose." | The detail explained or used | Several oddities |
| Just noticed | The speaker turns to something already there; the face changes a little | (looks at the open fridge) "Who organized this?" | What was noticed and why | A big gasp |

### Start mid-thought

| Form | First line | Example | Owes | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | The sound starts in the middle of the strongest sentence | "...and that's the part of the label nobody reads." | A video that makes sense without the start | A question the missing start was to answer |

**Labels that are hooks.** "POV: ..." (POV), "Myth: ..." (myth vs fact), a count ("3
things..."). Pair the label with a spoken line.

## 4. Rules for every hook

- **Specific beats general.** A number, a named thing, a time, a place: "four grams of sugar",
  not "less sugar". Sharp numbers, never round ones.
- **Confident.** Hedges come later in the video, never in the hook.
- **The first word lands at 0 s.** No greeting, no name, no logo. Never open on "So", "Okay",
  "Alright", "Hey guys", "Wait for it", "You won't believe", "Stop scrolling", "Let me tell
  you", "Story time", "In today's video". Later in a sentence these are fine.
- **Paid.** Write the payoff before the hook, then the hook that earns it.
- **Never** a request for comments, tags or follows in place of a payoff, a comment card,
  review or DM drawn into the generated frame (it comes out garbled: Video Studio text, or an
  image the person supplies), another brand's name, a real creator's line, or a real person
  outside a documentary or true-crime record.
- **Small faces render.** A look, a pause, a head tilt. Big gasps and shouted openers do not.
- **The brand's `donts` bind the hook too.**

## 5. Writing the options

1. **Three options** by default (five when the person asks for more choice), each from a
   different type of the script's genre (section 2), or a different family for a scene format
   (section 3). Three options of one type are one option reworded.
2. Label each with its type and mark the recommended one with a reason of a few words ("the
   label is the proof").
3. Give each option its spoken line and its on-screen text. Give the first frame for the
   recommended one; the others share it unless you say otherwise.
4. Test each option against the body: read it, then beat 2. If an option needs a different
   payoff, say which beat changes with it ("with hook 3, beat 3 says...").
5. The script opens with the recommended option. The others swap in for the first beat only.

## 6. Hooks only

When the person asks only for hooks ("give me 10 hooks for..."):

- Route it first (router.md): a spoken-word genre draws from its types (section 2), a scene
  format from the families (section 3).
- Write the number they asked for (10 when they named none), two or three per type or family,
  grouped under its name.
- Each hook: the spoken line and its on-screen text.
- Mark the three you would test first and why, in one line each.
- End with: a script for the winner, and a hook test once a video exists (the same video with
  several openings, `creatorline-ad-variations`).

## 7. Which families fit which scene format

| Format | Strongest | Also good | Weak here |
|---|---|---|---|
| POV | The situation (as the POV label) | Just noticed, one thing off | Value promise |
| Storytime as a scene | Start mid-thought | "This" first, one missing piece | Offer up front |
| GRWM chat | Intent | Value promise, the honest downside | Stop doing it |
| Day in the life | Start mid-thought | The situation | Offer up front |
| Before and after | Result first | One thing off | A real question |
| Unboxing, haul | One missing piece | Just noticed, offer up front | Flat no |
| Try-on, fit check | Result first | The objection first | Hidden cost |
| App or website promo | Hidden cost | The situation, value promise | Start mid-thought |

## 8. Weak and strong

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "Hey guys, today I want to talk about protein bars." | Warm-up, no gap, no number | "One number on this wrapper matters more than the protein." |
| "You won't believe what this does!" | Withholds everything, says nothing | "This is the one setting your air fryer manual skips." |
| "This changed my life." | Vague: no thing, no time, no change you can picture | "Three drops, and my dressing finally stopped splitting." |
| "Comment if you agree!" | An engagement prompt, no payoff | "Your fridge isn't too small. It's set up wrong." |
| "As a nutritionist, I..." | Opens on who is talking, not on what the viewer gets; a credential lands harder in beat 2 | "Read the back first. The front is marketing." |
| "Today we're going to learn how vaccines work." | Announces the topic, no image, no question | "Your body keeps a most-wanted list. Here's how it's made." |
| "I tested this for a month." | The month is there, the result is not | "Thirty days in, and it still ends the day at forty percent." |
