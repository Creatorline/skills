# Script craft for a talking head

## Contents

2. Emotion tags
3. Length and pacing

## 1. Writing to be said

The script is spoken exactly as written, by the avatar, in one steady frame. Nothing cuts away
to hide a weak line, so every sentence has to sound good out loud.

- **Short sentences.** One idea each, under about 25 words. The run speaks the script in parts
  that end at full stops, so a very long sentence with no break can fail ("too long to say in
  one breath"). A comma is not a break; a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark is.
- **Spoken words.** Contractions, everyday words, "you" and "I". Read it aloud in your head: a
  line you would not say to a friend gets rewritten.
- **Numbers and symbols as said.** "twenty percent", "three steps", "five o'clock". Spell out
  what a voice would stumble on: a web address as "creatorline dot io", an abbreviation as it is
  spoken.
- **No stage directions in the words.** "(smiles)" or "*pause*" is read out loud. Feelings and
  pauses go in tags (section 2).
- **One language per script,** the language the voice should speak. Another language: write the
  script in it, never mix two.

## 2. Emotion tags

Tags are square-bracketed cues the voice performs and does not say. Use these:

| Tag | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `[excited]`, `[cheerfully]`, `[playfully]` | good news, a reveal, energy |
| `[curious]` | a question to the viewer, a hook |
| `[warmly]`, `[sincerely]`, `[softly]` | a personal or reassuring moment |
| `[confidently]`, `[emphatically]` | the key claim, the call to action |
| `[sighs]`, `[sad]`, `[hesitant]` | a problem, a confession, before the turn |
| `[laughs]`, `[chuckles]`, `[amused]` | a joke that is in the words |
| `[whispers]` | a secret, an aside |
| `[sarcastic]` | irony, only when the words are ironic |
| `[pauses]` | a beat before the payoff |

- Put a tag right before the words it colours: `[curious] Ever wondered why...`.
- About one tag every two or three sentences. A tag on every line sounds acted.
- A tag never adds a sound the words do not carry: no `[laughs]` unless something is funny.
- Tags stay in English whatever the script's language.

## 3. Length and pacing

About 150 spoken words a minute (2.5 a second). Tags are not counted.

| Length | Words | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | about 35 | a hook and one point |
| 30 s | about 75 | hook, two or three points, a close |
| 60 s | about 150 | hook, the problem, three points, a close |
| 2 to 3 min | 300 to 450 | hook, a promise of what is coming, sections of 3 to 5 sentences, a recap, a close |

Over 3 minutes is refused: split it into two videos and quote them together. Under about 5
seconds still films, but a talking head that short rarely earns a post.

## 4. Polishing someone's script

The person's words are the script. Polish means:

- keep every word, in its order;
- fix typos, spelling, punctuation and capitals;
- break a run-on sentence where a speaker would breathe (a full stop where a comma ran on);
- add a few tags (section 2);
- write numbers and symbols as they are said (section 1), and say so.

Anything more (cutting, adding, rephrasing, translating) is a rewrite: offer it in one line,
show it, and use it only on their yes. A script over 3 minutes is the exception you must raise:
say how long it runs and what you would cut.

## 5. Openings and closings that work

- **Open with the point or a question,** never with "Hi guys" or "In this video". The first
  sentence is the hook: a surprising fact, a problem the viewer has, a promise.
- **Close with one clear next step:** follow, comment, try it, the link in the bio. One, not
  three.
- **A long talk** says early what is coming ("three things, and the last one surprised me") and
  recaps before the close.
- **Truth.** The avatar speaks in its own persona. Claims about a product stay within what the
  brand can stand behind (house rules, section 7).
