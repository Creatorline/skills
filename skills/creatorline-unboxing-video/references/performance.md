# Performance: reaction, words and voice

How the avatar reacts, what it says and how it says it. These are the details that separate a
person opening a box from a model holding a product. Write them into the brief as plain
staging and quoted lines; the writer keeps what the brief makes specific.

## 1. Register: set by words

Choose the register only from explicit words: the person's ask, or the brand's tone in
`get_brand` (`brand_voice`). Never from the product's category alone.

| Register | Words that set it | The reveal | The lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural (default) | Nothing said | One human-sized peak at the reveal; an audible gasp is fine | Short and warm |
| Excited | hyped, energetic, explosive, high energy, viral energy | A different body event on every beat | Faster, louder, still short |
| Calm | goth, deadpan, clinical, refined, quiet luxury, minimal, runway-cool, quiet | A slow breath out, a soft "oh", a long look | Few words, slow hands |
| Silent | ASMR, satisfying, no talking | A smile and a breath | None |

Staged screaming is never right. The most believable reaction is a person who likes what they
see.

## 2. Expressions are body mechanics

- Write what the face and body DO, never a mood word: "brows lift, mouth opens, a short laugh
  through the nose", not "she is excited".
- Every beat names at least two small behaviors (a glance, a lip press, a head tilt, a breath)
  that never add a second action for the hands.
- Never the same expression twice in one video.

## 3. The one peak

A peak is a body event, motivated by the product. Pick from this menu:

- a sharp gasp, the free hand to the chest or the mouth;
- leaning back, caught mid-recoil;
- the jaw drops and stays down for a moment;
- a frozen double-take;
- a hand over the mouth, the eyes laughing;
- a palm slapped flat on the table beside the box;
- the product pressed to the cheek, eyes closed (satisfaction beat only);
- the product given a little shake toward the lens.

| Register | Peaks |
|---|---|
| Natural | One, on the reveal beat only |
| Excited | One on every beat, each a different one |
| Calm | None bigger than a breath and a held look |

A peak takes the place of one small behavior; it is never stacked on top. It uses the hands
that are free: when both hands hold the product, choose the lean back, the jaw drop or the
double-take.

## 4. Small human moments

- **One unguarded beat** per video: an eye flick away and back, a half-laugh at herself, a
  stumble over a word she recovers from.
- **One goofy beat** per video: an eyebrow waggle, a quick tongue-out face, a mock-thinking
  finger on the chin. Drop it for the calm register.
- **One sound from the room, optional:** a sound that belongs to the place (a kettle clicking
  off, a dog's tags jingling, a door down the hall), named exactly in the Sound line. The avatar
  glances toward it and is back to the lens within about 1.5 s, with no new words. Place it in
  the build-up of the sealed beat or in the product focus, never on the reveal peak and never
  in the closing beat.

## 5. Quirks

**A physical quirk,** only when the person names one (a habit, a mannerism): one beat per
section shows it, by default the product focus. Stage it big, about a third larger than
life, so it reads on a phone. It is a charming habit, never a medical condition, and never about
appearance, body or age.

**A residue moment,** optional, for products that leave a mark when used (food, a drink with
foam, a lip product, a sticky snack): after the action, one brief unguarded reaction (a thumb
wipes the corner of the mouth, a lip press, a grin with the crumb still there), staged big, with
one sound named in the Sound line. Never a second use of the product, never on the same beat
as the peak, and skipped for the calm register.

## 6. The hook

- Frame one is already moving: the hands are at the sealed box, the box is sliding, a knuckle
  is tapping. The first spoken word, or the first sound, lands within the first 0.4 s.
- The first word of every section is hook content, never a warm-up word.
- The first section's lines follow the beats: the hook; a gut reaction on the reveal, carried
  by a body event; one turn (one concrete detail, something unexpected about it, or a bit of
  the persona's story); a settled verdict. Later sections continue mid-thought.
- The first section may open on one to three small natural sounds before the line: a soft gasp,
  a delighted "oh", a small laugh. Bigger sounds (a loud gasp, a squeal) only in the excited
  register. Later sections open on no sound, never greet and never introduce the product again.
- Describe a sound in the beat ("she opens on a soft gasp"); never write it inside quoted
  lines, where it would be read out.

| Shape | Example line |
|---|---|
| The wait | "I've waited months for this. It's finally here." |
| The cave | "I caved. I finally ordered the big one." |
| The weight | "This box is heavier than it has any right to be." |
| The arrival | "It's here. First look at the new Glow C Serum." |
| The size gap | "Why is the box this big?" |
| The drop | "The whole spring drop, in one box." |
| The question | "What do you think fits in a box this small?" |
| The sound (silent register) | No words: the tape giving way in one slice |

The wait and the cave are the format's proven openings: the persona has a past, and its
purchase story is welcome (house-rules.md, section 7). Give the writer the hook line in quotes
when you want it word for word; otherwise give its shape and let the writer word it in the
avatar's voice.

## 7. Words never used

**As the first word of any section:** OK, okay, so, alright, yeah so, um, well, like, wait,
hold on, OMG, hey guys, guys, story time, so basically, stop scrolling, you need this, let me
tell you about.

**Anywhere:** obsessed (any form), you have to try this, game changer, 10/10, 100%, "literally"
as filler, holy grail, changed my life, hits different, elevate, seamless, effortless, the
"this is X, not Y" construction, and empty praise (amazing, so good, mind-blowing).

**Every claim carries one concrete:** a place on the body, a time of day, a size against the
hand, how long the persona waited for it or has used it. Never compared with another brand by
name. "It's so nice" says nothing; "it fits in my palm" says something. "Changed my life" is out
because it is vague, not because it is a result: say what changed.

## 8. Delivery marks in quoted lines

When you write the lines yourself in quotes, mark the delivery sparingly:

- at most two stretched vowels in the whole video ("sooo");
- one or two words in CAPS per beat's line, at most;
- one sentence that breaks off at the peak, written with an ellipsis;
- at most one whisper that rises to a spike;
- breaths named in the beat ("a breath in before the reveal") and in the Sound line;
- in the wordiest beat, one moment with the lips closed while the hands work: lip sync is where
  renders break, and a closed-mouth beat gives it a rest.

## 9. Micro-beats and sound

- Name the body part and the object for every small move: "her right thumb runs along the
  seam", not "she checks it out".
- One movement at a time. Moves written as happening together render as a glitch.
- Put small moves between spoken phrases, never on a key word.
- Every sound you want heard is named in the Sound line (the slice, the flaps, the tissue, a
  gasp, the lid's click). A sound only in the beat text may come out silent.
- Music only when the person asks; then one line naming its feel, with no lyrics. Otherwise:
  "voice and room sound only".

## 10. Accent, persona and other people

- **Accent** only when asked, or when the person mentions where the avatar is from. Describe it
  by its qualities ("a strong Glasgow accent, rolled r's, clipped vowels"), never by phonetic
  spelling. Write it two steps stronger than asked; never "slight", "subtle" or "light"; and
  add "a strong accent in every line" to the Language line. Copy that line word for word into
  every take's brief, because separately filmed takes drift apart otherwise. English lines with no accent asked: an American accent, unless the brand's
  `country` says otherwise.
- **Persona and face** come with the avatar. Never describe its face, age or ethnicity in the
  brief; name it only as `@Avatar1`.
- **Age-blind words:** never girl, boy, child, kid, young or teen. Write the avatar's name,
  `@Avatar1`, "she", "he" or "the woman".
- **A second person,** only when asked: their own image after the avatar's in `characters`
  (`@Avatar2`), a contrasting energy, each person on a fixed side of the frame for the whole
  video, and anything passed between them is one beat.

## 11. Endings and the loop

- The satisfaction beat always lands: a settled verdict in the persona's own voice ("this lives
  on my desk now", "worth every week I waited").
- The last section ends loopable. One section: end mid-gesture, or settle back into beat 1's
  framing without the box. Several sections: end mid-gesture (the opening frame is too far
  back to match).
- No spoken call to action. The writer turns one into a verdict anyway; "link in bio" or a code
  goes in the post caption or as Video Studio text.

## 12. Optional devices

**An accidental start,** at most one, only when beat 1 is a selfie: the phone is caught mid-drop,
the avatar walks in and sets the sealed box down (the box is under her arm only during this
entry), the frame pulls back from the plain tape seam, or a light is switched on. Describe what
the frame does, name its sound in the Sound line, and let it resolve into beat 1 within about
1.5 s. It does not replace beat 1's own action.

**A continuous pick-up of the phone** (propped turning into selfie without a cut) is not
available: our writer turns any move of the phone into a cut. The change from propped to selfie
happens on the cut between beats 3 and 4.

No device ever crosses the cut between beats 1 and 2: that cut hides the opening.
