# Hands and props

How a product is really handled on camera, so each step reads as real and the video model does
not invent hands, caps or sizes. Write what you need from here into each step's action in the
brief (steps-and-script.md, section 11).

## 1. Hands

- Two hands, and each one has a named job in every step: "the bottle in her right hand, the
  left one presses". A hand with nothing to do rests somewhere you name (on the counter, at her
  side).
- A selfie leaves one free hand: the other holds the phone, out of sight. A step that needs both
  hands is filmed from a propped phone.
- Three jobs at once (hold, open, apply) grow a third arm. Split them over two steps.
- The camera is the phone. The phone is never in the picture, and there is no mirror and no
  reflection to film it in.
- Start each step mid-action, not from a still pose.
- No fingers held up to count the steps: the labels count.

## 2. How products are used by hand

One row's sequence may spread over two steps; never pack two rows into one step.

| Product | What the hands do |
|---|---|
| Perfume or body mist | Hold it by the base. Lift the cap straight up; the cap is gone from then on. Press the nozzle once, a mist on the wrist or the side of the neck. |
| Dropper bottle | Unscrew the top counterclockwise. Lift the dropper out. Squeeze the bulb once. Drops land on the fingertips. Pat into the skin. |
| Jar | Twist the lid off and set it down. One fingertip scoops. Smooth it on. |
| Soft squeeze tube | Hold it at the middle. Flip the cap open or unscrew it. Squeeze a short line onto one fingertip. |
| Pump bottle | Lift the cap off if there is one. Two fingers press the pump once into the palm. |
| Spray (hair, cleaning) | Lift the cap straight off. One press, aimed at the target. |
| Lipstick or balm stick | Pull the cap off. Twist the base up. One swipe across the lips. |
| Mascara or gloss wand | Unscrew. Draw the wand out slowly. One sweep. |
| Compact or palette | Flip the hinged lid open; it stays attached. Tap the brush in. One sweep. |
| Sheet mask or sachet | Tear at the notch. Unfold. Lay it on and smooth it flat. |
| Powder with a scoop | Open the lid. One level scoop. Tip it into the glass or shaker. |
| Can or bottled drink | Open it in one motion. One sip. |
| Kitchen appliance | Set it on the counter (two hands when heavy). Load it. Press one button. Show what comes out. |
| Gadget or device | Hold it, present it to the camera, point to the part that matters. No cables, ports or button presses: they draw badly. |
| Scarf, belt, laces | One fold, loop or pull per step, both hands, slow. |

## 3. Where it goes on the body

| Product | Goes on |
|---|---|
| Fragrance, body mist | The inside of the wrist or the side of the neck; never the palm or the face |
| Serum, cream, lotion | The fingertips first, then the skin, pressed or smoothed |
| Lipstick, balm, gloss | The lips only |
| Powder, blush, bronzer | The cheekbone, with a brush |
| Mascara | The lashes |
| Eyeliner | The lash line |
| Hair oil, cream, spray | The hair only, mid-lengths to the ends |
| Food and drink | The mouth: one bite or one sip |

When the person's wording names a wrong spot (perfume on the palm), put the right one in the
brief without making it a question: the plan shows the step as it will be filmed.

## 4. One action, cause before effect

- One product interaction per step: one press, one scoop, one swipe, one sip.
- Nothing happens without its action on camera first: no drops on the fingertips before the
  bulb is squeezed, no foam before the pour.
- Fix how the mechanism works once, in the product card ("pump on top, pressed down, the
  lotion comes out of the nozzle into the palm"), and use the same words in every step that
  touches it.
- One container, one target: the drops go from this bottle onto these fingertips.
- Features the product does not have are described by what it has instead: "a plain screw
  cap" rather than "no pump". Write it in the product card and again in the step that uses
  that part (our writer drops sentences that only say what is absent).

## 5. Caps, state and repeat words

- The cap comes off as its own action before anything comes out, even when the product photo
  shows it closed.
- A cap that came off is gone for the rest of the video. It is never mentioned again and never
  put back. An opened product is never shown closed in a later step.
- Each step starts where the last one ended, and its action says so: "the bottle already open
  in her right hand".
- A state change that takes time (a pan heating, a mask drying) happens across a step
  boundary, never inside one shot.
- Never write these in an action; the model reads them as a loop: again, keeps spraying,
  repeatedly, twice, back and forth, opens and closes, unscrews and screws back, several
  coats, over and over. A quantity belongs in the spoken line ("two drops") while the action
  shows one squeeze.

## 6. Size

Write the product's real size into the product card, measured against a hand. Typical heights
(for a jar or a compact, the width):

| Product | Size |
|---|---|
| Perfume bottle | 10 to 12 cm |
| Large cologne | 13 to 16 cm |
| Serum bottle | 8 to 10 cm |
| Cream jar | 6 to 8 cm wide |
| Soft tube | 12 to 18 cm |
| Lipstick | 7 to 9 cm |
| Mascara | 10 to 12 cm |
| 250 ml pump bottle | 18 to 22 cm |
| Compact | 7 to 10 cm wide |
| Spray can or bottle | 15 to 20 cm |
| Drink can | about 12 cm (slim, about 16 cm) |
| Snack bag | 15 to 20 cm |

Never make the product bigger so its label reads: bring the camera closer instead (a close
step).

## 7. Weight and grip

| Weight | Examples | How it is held |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy | An appliance, a bottle of a litre or more | Both hands, a slight lean, visible effort |
| Bulky but light | A large box, a pillow, a big bag of crisps | Both hands, a relaxed face |
| Light | Most bottles, tubes, phones, cans | One relaxed hand |
| Tiny | A ring, an earring, a capsule | Pinched between two fingers, near the lens |

- A heavy thing lifted with one hand, or effort on a light one, reads as fake at once.
- A pair (earrings, two shoes): one in each hand, or one shown and one set down, or both side by
  side on a surface. Never both balanced on one palm. In a selfie only one of the pair is held.

## 8. Which side shows, and where the product is

- Only the side the product photo shows is ever on camera, label toward the lens. To show
  another side, the person must send a photo of it, and the turn happens on a step boundary.
  Never invent a back label or a side panel.
- The product is fully in hand, fully inside a closed box or bag, or standing on a surface.
  Never half out of a pocket, wedged somewhere, floating or balanced on an edge.
- One hero product in frame: no look-alikes and no second bottle of it.

## 9. Optional devices

None of these is needed. Use one only when it fits, at most one of each per video:

| Device | Rules |
|---|---|
| An opener moment | Only when step 1 is a selfie, and only in step 1: a finger wipes the lens, the light switches on, the frame pulls back to reveal the step's detail, or the focus settles. Its sound counts in the three sounds a video may have. |
| An off-screen sound | One sound true to the place (a kettle clicking off, a timer, a phone buzzing face down on the counter) at a step boundary. Never in the first 0.4 s or in the closing tail. The avatar glances toward it and is back within about 1.5 s, with no new words. Allowed in any set. |
| Set it down, pick it up | Only when the person wants it to feel like one unbroken take. Never across a step boundary that hides a change of state (closed becoming open). |
| Camera life | At most one small exposure or focus adjustment, in a selfie step only. A little shake only in selfie steps; propped steps stay locked. |

## 10. The product card for a tutorial

The shared product card (product-intake.md, section 3) plus four things a tutorial needs,
written once and reused word for word in every brief:

- **Size:** in cm against a hand (section 6).
- **How it opens and works:** the mechanism, fixed (section 4).
- **The side we have:** "front label only".
- **What it does not have,** said as what it has: "a plain screw cap".
- **Tier,** read from the packaging, never from a looked-up price: luxury, premium or
  everyday. It sets the room: a polished, calm bathroom for luxury; an ordinary lived-in one
  for everyday.
- **One honest mark of use,** when it fits: a fingerprint on the glass, a crease in the tube. A
  spotless product reads as a render.
