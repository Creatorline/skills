# Story mode and review mode

How a talking video with a product is built. Two modes, both first-class: story mode (the
product as one step of a scenario) and review mode (the avatar's own take: a testimonial, a
hands-on review, a demo). The lines themselves are in monologue-craft.md, the staging in
staging.md.

## 1. Which mode

| The person asks for | Mode |
|---|---|
| A video of the avatar with the product, "UGC", "a TikTok about my serum", no format named | Your pick for this product: review mode as a testimonial when the product works over days (skincare, supplements, sleep, an everyday tool); story mode when it shines in one moment |
| "Review", "testimonial", "demo", "show how it works", "your take on it", "I've been using it for a month" | Review mode (section 4) |
| A named format (hot take, GRWM, storytime, tips) | That format (formats.md); the product moment follows section 7 |
| No product at all | Creator-led: the format's beats; never ask for a product |

Say the mode and the shape in the plan as a default the person can veto ("a short story: Mia
is late, the serum is the one step she keeps"). Never ask which shape they want.

## 2. Story mode

- **One scenario** carries the whole clip: a place, a person, something at stake.
- **The hook holds the stakes**: what is wanted, what could go wrong, said or shown in the
  first second.
- **Exactly one "but then"**: the turn that opens the middle. Two turns crowd a short clip.
- **The person first, the product second.** It comes in as a supporting actor at about 40 to
  60 % of the runtime and does its visible job right then, or the avatar tells what it has done
  for it.
- **The ending resolves the scenario** with a verdict on what the product just did. The call
  to action lives in the caption: our server's writer turns a spoken one into a verdict anyway.
- **The deletion test**: take the product out and the story must still stand. If it falls
  apart, it is an ad pretending to be a story: pick another shape.
- **Three shapes, then one.** Sketch at least three shapes from section 3 for this product and
  brand, pick the one with the sharpest stakes, and state it in the plan.
- **The persona has a past** (truth-and-safety.md, section 4): the scenario may be its own
  history ("the week before my sister's wedding, my skin broke out"), and the product's job may
  be a result over time it tells. What a line promises the viewer to SEE is shown.

## 3. Story shapes

Each shape with the template it runs on. Name the shape in the brief's Idea line.

| Shape | What happens | `ugc_style` |
|---|---|---|
| Demonstration discovery | The avatar works out what the product does while using it; the discovery lands on camera | `Auto` |
| Mechanism loop | Built around the product's own mechanism (the click, the pump, the twist), opened and closed on it so the end runs back into the start | `Product ad` |
| Alternate use | A second, real use of the product the label or the page supports | `Auto` |
| Guided comparison | Two ways of doing a thing, compared only by what the camera can see (texture, steps, how it works); never a named rival | `Talking head` |
| Behind the concept | Why the product is built the way it is, from label or page facts | `Talking head` |
| Interrupted storytime | A story told to the lens is cut short by the product moment, then finished | `Storytime` |
| GRWM with stakes | Getting ready for something that matters (an interview, a first date); the product is one step | `GRWM` |
| New-place mini-vlog | First moments in a new place; the product is one of them | `Day in the life` |
| Day in the life with a twist | Moments through a day and one turn | `Day in the life` |
| Staged product reveal | The product revealed with a little theatre (from behind the back, out of a pocket), then used | `Product ad` |
| POV frame | "POV: you..." drops the viewer into the scene | `Storytime` |
| Process win | A work task (a small business, a team) done cleaner or simpler, shown on camera | `Talking head` |
| FAQ reply | The avatar answers a question the person supplies, or one its viewers would ask; the question goes on screen afterwards as Video Studio text (a comment-reply card is fine there), never drawn into the generated frame | `Talking head` |
| Green-screen commentary | The avatar comments on a picture the person supplies, placed beside or behind her afterwards in Video Studio | `Talking head` |
| Feature checklist | Features gone through one at a time, a nod for each, never counted on fingers | `Talking head` |
| Host question | The avatar asks the viewer one question and answers it | `Talking head` |
| Baseline to demo | The starting state, then the demonstration on camera; a result that needs time is told, or cut from two takes in Video Studio | `Before & after` |
| Personal turnaround | The avatar's own before, the moment it caved and tried the product, what changed since | `Storytime` |
| Silent flex | No words: looks, gestures and the product's own sounds (section 5) | `Auto` |
| ASMR | Whispered, close, built on sounds (section 5) | `Auto` |

- Process win is the shape for a B2B or small-business brand.
- A shape must fit what the product really does and what the camera can show; when none fits,
  the plain format (formats.md) is better than a forced story.

## 4. Review mode

The avatar's own take, in its own words. Pick ONE compact skeleton. Two to four spoken lines in
all; the first line carries the most specific thing: a result, a duration, a detail the camera
can see. When the person gave an approved-claims list, keep to it.

| Skeleton | The beats |
|---|---|
| Testimonial | The hook (the result, or the doubt it had); the problem; how it found the product or caved; how long and what changed; a verdict |
| Purchase story | "I caved" or "I waited months for this"; why; what it is like now |
| Results over time | Day one against a month in, told; the product in hand now |
| Problem, agitate, solve | The problem in one line; why it bites; the product doing its visible fix |
| Before, after, bridge | The before state, shown; the after, shown on camera; how it got there |
| Hook, story, offer | A hook; a tiny scene; the product (an offer only when the person supplied one, and it goes in the caption) |
| Us against them | The product against a lazy, overpriced default way of doing it, never a named brand |
| "Watch this" | About 5 seconds: "watch this" and the demonstration, nothing else |

Before, after, bridge: a before the avatar tells needs nothing more; a before the viewer SEES
is a change made on camera (a look, a surface, a setup) or two takes cut together in Video
Studio, since one take cannot show weeks passing. The template is `Product ad` (`Talking head`
for a testimonial), the product in `objects`.

## 5. Quiet, silent and ASMR clips

| Register | Words | What carries it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet, process-led | At most 20 per 15 s | The sounds, each named exactly and placed (the pump's click, the cap's snap, water on the sink) | The product is one honest step of a routine; skincare starts bare-faced. Fits "quiet", "GRWM", "routine", "process" |
| Silent flex | None | Looks, gestures and the product's own sounds | No `Lines:`, no voice step; `Sound: room sound and the product's own sounds` |
| ASMR | At most 15, whispered | Close framing; each trigger named with its moment (tapping the lid, the peel of a seal) | Write "a clean frame with nothing written on it": the model tends to draw karaoke subtitles into ASMR |

**Music** only when the person asks for it: one line in the brief, "a soft instrumental bed,
low under the voice, no singing". Never with the voice step planned: then the music goes on
afterwards in Video Studio.

## 6. The beats by length

The full arc has eight beats: hook, context, a slice of life, the first touch or the story's
turn, the core action, the reaction, the result, the verdict and settle.

Our server's writer puts at most two beats in one shot and keeps every shot at 3 seconds or more
(video-briefs.md, section 6). So the beats pair up:

| Length | Shots | The beats, shot by shot |
|---|---|---|
| 10 s | 3 | Hook and context; the turn or first touch and the core action; the result and the verdict (the slice of life drops, the reaction rides on the result) |
| 15 s | 4 | Hook and context; a slice of life and the turn or first touch; the core action and the reaction; the result and the verdict |
| 30 s | 8, in two sections | Section one, setting up: the grab, the setup, context, a slice of life, the first touch of the product, it develops, a reaction, the lead-in. Section two, closing: the application, the effect, a close look at the moment, the reaction, the result, the verdict, the settle, the final look |

- Past 20 seconds the writer builds sections. The second section never greets or re-introduces
  anything: it starts mid-thought where the first one stopped.
- A greeting or a product introduction belongs only to the opening, and the banned first words
  still apply (monologue-craft.md, section 3).
- With a script or shot list from the person, map their beats onto the shots one for one, in
  their order.

## 7. Where the product enters

- **Story mode**: at about 40 to 60 % of the runtime (15 s: around second 6 to 9; 30 s: around
  second 12 to 18). Before that, the person and the stakes.
- **Before it enters** it is fully inside a closed bag, box or pocket, or held upright and
  whole in one hand, or out of frame (staging.md, section 3).
- **Review mode and the mechanism-first hook**: in hand from the first frame, label to camera.
- **Shot 1** is the setup of the moment, already mid-event (mid-reach, already wincing), never
  a product shot by obligation.
- The biggest body reaction of the clip goes to the moment the product enters.

## 8. The ending and the loop

- The last shot ends in motion, or, in a short clip, on a frame that matches the first shot, so a
  replay runs on without a seam. The loop is visual only: never repeat a line.
- The verdict still lands inside that last shot.
- The call to action goes in the caption (`creatorline-publish`) or on screen as Video Studio
  text, never as the spoken ending.
