# Staging

What the camera, the hands and the product do in a talking video, so the take renders clean.
Write these as plain cues inside the brief's Idea, Setting and shot lines. The server's writer
directs the rest; it never sees what you leave out.

## 1. Selfie or propped

| Phone position | What it allows | Brief wording |
|---|---|---|
| Selfie | One hand holds the phone, never seen. The other hand holds at most ONE thing: the product or a bag, never both | "arm's-length selfie" |
| Propped | The phone stands steady at arm's length, facing her. Both hands are free: presenting, opening, twisting, two-handed applying | "phone propped on the counter" |

Any action that needs two objects or two hands is a propped shot. Worked cases:

- Walking with a bag while the product shows: cannot be a selfie. The product goes into the
  bag, or the bag goes, or the shot is propped.
- Opening, pumping or applying while the phone is in hand: propped.
- The product close up in the palm: a propped close shot.

## 2. Hands

- Give each hand one job in every shot, and park the other one in words: "her left hand rests
  flat on the counter", "her right hand holds the phone, out of frame".
- Never more than two jobs at a time. A shot whose action needs a third hand grows a phantom arm,
  and a product floating beside busy hands does the same. Rest the product on a surface instead
  of adding a hand to hold it.
- A task with steps spreads across shots: shown in one shot, opened in the next. Never all of
  it in one beat.
- Never count on fingers: a nod for each item, or the voice alone.

## 3. The product in frame

- In frame only when the shot's action needs it. Otherwise it is fully inside a closed bag, box
  or pocket, or held upright and whole in one hand, or out of frame.
- Never half out of a bag, balanced on a bag, wedged in somewhere, floating or peeking in.
- Present from the first second of its shot, label to the camera. A thing that enters later is
  redrawn and drifts from its photo.
- Write the cap coming off before it is used, even when the photo shows it on. Once off, the cap
  is gone: never mention it again. One state per prop per beat (never a cap on the counter while
  the bottle still wears one).
- One interaction with the product per shot. What is opened stays open.
- The right place on the body, whatever the person's wording said (correct it quietly):

| Product | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Perfume, body mist | Wrist or neck, never the palm or the face |
| Cream, serum | A fingertip first, then the face or the hands |
| Lipstick, balm | The lips |
| Drink | To the mouth |
| Powder, blush | The cheek, with a brush |
| Mascara | The lashes |
| Eyeliner | The lash line |
| Foundation | A fingertip or a sponge, then the face |
| Hair product | The mid-lengths to the ends |
| Food | The mouth |
| Anything not for the body | Its own surface: the carpet, the screw, the jug |

- Residue products (foam, gloss, chocolate, crumbs, drips) may get one short after-beat (a
  thumb-wipe, a lip press), in place of another small gesture.

## 4. A different picture at every cut

The model cuts cleanly only when the two shots on each side of a cut look far apart. Neighbours
that look alike melt into each other and the face morphs.

- Every cut changes at least one of: phone position (selfie or propped), distance (close,
  medium, wide), the action. Moving to another spot in the room is the strongest change.
- No two neighbouring shots at the same distance. Across a 30 s clip each distance comes back
  at least twice and never three times in a row.
- The default order, taken from the start: selfie medium, propped wide, propped close, selfie
  tight, propped medium, propped close, propped wide, selfie tight. 10 s takes the first three,
  15 s the first four, 30 s all eight.
- Write it into the shot cues ("arm's-length selfie at the sink; then the phone propped on the
  shelf, wider"), without timestamps: the writer sets the seconds.
- A propped shot carries no handheld words (shake, drift, wobble, sway, subtle movement): they
  leak motion into a still frame.

## 5. Setting and light

The tier is read from the packaging only (luxury, premium or drugstore), never from a price
lookup. A setting the person gives, or a `location` photo, wins.

| Product | Setting |
|---|---|
| Luxury fragrance | A designer bedroom or vanity; a silk robe or a curated outfit |
| Premium skincare | A bright modern bathroom; a cotton robe |
| Other makeup and fragrance | A vanity in a bathroom or a bedroom |
| Skincare, hair, body | A bathroom |
| Food and drink | A kitchen, finished to the tier |
| Supplements | A kitchen after a workout, or a home gym |
| Luxury fashion | A dressing room |
| Clothing, jewelry | A bedroom or a dressing room |
| Fitness gear | A home gym or a yoga corner |
| Cars | Outdoors by the car, neutral midday light |
| Outdoor gear, sunscreen | A cafe terrace or a park |
| Tech | A desk or a living room |
| Decor, candles | A living room |
| Cleaning | The surface it cleans |
| Pet products | The pet's corner |
| Tools | A workshop |
| Anything else | A cosy home |

- **Light**: neutral daylight from one side window. Write it in the Setting line: left to
  itself the writer may pick golden-hour light. No golden hour, sunset or amber light and no
  studio lights unless the person asks.
- **The room holds the product's world**: the bathroom shelf for a serum, the desk for a mouse.

## 6. Wardrobe

The avatar's image decides the outfit unless the brief says otherwise. Write a wardrobe line only
when the outfit matters or changes.

- Never describe the avatar's face, hair or body: the face comes from the avatar. Write
  `@Avatar1`, never a description of her.
- One outfit per place, kept the same in every video of a series shot there.
- The tier sets the materials: luxury is silk, cashmere, fine gold; premium is good cotton;
  drugstore is everyday cotton and plastic clips.
- Rules that make an outfit read styled: one fitted piece with one loose piece; a one-colour base
  with ONE metal; texture over print; white ribbed socks; glasses or headphones worn like
  jewelry; one deliberate imperfection (sleeves pushed up, one side untucked); accessories as a
  set of 2 or 3 related pieces.
- Any print or lettering on a garment is big and made up. Small logos come out garbled, and a
  real brand's name or character never appears.
- The outfit shares at least one colour with the room.
- Modesty, written as what is there: a top closed at the collarbone, a high-coverage fit, a robe
  tied tight with the lapels overlapping, never a tank top or camisole on its own. Write "a cream
  crew-neck tee", never "no cleavage": a named thing gets drawn.

## 7. Casting the look for the product

Start from the look the product works on, so the demo is honest:

| Product | The avatar starts |
|---|---|
| Skincare, cosmetics (makeup too) | Bare-faced |
| Hair care | Hair down and untouched |
| Teeth products | Natural teeth |
| Sleep or energy | Faint shadows under the eyes |
| Fitness | A light post-workout flush |
| Fashion, jewelry | Fully styled |

The starting look is never shown changing because of the product unless the change happens on
camera in the clip (makeup going on, hair being styled). A result that needs time is told, or
shown across a cut (formats.md, section 12).

## 8. Two or three people

- At most three people in one shot.
- A second avatar: its `image_url` in `inputs.characters`, `@Avatar2` with its role.
- A person who is not an avatar gets one fixed description (role, clothing), repeated word for
  word every time they appear, a different type and voice from the avatar.
- Each person keeps a side of the frame (left or right) and a direction they face; say it again
  after each cut.
- An object passes between them in a single beat.
- Someone who leaves the frame is gone for the rest of the clip.
- Two voices: no voice step.

## 9. Camera moves

- A move is opt-in: at most one per shot, never on every shot. A slow push-in or a pull-back on
  the reveal or the reaction shot.
- A propped shot may carry one deliberate push-in.
- A quick handheld pinch-zoom toward the face works as a real-phone opener.
- Camera-arrival devices for a selfie first shot are in monologue-craft.md, section 3.

## 10. The product card, finished for video

Add these lines to the card from product-intake.md, section 3, and reuse the whole card word for
word in every brief:

```
Category: <exact: serum, mascara, protein powder>
Opens by: <uncap, unscrew, pull tab, flip top, press pump: always shown before anything comes out>
Used by: <spray, squeeze and apply, pump, swipe, brush, drop, scoop, swallow, mix>
Tier: <luxury / premium / drugstore, from the packaging>
Size: <against a hand, plus centimetres: "fits in one palm, about 10 cm tall">
Mechanism: <which part is where, what moves, where the product comes out: one phrase>
Has only: <what it lacks, said as what is there: "a smooth cordless body; the trigger is the only control">
Detail: <one honest imperfection: a faint scuff on the cap, a fingerprint on the glass>
```

- Size and how it opens are the two facts most often wrong when guessed: ask when the photo
  cannot tell.
- A missing feature named only as an absence comes back in the render; describing what is there
  instead keeps it out.
- With a real photo the label renders as designed: never ask for it turned away.
