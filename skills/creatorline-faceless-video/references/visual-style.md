# Visual style

How every picture of one video comes out in one look: the style line, the look reference, the
roster of figures, places and props, the shot pictures composed from them, motion briefs,
Picture story frames, and how the video stays faceless. All picture and motion briefs are
written in English; only the narration uses the brand's language.

## 1. One look: two things, used every time

Consistency comes from two things, and only from these two, repeated on every call:

1. **The style line.** 80 to 100 words in at most two sentences: the line and surface work,
   the shading, the palette, one signature accent colour, how the background is treated, the
   motion timing, and the render discipline, always ending as an illustration, never a
   photograph. It is pasted word for word as the first line of EVERY picture and motion brief.
   Never paraphrase it between calls; the repetition is the whole mechanism.
2. **The look reference.** One picture made first in the look (section 3) and attached to every
   later picture call as a reference. The person's own look images, when they sent some,
   replace it as the reference.

The server's picture writer rewrites each brief, so a look named once and loosely gets lost;
named exactly and repeated with its reference attached, it holds. Every look is
non-photorealistic: drawn, painted, cut or modelled. Real-looking footage comes only from the
stock library or the brand's own footage (section 12), never from an AI picture.

Never name a living artist, a studio, a film, a comic, a game or a brand in a style line or in
an option you show ("in the style of..."). Describe what the look is made of.

## 2. Looks to offer

Offer the format's default first ([formats.md](formats.md), section 1) and two or three others,
each with its one-liner. The style line is for the briefs, not for the person. Two motion
families: **flat** looks move as drawn animation on twos (each drawing held for two frames),
**dimensional** looks move like handmade stop-motion.

**Editorial collage** · "Black-and-white photo cutouts on cream paper, one accent colour" ·
History and Explainer (the flagship) · flat
> Editorial paper collage on a warm cream paper stage: black-and-white halftone cutouts with a
> thin white keyline and a soft drop shadow, flat printed shading, a muted palette of cream,
> ink black and warm grey with exactly one accent of signal red, and a background that stays
> the same plain paper stage in every shot. Motion is limited cut-out animation on twos, pieces
> slide, snap and stamp into place by hand, everything reads as printed paper and scissors, an
> illustration made of paper, not a photograph.

Its own rules: three layers in every shot (the paper stage, the cutout subjects, foreground
devices). Rotate one or two signature devices per shot: a big accent-colour disc behind the
subject; exactly one element popped into the accent colour; a giant hand placing a prop; a
timeline ribbon with tick marks and no numerals; a marker circle drawn onto one detail; a
dot-matrix chart; cards pinned and taped to the stage. A second angle is a new arrangement of
the same stage. A dark pinboard of evidence appears at most twice per video. Choose the accent
once per video; red is the default.

dimensional
> A handmade miniature paper diorama built from aged sepia newsprint and card, folded and
> glued figures and buildings with visible paper fibres and torn edges, warm tungsten light
> from one side with soft falloff, shallow tilt-shift focus that makes everything look
> table-sized, a palette of sepia, cream and dusty brown with one accent of deep teal, and a
> background that is always more paper set, never a sky photo. Motion is smooth handcrafted
> stop-motion, small and deliberate, a tabletop world of paper, not a photograph.

Its own rules: figures are anonymous through a rotating device: a blank face, turned away, in

third of the blocks). Figures are told apart by silhouette and size, not by colour. One small
letterpress label of one or two words on a prop is allowed per block; it is checked like any
words in a picture and dropped after one failed try (section 9).

**Clay figures** · "Smooth faceless clay figures, each role its own colour" · Explainer, Top-N,
History · dimensional
> Smooth featureless clay figures with rounded bodies and blank oval heads, each role in one
> solid matte colour that never changes, set in a simple clay world of soft rounded props on a

> where every colour belongs to one role, and an accent of warm yellow for the one thing that
> matters in the shot. Motion is smooth handcrafted stop-motion with tiny squash and settle, a
> tabletop clay world, not a photograph.

Its own rules: colour IS identity, so a role keeps its colour in every shot. The first figure
made is the body reference for all the others. Give feminine figures their own base shape.
Animals are creature-shaped clay forms. Crowds are fully modelled, never painted on. Show
strength by size and posture; never describe muscles.

**The person's own look** in words: write a style line in the same shape, 80 to 100 words, two

## 3. The look reference

Made first, once, before anything else. It is a reference only: it is never shown in the video.

- **What it shows:** a small, pretty, readable vignette with ONE simple subject from the topic
  (a lantern on a rock, a coin on a table), so the line weight, shading and colours read at a
  glance. Never a palette strip, swatches, labels or a layout of several panels: the reference
  goes into every later call, and anything drawn in it leaks into the video.
- **With a mascot:** the reference is the mascot instead: one full-body character, expressive,
  a clear readable silhouette, looking at the camera, centred on a simple background.
- **The call:** `generate_text_to_photo` with `brand_id`,
  `params: { aspect_ratio: "2:3" }` (the frame every later picture uses), and the prompt:
  `<style line>. A small vignette: <the one subject>, centred, on the look's own background.`
- **From the person's images:** `generate_photo_edit`, `include_avatar: false`,
  `inputs: { other_elements: [<their images>] }`, `params: { aspect_ratio: "2:3" }`, and a
  prompt that opens: "Take only the rendering and the colour grading of the input images, mixed
  if there are several. Never their people, words, logos or objects. Then draw: <vignette>."
- Tell the person in one line, when you show it, that the look reference may look a little odd:
  it is a sample of the look, not a scene from the video.
- If it cannot be made after the retry ladder ([checks.md](checks.md), section 6), stop: say
  the look could not be set, what was tried, and offer another look. Nothing else runs without
  it.

## 4. The roster

Every figure, place and prop the script needs, made once from the look reference, before any
shot picture. A shot made without the asset it needs drifts. Every roster call:
`generate_photo_edit` with `brand_id`, `include_avatar: false`,
`inputs: { other_elements: [<look reference>] }`, and the style line first in the prompt,
followed by "@Input1 is the look reference: copy its look only, never its subject."

| Asset | `aspect_ratio` | The brief after the style line |
|---|---|---|
| Figure | `2:3` | `<the figure line>`, full body, centred, standing on a plain flat `<colour>` backdrop, a distinctive readable silhouette |
| Place | `2:3` | `<interior or exterior>` with its key furnishings and ONE named anchor object and where it stands (`"the brass lamp on the table by the window"`), empty of people, a wide establishing view |
| Second angle of a place | `2:3` | The same place from `<the reverse | the side | a close detail>`, the anchor object still where it was, empty of people. Add the place picture as a second reference |
| Prop | `1:1` | One `<object>`, isolated, centred on a plain backdrop, no hands, no scene |
| Insert (optional) | `2:3` | The subject on a solid colour card; a pop-up diagram drawn in the look; an object with a face |
| Mascot (when chosen) | `2:3` | The look reference itself is the mascot (section 3) |

- **Places:** enough that no place carries more than 2 blocks in a row. A 1 min video wants
  2 or 3, a 2 min video 4 to 6, each with one or two second angles.
- **The thread object always gets a prop picture.**
- Start the roster calls together, at most 6 at a time without waiting between them, then wait
  for the group (SKILL.md, the flow). Runs past the workspace's parallel limit queue and start
  on their own.
- Total the roster before the price: figures (times eras, section 5) + places + second angles +
  props + inserts.

## 5. The thread, recurring figures and eras

- **A figure line** is written once and pasted word for word wherever the figure appears:
  "the keeper: a tall old man in a long yellow oilskin coat and a grey wool cap, always seen
  from behind". Same words, same clothes, every time.
- **A mascot** (when chosen) is the look reference itself, attached to every shot it is in. In
  block 1 it greets the viewer with a gesture, mouth closed; in the last block it waves goodbye;
  in between it appears only where it helps the line, always the same design. It never talks.
- **The thread** (formats.md, section 2) is named the same way in every shot brief, with its
  state in that block: "the brass lamp, burning bright" in block 1, "the brass lamp, flickering
  low" in block 5.
- **Eras.** When a story spans years and a person ages or changes, split it into eras and make
  one figure picture per era the figure appears in. Repeat the figure's unchanging traits word
  for word in every era's brief, then add that era's changes. Attach the FIRST era's picture as
  a reference to every later era (and the previous era too when there are three or more), never
  build a later era from the look reference alone: that draws a stranger. Name them
  (`keeper_young`, `keeper_old`) and use only the right one in each block. Places get era
  versions only when the story changes them.

## 6. Shot pictures

Every shot of every block is one picture composed from the roster: a full staged scene, never a
lone object on blank white, never drawn from the look reference alone. `generate_photo_edit`:

```json
{
  "brand_id": "<brand id>",
  "include_avatar": false,
  "inputs": {
    "location": ["<the place, or its second angle>"],
    "other_elements": ["<look reference>", "<figure>", "<prop>"]
  },
  "params": { "aspect_ratio": "2:3" },
  "prompt": "<brief>"
}
```

The brief, in this order:

```
Style: <the style line, word for word>
Palette: only the colours and the background treatment of @Input1; no new colours, nothing recoloured
References: @Input1 is the look reference (its look only, not its subject). @Location1 is <the place>. @Input2 is <the figure, by name>. @Input3 is <the prop>.
Shot: <size and angle>, <what this shot shows: one subject, one moment caught mid-action>
Figures: <who is in it and how they stay anonymous>
Thread: <the thread object and its state in this block>
Frame: a tall frame, the subject in the upper middle, a calm lower third
```

- **References, at most 7 per picture**, in this order: the place in `location`, then in
  `other_elements` the look reference, the figures, the props. Over 7: drop extra props first,
  then spare second angles; never the place, the look reference or a figure who is on screen.
- **Show what the line names.** A line about spices puts the sacks and the scales on screen.
- The lower third is for captions and the apps' buttons: keep it calm.
- `generate_photo_edit` has no 9:16; `2:3` is the closest. The 9:16 frame crops a little off each
  side, so keep the subject centred.

## 7. Shot variety

- **Every shot differs from the one before in BOTH size and angle.** Sizes: wide, medium,
  close-up, extreme close-up. Angles: eye level, low, high, overhead, from the side, macro,
  over the shoulder.
- **Over the shoulder only with a named figure's shoulder and head in the foreground.** In a
  shot of objects, a diagram or an empty place it makes the model invent a person: use
  overhead, macro, low, high or a side view instead.
- **A wide establishing shot only opens the first block in a new place.** Later blocks there
  open on a close-up, a medium shot or a second angle.
- **At most 2 blocks (about 20 seconds) in one place or at one distance,** then a new place, a
  second angle or an insert. Move the subject's distance and its place in the frame; never park
  it back in the opening framing.
- **Rotate the kind of device:** a scene, a map, an object or quantity, a close human moment.
  Never three of one kind in a row.
- **Quantities are pictures.** When the line cites money, armies, years or sizes, show a
  comparison (stacks of coins, rows multiplying, a bus beside the whale), with no numerals in
  the picture.
- **History: at least one map beat**, and from 2 min on, one per stretch of the story.
- **A motif ledger.** A recurring action beat (a stamp, a seal, a signature, a march) appears at
  most twice in a video and never in two blocks in a row. The thread is the exception, but each
  of its beats shows a new state.

## 8. Keeping it faceless and safe

- **People are anonymous.** From behind, in silhouette, far away, as hands at work, the face
  under a hat brim or turned to the scene, at a medium shot or wider. Faces drift from picture
  to picture, and a real person must never be recognizable.
- **Real people, living or historical, are named in the narration only.** On screen they are
  an archetype in the right clothes, told apart by silhouette and build, from behind or far
  away. No celebrity, politician or anyone who has not agreed.
- **The brand's avatar stays out.** `include_avatar: false` on every `generate_photo_edit` call.
  Describe every figure fully so none comes out looking like the avatar.
- **Figures read as adults.** Never a young person in a picture. Never write "child", "kid" or
  "childlike" in a brief: write "small", "simple" or "naive" for the look.
- **Calm staging.** Avoid tight close-ups of animal faces, and aggressive or intimate
  poses. A figure that could read as unclothed becomes a shadow with one glowing feature.
  Clay figures never have muscles described: size and posture show strength.
- **Style images the person sends** give the look only. Decline an image that is mainly an
  identifiable real person, and never one of a minor.
- No personal data in any brief: scene and style words only.

## 9. Words, numbers and grim things in pictures

- **No words or numerals inside pictures.** Image models spell badly. Describe signs, pages,
  screens and labels as plain or blank. Titles, labels and numbers go on as Video Studio text
  (`add_text`), where they are exact; the narration says the rest.
- **A beat about words becomes a device:** a highlight bar across blank lines, a black
  redaction block, a marker circle, a timeline ribbon with tick marks, or a symbol (a warning
  triangle, a coin, a check mark, a cross).
- **The diorama's one label** (section 2) is the only exception: one or two words, one try; if
  they come out wrong, redo the shot without the label.
- **Grim things become safe images:** an explosion becomes an hourglass running out, a dam
  cracking, or a fuse reaching a barrel; death becomes architecture, light and nature (a
  gateway, a torch going out, mist over ruins, moonlight on an empty field).

## 10. Motion briefs

Animated mode only. Each shot picture becomes a short moving take with `generate_photo_to_video`:

```json
{
  "brand_id": "<brand id>",
  "model": "google-gemini-omni-flash-image-to-video",
  "inputs": { "image_url": "<the shot picture's asset id>" },
  "params": { "duration": 3, "aspect_ratio": "9:16" },
  "prompt": "<brief>"
}
```

The 3-second take on that model is the shortest and least costly that covers a 2 to 2.5 second
shot. When it fails twice, use the tool's default model with `params: { duration: 4,
generate_audio: false }` instead.

```
Style: <the style line, word for word>
Motion: <choreography only: what moves, in order>
Camera: <exactly one: locked | slow push-in | slow drift sideways | slow tilt up>
Feel: <the look's motion: drawn animation on twos | handmade stop-motion>; moving from the very first frame; figures gesture but never talk; it ends settled, still gently moving
```

- **Choreography only** in the Motion line: no style, colour or material words (the style line
  owns the look).
- **Things arrive one after another**, never all at once: "the coin drops, then the second, then
  the stack tips".
- **Landing verbs**: snaps into place, stamps down, drops with a bounce, settles.
- **One camera behaviour per shot**, stated once. Two moves in one shot read as a mess.
- **Every block has at least one named impact** (a slam, a stamp, a snap, a collapse, a whip
  hit): about one accent every 3 seconds.
- **The last shot of a block eases into a settled frame** that still breathes.
- **Never** a cut, a speech, a lip movement, words on screen, or a description of what the
  picture already shows. The style line is the one exception: repeated first, it keeps the take
  in the look instead of drifting toward a photograph. The clips are muted in the edit; the
  narration carries the sound.

## 11. Picture story frames

The Picture story mode has no motion: the cuts are the motion, so frames change fast.

- **Timing:** each frame is on screen 0.7 to 1.2 seconds, never more than 1.5. The floor is the
  narration's seconds divided by 1.5, rounded up: a 1 min video needs 45 to 70 frames, never
  fewer than about 40.
- **A new framing only when the action or the place changes.** Inside one framing, 2 to 4
  variation frames each change ONE detail (the eyes open, the brow knits, the hand lowers, the
  candle shortens). About 2 of every 3 frames are variations.
- **A new framing** is a shot picture (section 6), with the roster references.
- **A variation is an edit of the frame before it**, and that frame is its ONLY reference: no
  look reference, no figures, no places. Attaching the roster makes the model rebuild the scene,
  the most common failure of this mode.

  `generate_photo_edit`:

  ```json
  {
    "brand_id": "<brand id>",
    "include_avatar": false,
    "inputs": { "other_elements": ["<the frame before>"] },
    "params": { "aspect_ratio": "2:3" },
    "prompt": "Keep everything in @Input1 exactly as it is: the composition, the crop, the camera, the figures, the colours, the background and the style. Change only this: <one detail>."
  }
  ```

  A chain of variations edits the frame just before each one.
- **Shot sizes:** wide, medium and close-up in any order, with one ban: never two close-ups in a
  row. The strongest handoff is a close-up on the feeling, then a medium or wide shot of the
  movement it causes.
- **Every frame shows the nouns its words name.**
- Make them in waves: every new framing first, then the first variation of each, then the
  second, and so on. Each wave starts together, then is waited on.

## 12. Stock footage

- **The stock hooks library** (`list_stock_clips` with `library: "hooks"` and a `search` of one
  to three plain words): short 9:16 clips of people in everyday places (training, walking,
  driving, an office, a park, a beach, a kitchen), 2.5 to 10 seconds, mostly silent. Every search
  word must match; `matched: "some"` means the nearest were returned. Look with `preview_assets`
  (`stock_ids`). Free. Many are people reacting to the camera: for b-roll, pick the ones caught
  doing something. These are real strangers filmed as stock performers: never present one as
  the narrator, the brand or a named customer, which puts a real person's face on words they
  never said (house-rules.md, section 7, Real people).
- **The green screen library** (`library: "green_screen"`): people and animals filmed on green,
  keyed out over your scene. One reaction (a shocked cat over a surprising fact) suits an
  explainer or a top-N. Skip clips of characters someone owns.
- There are no stock clips of objects alone, empty landscapes or skylines, and no music. A
  block stock cannot show is rewritten to something it can, or the video moves to the Animated
  or Picture story mode: AI pictures are illustrated and would break a real-footage video's
  look.
- **The brand's own footage:** `list_assets` with `brand_id`, `file_type: "video"` and a
  `search`, then `preview_assets`.

## 13. Checking the pictures

`preview_assets` takes up to 8 asset ids a call and is free. For each picture:

- the same look as the look reference (line, palette, accent, background);
- no letters, numerals or garbled writing;
- every figure anonymous, matching its roster picture, and nothing that looks like the brand's
  avatar;
- the 2:3 frame, the subject in the upper middle, nothing important at the side edges;
- what its line names is in the picture, at the size and angle the plan gave it.

A picture that fails one of these gets the retry ladder or one paid redo
([checks.md](checks.md), section 6). The person may not see your previews in their chat: give
them picture links (`url` from `get_generation`) at the review stops.
