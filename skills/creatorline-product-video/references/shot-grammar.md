# Shot grammar for product videos

How a product looks when a real person films it on a phone, and how to show it being used so a
viewer believes it. Use it to write the Shots and Use lines of a brief.

## 1. The camera is a phone

- One phone, held in a hand or propped against something. Never a crew, a rig or a second camera.
- One real light source the place explains: a window, the kitchen's ceiling light, a bathroom
  vanity, the windscreen. Neutral daylight unless the person asks for evening or warm light.
- Everything in focus, the background included. Real speed. A held phone drifts a little; a
  propped one does not move at all.
- A little mess that belongs to a real room: a towel edge, a mug, a charger cable at the frame's
  edge. A spotless studio reads as an ad.
- No film words in the brief (video-briefs.md, section 5): the writer drops them, or the clip
  stops looking like a phone video.

## 2. Where the phone is

| Shot | What the frame is | Good for | Who is in it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counter | Phone propped at counter height; the product on a real surface; nothing moves but what a hand does | The intro, the result | Hands that enter |
| First person | The viewer's own eyes: one working hand rises from the bottom of the frame; the other holds the phone and is never seen | Using it: pump, pour, spray, press | One hand |
| Top-down | Phone held flat above a table, a bed, a floor | Flat lays, sets, food, unpacking | Hands |
| Close | Phone brought near one detail: the mechanism, a texture, the pour | The how-it-works moment | None, or fingertips |
| In place | The product where it lives: the shower shelf, the gym bag, the cupholder, the desk | The intro, "where it fits" | None |
| Walk-up | Handheld, a few steps toward the product | Opening on a reveal | None |
| Car | Phone on the dashboard or held from the driver's seat, daylight through the windscreen | Car accessories, drinks, things used on the go | Hands; a body on path C |
| Mirror | The avatar holds the product; the phone covers the face in the mirror | Things carried: a bag, a bottle, a case (worn is `creatorline-try-on-video`) | Path C only, one shot at most |

A mirror doubles everything in it: arms, hands, the product. Use it only on path C, for one shot,
with a matte wall around it. On paths A and B there is no mirror in the scene at all.

## 3. Distance and cuts

- Every shot at its own distance: wide (the whole surface and the room behind), medium (the
  product and the hands), close (the mechanism). A clip has at least one close and one wide.
- A change of distance or place is a cut. Getting closer inside one shot happens only as one slow
  push-in.
- Each shot lasts 3 seconds or more (video-briefs.md, section 6).
- Path B is one take: no cuts at all. Its one move is locked, a slow push-in or a slight
  handheld drift.
- A good default order for 8 to 16 s: wide in place, medium use, close mechanism, medium or
  wide result. The first and last shots differ in angle or distance.

## 4. Hands

- **First person:** one working hand. The other holds the phone, so a two-handed action never
  happens in a first-person shot.
- **Two-handed actions** (a jar lid, a tear strip, a zip): a propped phone, the hands cropped at
  the forearms.
- **Name each hand's job** and park the other: "the right hand presses the pump; the left rests
  flat on the counter".
- **Weight decides the grip.** Heavy (an appliance, a 1 L bottle, a tool): two hands, a little
  effort. Light (cosmetics, a phone): one relaxed hand. Tiny (a ring, a pill, an earbud): pinched
  between thumb and finger near the phone. When unsure, treat it as heavier.
- **Grips that look right:** a bottle by its body, never by the cap; a mug by the handle; a phone
  by its edges; earbuds by the stem; over-ear headphones by the band; a mouse under the whole
  palm; a watch on the wrist or by its strap; a laptop resting on a surface.
- **A pair of things** (earrings, dumbbells): one in each hand, or one held and one set down.
- **The same hands in a series:** write one hand line and reuse it word for word, like the
  product card: "a woman's hands, short natural nails, a thin silver ring". Describe hands by what
  is on them, never by age or looks.

## 5. Show the use honestly

- Open it before it is used: the cap comes off in one shot, the product is used in the next.
- One interaction with the product per shot.
- What is opened stays open; a cap that was set down stays on the counter.
- The result is the result of what the clip showed: the glass the pour filled, the stripe the
  vacuum cleaned, the wrist that was sprayed.
- Use it for what it is for, the way the label or the page says.
- Never a change that needs time (skin, hair, weight, a plant growing): the frame cannot show it,
  and it is a claim (house rules, section 7).
- If the person's wording puts it in the wrong place ("spray it on your palm"), use the right
  place and say so in one line.
- When unsure how it works: hold it, show the label, set it down. Showing less beats showing it
  wrong.

## 6. Opening and using, by pack

| Pack | The sequence to write |
|---|---|
| Pump bottle | Two fingers press the pump; a measured amount lands in the palm |
| Dropper | Unscrew the dropper, lift it, squeeze the bulb, drops land on a fingertip |
| Spray or mist | Cap lifted straight off and set down; one press; the mist settles on the target |
| Jar | Twist the lid off and set it down; one fingertip scoops |
| Tube | Flip the cap; squeeze a short line onto a fingertip or a brush |
| Stick (balm, lipstick, deodorant) | Pull the cap; twist up; one swipe |
| Can | Fingertip lifts the tab; the hiss; pour into a glass of ice |
| Bottle with a twist cap | Twist off; pour; set the bottle down label to the camera |
| Sachet or powder | Tear the corner; pour into the shaker or glass; shake or stir |
| Snack pack | Pull the pack open at the seam; one piece lifted out toward the camera |
| Device with a button | The thumb presses the button; the light, the screen or the motor answers |
| Small appliance | Set on the counter; lid closed; press; it runs; pour or lift out |
| Cleaning tool | The surface is dirty first; one pass; the clean stripe shows where it went |
| Candle or diffuser | A match or a switch; the flame or the mist starts; a slow look |
| Phone accessory | The phone clicks into it, or the cable clicks in; the screen lights |
| Bag, wallet, case | The zip or the clasp opens; one item goes in or comes out |

## 7. Where it goes

| Product | Where the use lands | Without a face |
|---|---|---|
| Fragrance | Wrist or the side of the neck | Wrist |
| Serum, cream, lotion | A fingertip first, then the cheek or the back of the hand | The back of the hand |
| Lip product, blush, mascara, liner | The face | A swatch on the back of the hand or the inner wrist |
| Hair oil, mask, spray | Palms, then the lengths and ends | Hands working through hair seen from behind |
| Body care | Forearm, shoulder, leg | Forearm or calf |
| Drink | Into a glass; then lifted to the mouth | The glass lifts out of the top of the frame |
| Food | A piece lifted; then a bite | The piece lifts out of the frame, or is broken in two to show the inside |
| Device or tool | Its own surface: the screw, the carpet, the desk | Same |

A mouth moving in frame while the voice speaks off screen looks wrong: keep mouths out of frame.

## 8. Size

- Give size against a hand plus a number: "fits in one palm, about 10 cm tall". Comparing it to
  another object ("about the size of a can") tends to make it come out too big.
- Size and how it opens are the two facts most often wrong when guessed (product-intake.md):
  ask when the photo cannot tell.
- If the label is too small to read, the phone comes closer. The product never gets bigger.

| Product | Usual height or width |
|---|---|
| Lipstick, balm stick | 7-9 cm |
| Dropper bottle, perfume | 8-12 cm |
| Cream jar | 6-8 cm wide |
| Squeeze tube | 12-18 cm |
| Pump bottle, 250 ml | 18-22 cm |
| Drink can | about 12 cm (slim cans about 16 cm) |
| Snack bag | 15-20 cm |
| Blender | 35-45 cm |
| Stick vacuum | 100-115 cm |

## 9. Keep the product the same

- The same words for it in every brief: the product card (product-intake.md, section 3).
- It shows only the sides its photo shows. For the back or the side, add that photo (path A takes
  up to 3 images, so the product can take two of them); otherwise the model invents it.
- One of it in frame. Nothing else of its shape nearby (a second bottle, a second can).
- Label toward the camera at the intro and the end.
- Path B: the first frame IS the still, so the product is exactly right at second zero. Make that
  still with the product photo in `other_elements`, never from words alone.

## 10. What breaks a product shot

| Avoid | Because | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Mirrors, glass tables, chrome | Hands and products double | A matte surface: wood, stone, a towel |
| Readable words on other things (receipts, screens, other packs) | They come out garbled | Plain props; real words go in as Video Studio text |
| Another brand's product or logo | It is not ours to show | Plain, unbranded props |
| The product floating, balanced on an edge, half in a bag | It reads as fake | Standing on a surface, or held |
| A second person, a crowd | Faces drift and pull focus | Hands only |
| A time jump inside one shot (full to empty) | The model cannot skip time | Put the change on a cut |
| Very close shots of skin, food, water or fur | That is where flaws show | Medium distance; the close shot on the mechanism |
| Many actions in one shot | Something gets dropped or blurred | One action per shot; more shots or fewer actions |

## 11. Sound

- The voice, the room, and two of the product's own sounds: the cap's click, the pump, the pour,
  the fizz, the zip, the button. Name them in the brief's Sound line.
- No music in the clip. Music can be added later in Video Studio (`set_sound`), and music under the
  voice garbles `change-voice`.
- Path D with a Video Studio voiceover: film the clips with `generate_audio: false`, or turn their
  sound off in the video, so the voiceover is the only voice.
