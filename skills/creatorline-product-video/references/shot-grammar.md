# Shot grammar for product videos

How a product looks when a real person films it on a phone, and how to show it being used so a
viewer believes it. Use it to write the card, the Shots line and the Use line of a brief.

## 1. The video card

Write the product card once (product-intake.md, section 3) and add these lines. Reuse the whole
card word for word in every brief and every clip, so the product never drifts.

```
Category: <exact: perfume, serum, mascara, protein powder, cordless vacuum>
Opens by: <uncap, unscrew, pull tab, flip top, press pump>
Used by: <spray, squeeze and apply, pump, swipe, brush, drop, scoop, swallow, mix>
Tier: <luxury / premium / drugstore, read from the packaging, never from a price>
Size: <against a hand, plus centimetres: "palm-sized, fits in one hand, about 15 cm">
Mechanism: <which part is where, what moves, where the product comes out: one phrase>
Has only: <what it lacks, said as what is there>
Label: <what the front shows; it renders as designed from the photo>
Detail: <one honest imperfection: a faint scuff on the cap, a fingerprint on the glass>
```

- **Opens by** is always shown before anything comes out of it.
- **Mechanism** is one fixed phrase ("the pump sits on top, presses straight down, the oil comes
  out of the short spout facing front") reused in every shot that touches it.
- **Has only**: a feature named only as missing comes back in the render. Say what is there
  instead, in the card and again in the shot that would show it: "a smooth cordless body; the
  trigger under the handle is the only control".
- The use line for the brief comes from Opens by, Used by and Mechanism.

## 2. The camera is a phone

- One phone, held or propped. Never a crew, a rig or a second camera.
- A wide phone look, everything in focus with the background sharp, a slight wideness at the
  edges but never a fisheye, flat phone exposure, a window that clips a little bright, a little
  grain in the shadows.
- One real light source the place explains: a window, the kitchen's ceiling light, the
  windscreen. Neutral daylight. Never golden hour, sunset or amber light unless asked: the writer
  may reach for golden light when none is named, so name the daylight.
- Real speed, real weight, real contact shadows under what touches a surface.
- A first-person shot may shake slightly in the hand. A propped shot and a close detail shot stay
  perfectly still. At most one small focus or exposure shift in the whole clip.
- A little mess that belongs to a real room: a towel edge, a mug, a cable at the frame's edge.
- **Put these in the Format line**: "a casual iPhone video, real texture, nothing retouched, a
  creator's own phone footage".
- **Keep these words out of the brief**: cinematic, moody, dramatic lighting, shallow depth of
  field, bokeh, DSLR, lens flare, editorial, commercial product photography, studio strobes,
  softbox, ring light, glossy or flawless finish, glowing skin, golden hour, color grade, film
  grain, slow motion, gimbal, beauty filter.

## 3. Where the phone is

| Shot | What the frame is | Good for | Who is in it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counter (propped) | Phone propped at counter height; the product on a real surface; nothing moves but what a hand does | The intro, the result, two-handed actions | Hands that enter, cropped at the forearm |
| First person | The viewer's own eyes: one working hand rises from the bottom of the frame | Using it: pump, pour, spray, press | One hand |
| Top-down | Looking straight down at a table, a bed, a floor | Flat lays, sets, food, unpacking | Hands |
| Close | Brought near one detail: the mechanism, a texture, the pour | The how-it-works moment | None, or fingertips |
| In place | The product where it lives: the shower shelf, the gym bag, the cupholder | The intro | None |
| Walk-up | Handheld, a few steps toward the product | Opening on a reveal | None |
| Car | From the dashboard or the passenger seat, daylight through the windscreen | Car accessories, drinks, things used on the go | Hands; a body on path C |

- **First-person wording**: write "seen from her own eyes, her right hand reaches in". Never write
  the phone, a mirror, a reflection, "over the shoulder" or a phone screen into a first-person
  shot: the phone then appears as an object in the frame.
- **Propped wording**: never handheld, shake, drift, wobble, sway or "subtle movement": they leak
  motion into a still frame.
- No mirror in any shot (section 14).

## 4. Distance and cuts

- Every shot at its own distance, named in the brief: tight close-up, close, medium,
  medium-wide, wide, three-quarter.
- With three shots or more, at least one tight, one middle and one wide. All in one band:
  rewrite.
- **The default order**: propped wide, propped medium, a close detail or first person, propped
  wide or medium. A clip of two shots takes two different distances.
- **Every change of distance or phone position is a cut**, never a smooth move inside a shot. A
  shot that holds two moments keeps one framing.
- At least one close shot of the mechanism.
- Each shot lasts 3 seconds or more (video-briefs.md, section 6).
- Path B is one take: no cuts at all. Its one move is locked (the default), a slow push-in or a
  slight handheld drift.
- **One opening device, optional**, only when the first shot is first person: a quick pull-back
  from a texture or the mechanism (never the label) that reveals the product in its place, or the
  focus hunting once and snapping sharp on the product as the key word lands. It settles within
  about 1.5 s and is the clip's one focus or exposure shift.

## 5. Hands

- **First person: ONE working hand.** The other holds the phone, so a two-handed action never
  happens in a first-person shot.
- **Two-handed actions** (a jar lid, a tear strip, a zip): a propped shot, the hands cropped at
  the forearms.
- **Name each hand's job** and park the other: "the right hand presses the pump; the left rests
  flat on the counter". Never more than two hand jobs at once.
- **A surface, not a third hand.** A product that needs steadying rests on the counter; a shot
  that needs a third hand grows one.
- **Steps go across cuts**: shown in one shot, opened in the next. Never piled into one beat.
- **Weight decides the grip.** Decide it before writing:

| Weight | Grip |
|---|---|
| Heavy (an appliance, a bottle of 1 L or more, a drill) | Both hands, a little visible effort |
| Bulky but light (a pillow, a box) | Both hands, relaxed |
| Light (cosmetics, a phone) | One relaxed hand |
| Tiny (an earring, a pill) | A thumb-and-finger pinch near the lens |
| Unsure | Treat it as heavier |

- **A pair** (earrings, dumbbells): one in each hand, or one held and one set down, or both on a
  surface. Never both balanced on one palm.

## 6. Grips that look right

Fingertips holding a thing up "to present it" render badly. Hold it the way it is used:

| Item | Grip |
|---|---|
| Mouse | The whole palm over it, fingers on the buttons, the heel of the hand on the pad |
| Keyboard | Hands on the home row |
| Over-ear headphones | By the headband |
| Earbuds | Pinched by the stem |
| Game controller | Both grips, thumbs on the sticks, index fingers on the triggers |
| Phone | By the side edges; the other hand swipes |
| Watch | Worn, or held by the strap |
| Tablet | Cradled under the bottom edge |
| Laptop | Resting on a surface |
| Stylus | Held like a pencil |
| Bottle | By the body, never by the cap |
| Mug | By the handle |

## 7. Safe actions by material

| Material | Actions that render | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Rigid glass, plastic, metal | Rest, cradle, present, tap, set down | Squeeze, crush or twist the body |
| Soft tube | A gentle squeeze | |
| Fabric | Wear, smooth, drape | |
| Food | Bite, pour, scoop, stir | |
| Electronics | Hold, present, point | Opening compartments, cables |
| Appliances | Grip, glide, press a button, set upright | Flipping, spinning, dropping |
| Unsure | Hold and present | |

## 8. Show the use honestly

- Open it before it is used, on screen.
- One interaction with the product per shot, and at most one change of state.
- A cap or lid that comes off is gone: never mention it again. One state per prop per beat:
  never a cap on the counter while the bottle still wears one.
- What is opened stays open, in every later shot and clip.
- Cause before effect: the result is what the uses acted on (the glass the pour filled, the
  stripe the vacuum cleaned, the wrist that was sprayed).
- The state moves across the shots: at rest, working, working differently, after.
- Use it for what it is for, the way the label or the page says.
- Never a change that needs time (skin, hair, weight, a plant growing) inside the clip: one take
  cannot show weeks passing. A before and after on screen is two takes cut together in Video
  Studio (truth-and-safety.md, section 4).
- No wording that makes an action repeat: "sprays again", "back and forth", "presses
  repeatedly".
- When unsure how it works: hold it, show the label, set it down. Showing less beats showing it
  wrong.

## 9. Opening and using, by pack

| Pack | The sequence to write |
|---|---|
| Perfume | Hold the base; lift the cap straight up (gone from then on); press the nozzle; the mist arcs to the wrist or neck |
| Spray or mist | Cap off (gone); one press; the mist settles on the target |
| Pump bottle | Two fingers press the pump; a measured amount lands in the palm |
| Dropper, serum | Unscrew the dropper, lift the pipette, squeeze the bulb, drops land on a fingertip |
| Jar | Twist the lid off (gone); one fingertip scoops |
| Tube | Flip or unscrew the cap; squeeze onto a fingertip or a brush |
| Stick (balm, lipstick, deodorant) | Pull the cap; twist the base up; one swipe |
| Compact | Flip the hinged lid (it stays attached); tap the sponge |
| Can | Fingertip lifts the tab; the hiss; pour into a glass of ice |
| Bottle with a twist cap | Twist off; pour; set the bottle down label to the camera |
| Sachet or powder | Tear the corner; pour into the shaker or glass; shake or stir |
| Snack pack | Pull the pack open at the seam; one piece lifted out toward the camera |
| Device with a button | The thumb presses the button; the light, the screen or the motor answers |
| Blender | Set it on the base; lid secured; press; the contents spin; pour |
| Vacuum | Grip; trigger; glide across the surface; release |
| Drill | Grip; line the bit up; squeeze the trigger |
| Cleaning tool | The surface is visibly dirty first; one pass; the clean stripe shows where it went |
| Candle or diffuser | A match or a switch; the flame or the mist starts |
| Phone accessory | The phone clicks into it; the screen lights |
| Bag, wallet, case | The zip or the clasp opens; one item goes in or comes out |

## 10. Where it goes

The use lands on its own place. A wrong place in the person's wording ("spray it on your palm")
is quietly corrected.

| Product | Where the use lands | When no face may show |
|---|---|---|
| Perfume, mist | Wrist or neck, never the palm or the face | Wrist |
| Cream, serum | A fingertip first, then the face or the hands | The back of the hand |
| Lipstick, balm | The lips only | A tight crop of the lips, the mouth closed between swipes; a face that keeps appearing: a swatch on the back of the hand |
| Powder, blush | The cheek, with a brush | The brush loading on the pan |
| Mascara | The lashes | A tight crop of one eye and its lashes; a face that keeps appearing: the wand and its brush, close |
| Eyeliner | The lash line | A tight crop of one eye; a face that keeps appearing: a line drawn on the back of the hand |
| Foundation | A fingertip or a sponge, then the face | A swatch on the back of the hand |
| Hair product | The mid-lengths to the ends | Hands working it through the ends, seen from behind |
| Drink | To the mouth | A tight crop at the lips, or the glass lifting out of the top of the frame |
| Food | To the mouth | A piece broken in two, or lifted out of the frame |
| Anything not for the body | Its own surface: the carpet, the screw, the jug's contents | Same |

## 11. Size

- Size against a hand plus centimetres: "palm-sized, fits in one hand, about 15 cm". Comparing
  it to another object ("the size of a water bottle") makes it come out too big.
- Size and how it opens are the two facts most often wrong when guessed: ask when the photo
  cannot tell.
- If the label is too small to read, the phone comes closer. The product never gets bigger.

| Product | Usual height or width |
|---|---|
| Perfume | 10-12 cm |
| Large cologne | 13-16 cm |
| Serum dropper bottle | 8-10 cm |
| Cream jar | 6-8 cm wide |
| Soft tube | 12-18 cm |
| Lipstick | 7-9 cm |
| Mascara, lip gloss | 10-12 cm |
| Pump bottle, 250 ml | 18-22 cm |
| Compact | 7-10 cm wide |
| Mist bottle | 15-20 cm |
| Drink can | about 12 cm (slim cans about 16 cm) |
| Snack bag | 15-20 cm |
| Cordless vacuum | 100-115 cm |
| Blender | 35-45 cm |
| Drill | 25-30 cm |

## 12. People in frame

- A person is never the subject: no full-body portrait, no centred selfie. Only hands, a
  first-person forearm, a body cropped below the face, or a small figure far away. One person at
  most.
- Silent: the mouth is closed whenever any part of the face shows. A mouth moving under an
  off-screen voice gets lip-synced to it.
- The same gender as the voice, the same in every shot and every clip.
- Described only by what they are and wear: "an adult woman's hands", "a grey knit sleeve at the
  wrist". Never by age, ethnicity, looks or makeup, and never "girl", "boy", "kid", "young",
  "teen".
- For a series, one hand line reused word for word, like the card.

## 13. Keep the product the same

- The same card in every brief (section 1).
- **Angle lock**: the product shows only the sides its photo shows. The camera may move; the
  product never turns to reveal a side nobody photographed. With several angles in `image_urls`
  (path A takes up to 3 images, so the product can take two), switch angle only on a cut.
- "Exactly one <product> in frame", in those words: the model clones the hero. Nothing else of
  its shape nearby (a second bottle, a second can).
- The label renders as designed from a real photo: never ask for it turned away. Label toward the
  camera at the intro and the end.
- The brand's name is on the product and on nothing else in the scene.
- Path B: the first frame IS the still, so the product is exactly right at second zero. Make that
  still with the product photo in `other_elements`, never from words alone.

## 14. What breaks a product shot

| Avoid | Because | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Mirrors, glass tables, chrome, any reflective surface | Hands, bodies and products double | A matte surface: wood, stone, a towel |
| Readable words or numbers on other things (receipts, screens, price tags, other packs) | They come out garbled | Plain props; numbers live in the voice, words in Video Studio text |
| Another brand's product, logo or character | It is not ours to show | Plain, unbranded props |
| The product floating, balanced on an edge, wedged, half in a bag | It reads as fake | Standing on a surface, or held |
| A second person, a crowd | Faces drift and pull focus | Hands only, one person at most |
| A time jump inside one shot (full to empty) | The model cannot skip time | Put the change on a cut |
| Very close shots of skin, food, water or fur | That is where flaws show | Medium distance; the close shot on the mechanism |
| Many actions in one shot | Something gets dropped or blurred | One action per shot; more shots or fewer actions |

## 15. Sound

- The voice, the room, and two of the product's own sounds: the cap's click, the pump, the pour,
  the fizz, the zip, the button. Name them in the brief's Sound line.
- No bracketed sounds in the voiceover (a laugh, a gasp): those are sounds a person on camera
  makes.
- No music in the clip unless asked (SKILL.md, The brief). Music can go on later in Video Studio
  (`set_sound`), and music under the voice garbles `change-voice`.
- Path D with a Video Studio voiceover: film the clips with `generate_audio: false`, or turn their
  sound off in the video, so the voiceover is the only voice.
