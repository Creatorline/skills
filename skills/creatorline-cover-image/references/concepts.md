## 1. The one rule

Every cover opens an **information gap**: it raises a question that only the video answers.
And it stays **true to the video**: it may exaggerate, it never misrepresents. A cover that
misleads costs the viewer's trust in the next one.

It has to work in **under one second at about 120 px wide**: a tile in a profile grid, a
search result, the column beside a YouTube video. If it does not read at that size, the
composition is wrong, and a sharper image will not save it; a simpler concept will.

The rules every framework serves:

- one clear focal subject;
- a strong information gap;
- high contrast, readable at about 120 px wide;
- emotion or intrigue on any face;
- no clutter;
- true to the video.

## 2. Picking a concept

1. Read what the video offers: its hook line, its best moment, its product, its number, its
   verdict.
2. Brainstorm **at least five concepts** across the frameworks in section 3, for yourself.
   Keep the one with the strongest information gap and the fewest elements.
3. Frameworks combine (a posed portrait over a map, a product with a size difference) when the
   mix deepens the gap without clutter and still reads at 120 px.
4. When the person names what they want ("me looking shocked", "a before and after"), that is
   the concept. Fill in the rest from the video.
5. With nothing in the video pointing elsewhere: a Posed portrait (framework 5).

## 3. The sixteen frameworks

| # | Framework | What it shows | How it is made here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before and after | One subject in two states, the contrast between them at its strongest | A split layout in before-and-after mode (section 8). Only a change the video shows |
| 2 | Social message | A generic chat bubble, DM row or comment card beside the subject, with a short message | Key elements block, the message as baked text (headline text, section 5). Only a real message the person supplies (the comment a reply video answers, a DM they got); generic styling, no platform's name or logo |
| 3 | Three steps | Three vertical panels: the start, a middle beat, the end | A split layout, three panels; a small number per panel only when asked |
| 4 | The real frame | A real moment of the video itself, not a generation | The video's own opening, or the cover put in front of it ([using the cover](using-the-cover.md), section 3); or a Posed action that matches the video's first frame. Say so when the person has the source clip |
| 5 | Posed portrait | The subject LARGE, filling much of the frame, one strong expression, very little behind them: the subject is the focus | The default: Subjects block with the identity lock, the lighting rig and the expression |
| 6 | Posed action | One intriguing action, frozen mid-moment | Scene block = the action; an uncrowded composition with one hero action |
| 7 | Highlighted day | Any framework plus a big "DAY N" badge | The badge as baked text; pick a day from the last fifth of the story |
| 8 | Graphic | A clean chart or diagram: a curve, a simple familiar chart | A non-photoreal medium ([cover briefs](cover-briefs.md), section 7): no photo frame, no lighting rig |
| 9 | Landscape | The place is the hero, one small subject on a third | Location and Composition blocks; the lighting rig is optional |
| 10 | Map or aerial | A map or an aerial view with a highlighted route, pin or circled point | Key elements block; a place name only as baked text |
| 11 | Product | The product as the hero, sharp, and as the answer to the title's question | The product photo in `other_elements` (its own label may show) |
| 12 | Added text | Words as a callout (an arrow and one word), or words that continue or answer the title | The words baked in, or typed later ([headline text](headline-text.md)) |
| 13 | Repetition | A great many of one object filling the frame, still legible, with one context element and a subject for scale | Key elements block |
| 14 | Size difference | An extreme giant-versus-tiny contrast between two things central to the story | Composition block |
| 15 | News strip | A generic "breaking news" strip with one short line over the image | Baked text, generic broadcaster styling. Only the brand's own real news (a launch, a restock); never a real channel's name, never a hint that the press covered it. On 9:16 the strip sits above the bottom quarter |
| 16 | Amplified reality | A posed action with ONE real element of the story exaggerated | That element oversized in Key elements; keep it plausible, since over-amplifying kills trust |

Frameworks 1, 5 and 12 are the everyday ones. The rest are picked here and made through the
same brief blocks: never a different kind of request.

**A series card** (ours) fixes one layout for every episode (section 10).

| Tutorial | 3 Three steps | 12 Added text (a callout) | "3 STEPS" |

| Reshoot of a trend | 4 The real frame | 6 Posed action | the trend's own question |

## 5. The words

- **None by default.** The image is clean unless the person asks for words, gives them, or
  picks a framework built on words (2, 7, 12, 15, or 10 with a place name). Compose with one
  quarter of the frame free anyway, so a headline can go on later.
- **2 to 4 words**, up to 6 only when they are the person's own. A headline, never the title, a
  sentence or the caption.
- **ALL CAPS**, digits for numbers ("3", not "three"), one mark at most (`?` or `!`). No
  hashtags, handles, links, emoji or "link in bio".
- **The brand's language** (`language` from `get_brand`), the same as the video's, unless the
  person asks for another. Letters with marks (ş, ğ, ü, é, ñ) and other scripts come out wrong
  more often when drawn: check each letter, or type them ([headline text](headline-text.md),
  section 6).
- **YouTube**: the thumbnail's words add to the title, never repeat it. A title "I tried the
  cheapest phone" pairs with "IT WORKS?", not "CHEAPEST PHONE".
- **The person's own words win.** Use them as given, in capitals, unless the video does not
  keep their promise or section 9 says otherwise; then say so in the plan.

| Pattern | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Number and noun | "3 MISTAKES" | lists, tips, tutorials |
| The question | "WORTH IT?" | reviews, first looks |
| The warning | "DON'T DO THIS" | mistakes, how-tos |
| The contrast | "$10 VS $100" | comparisons |
| The reveal | "WHAT'S INSIDE?" | unboxings, reveals |
| Time or place | "6 AM ROUTINE" | routines, vlogs |
| The verdict | "FINALLY" | a moment the video lands on |

How the words look and where they go: [headline text](headline-text.md).

## 6. Expressions

Eleven expressions, each written as what the face does. Put the wording in the brief's
Subjects block, after the identity lock.

| Expression | Write in the brief |
|---|---|
| shock | a gasp, mouth open, eyes wide, eyebrows high |
| hype | an ecstatic open grin, eyes blazing with excitement |
| rage | teeth bared, brows pulled down hard, a furious glare |
| awe | jaw dropped in wonder, eyes wide, looking up at something |
| laugh | laughing hard, head thrown back, eyes creased |
| fear | a terrified, frozen stare, eyes wide, shoulders pulled up |
| smug | a knowing smirk, one corner of the mouth raised, eyes a little narrowed |
| charisma | a calm, magnetic gaze into the lens with a faint, composed half smile |
| confusion | one eyebrow raised high, head tilted, lips pressed to one side |
| determination | jaw locked, eyes fixed on the lens with laser focus |
| disgust | recoiling, nose wrinkled, upper lip pulled up in a grimace |

- **The default** with a person in frame: shock. For a brand whose voice is calm or premium
  (`brand_voice.tone`): charisma, the calm positive option (determination reads harsh).
- **A count with no names** ("4 versions"): take them in this order, shock, hype, rage, awe,
  laugh, fear, smug, charisma, confusion, determination, disgust. For a calm or premium brand
  voice, skip rage, fear and disgust: the brand's voice is the person's standing ask.
- **The person's own words** for a feeling replace the preset, word for word.
- **When you offer expressions to choose from**, always add a free answer ("Other: tell me
  the feeling").
- Eyes on the lens make contact; eyes on the product send the viewer's eyes there.

## 7. Camera takes and variant sets

| Take | The line added to the Subjects block |
|---|---|
| 1 | The framing as designed |
| 2 | Low-angle hero: the camera below eye level, the subject towering over the viewer |
| 3 | Extreme close-up: the face fills more than half the frame, the background melted into soft blur |
| 4 | A wider shot with a slight tilt of the frame, the subject off-centre on a third, more of the place and more movement |

Every take adds "the same scene and lighting".

- **Variants = expressions times takes**, 16 at most. With nobody in frame, variants are takes
  only.
- Variant briefs differ ONLY in the expression line, the take line, or both. Every other block
  stays word for word, so the set compares one thing.
- **A set of 4**, the suggestion when the person wants options: one concept in four
  expressions (in the order of section 6) at take 1; or, when the feeling is fixed, one
  expression in takes 1 to 4.

## 8. Split layouts

- **Only on request**: the person asks for a layout (split, before and after, versus, side by
  side), or the reference cover they want matched is split. A topic "X vs Y" stays ONE unified
  frame with both subjects in it.
- Two states: two halves. Three or more: vertical panels with clean, bold seams, each panel a
  complete small scene, one grade across all of them.
- Modes: **plain panels**; **before and after** (the strongest contrast between two states of
  one change); **versus** (each side lit like a fight poster, equal weight); **custom**.
- No labels, words or numbers on or between the panels, unless the person asked for short,
  true ones.
- The brief: the split replaces the Frame block's "one unified frame" ([cover briefs](cover-briefs.md),
  section 6).

## 9. The persona on a cover

Turn up the feeling, never promise what the video does not deliver. The avatar's verdict makes
strong cover words whenever the video lands on one.

| Asked for | Do |
|---|---|
| "#1 SERUM" | As given. With an approved-claims list, only its words. A number is typed, not drawn ([headline text](headline-text.md), section 6) |
| A message, comment, review or rating | Typed over the clean cover, or the person's own screenshot: drawn in, small words come out garbled |
| A news strip about something that did not happen | none: a news look reads as a real outlet reporting it |
| "DAY 100" when there is no hundredth day | the real episode or day number, or none |
| Another brand's product, logo or interface as a prop | a plain, unbranded item; generic styling |
| A celebrity or a real person's face | the avatar, or the person's own photo with their consent |
| A before and after | When the video shows the change: a split in before-and-after mode (section 8) |

When you change the person's words, say so in one line in the plan, with the reason: "The
video is her first try, so 30 DAYS LATER would not be kept; I'd write WORTH IT? instead."

## 10. A series

A series is recognized by its covers before anyone reads them.

- Fix once: the frame, which side the face is on, the word look and colour, the background
  colour family, and the corner that carries the episode number ("EP 4", "DAY 12").
- Keep the series' brief in the conversation and reuse it word for word. Change only the
  words, the expression and the number.
- One colour per series, or one per content pillar, so a grid of covers reads as sorted.
- The same avatar in every cover is Creatorline's edge: no face photos, no drift between
  shoots.
