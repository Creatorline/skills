# Cover concepts

What a cover shows and what it says. Pick the concept here first, then write the brief with

## 1. The one rule

A cover wins the tap by raising a question that only the video answers, and it has to do it in
under a second, at the size of a thumb: a tile in a profile grid, a search result, the column
beside a YouTube video. Everything else serves that.

- One subject the eye lands on first: a face, a product, a number.
- One feeling on the face.
- 2 to 5 words that name the topic or ask the question.
- A background that stays out of the way.
- A promise the video keeps.

A cover that needs a second look has the wrong idea. A sharper image will not save it; a
simpler concept will.

## 2. Picking a concept

1. Read what the video has to offer: its hook line, its best moment, its product, its number,
   its verdict.
2. Think of three concepts from section 3 that could carry it. Keep the one with the clearest
   question and the fewest elements.
3. Two concepts may be combined while there is still one subject: a big number with the face
   reacting to it, a product close up with a pointer. Never three.
4. When the person names what they want ("my avatar looking shocked"), that is the concept.
   Fill in everything else from the video.

## 3. The concepts

**Big face.** The avatar's face fills about half the frame with one strong feeling. The words
name what caused it.
- Words: the topic or the trigger. "RENT IN 2026", "THIS IS WILD".
- Fits: talking videos, hot takes, reactions, storytime. The default when nothing else stands out.
- Watch: the feeling must match the video's own moment. A shocked face on a calm tutorial lies.

**Open question.** The words ask, or start a sentence the video finishes. The picture shows one
clue.
- Words: "WORTH IT?", "DON'T SKIP STEP 2", "WHY IT WORKS".
- Fits: reviews, explainers, tips.
- Watch: the video must answer it, early. A question the video never answers burns the viewer.

**Big number.** A number is the hero, in digits, as big as the face.
- Words: "3 MISTAKES", "5 MIN DINNER", "$12 OUTFIT".
- Fits: tutorials, lists, tips, budget content.
- Watch: the number must be in the video. Prices and amounts only when they are real.

**Product close up.** The product sits large in the foreground, held toward the lens or on a
surface, with the avatar reacting behind it. Without a person, the product alone
(`include_avatar: false`).
- Words: a verdict or a question. "WORTH IT?", "FIRST LOOK".
- Fits: reviews, demos, unboxings, product videos, faceless product content.
- Watch: the product comes from a real photo ([product intake](product-intake.md)). Its label
  may blur or misspell at this size; the cover words carry the name when it matters.

**Before and after.** Two halves of one frame: the same subject in two states.
- Words: "BEFORE" and "AFTER", or one word for the change. "10 MIN MAKEOVER".
- Fits: a look, a room, a desk, an outfit, a recipe: a change the video shows on camera.
- Watch: only a change the video really shows. Never a body, skin, health or weight result
  for an avatar.

**This or that.** Two options side by side with equal weight, the avatar between them or
pointing at one.
- Words: "A OR B?", "$10 VS $100", "CHEAP VS PRICEY".
- Fits: comparisons, choices, "which one would you pick".
- Watch: the brand's own products or plain generic items only. Never another brand's product or
  logo (house rules, section 7).

**Frozen moment.** The most interesting instant of the video, stopped: the lid lifting, the
twirl, the first bite, the reveal.
- Words: the question the moment raises. "WHAT'S INSIDE?", "WAIT FOR IT".
- Fits: unboxings, try-ons, reshoots of a trend, character swaps, recipes.
- Watch: describe the moment as the video has it: same outfit, same place, same product.

**Statement.** A bold opinion in big words that take half the frame. The avatar is smaller,
with a calm, sure look.
- Words: the take in 3 to 5 words. "STOP BUYING FAST FASHION".
- Fits: hot takes, advice, faceless channels (the words alone over a scene).
- Watch: an opinion is fine; a fact claim needs the video to back it.

**Pointer.** The avatar points at one detail, which carries a bold circle or arrow. One or two
words.
- Words: "THIS", "STOP", "DO THIS INSTEAD".
- Fits: tutorials, mistakes, app promos, "the one thing".
- Watch: one arrow, one detail. Two arrows are clutter.

**Setting hero.** The place is the star: a city, a kitchen, a gym. The avatar is smaller, on
one side.
- Words: the place or the time. "6 AM IN TOKYO", "MY DESK SETUP".
- Fits: day in the life, travel, routines, faceless scenes.
- Watch: keep the avatar's face big enough to read; otherwise drop the person entirely.

**Series card.** One fixed layout for every episode of a series; only the words, the expression
and the episode number change (section 8).
- Fits: AI host episodes, recurring formats, a content plan's pillar.

**The video's own first frame.** No new image: the cover words go onto the video's opening
frame in Video Studio, exact and free apart from the export
([using the cover](using-the-cover.md), section 4).
- Fits: a video whose first frame already shows the avatar's face clearly.

## 4. Which concept for which video

| The video | First choice | Second choice | Words, for example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talking video, hot take | Big face | Statement | the take in 3 words |
| Product review or demo | Product close up | Open question | "WORTH IT?" |
| Unboxing | Frozen moment (the reveal) | Product close up | "WHAT'S INSIDE?" |
| Try-on, outfit | Frozen moment (the look) | Before and after | "3 WAYS TO WEAR IT" |
| Tutorial | Big number | Pointer | "3 STEPS" |
| App or website promo | Pointer | Open question | the outcome the app gives |
| Reshoot of a trend | Frozen moment | Big face | the trend's own question |
| Character Swap | Frozen moment | Big face | what happens in it |
| AI host episode | Series card | Big face | the episode's topic |
| Faceless video | Statement | Setting hero | the story's question |
| Clip from a podcast or long video | Big face | Statement | the clip's sharpest line, cut to 4 words |
| Photo carousel | Big number | Open question | "5 IDEAS FOR ..." |

## 5. The words

- **2 to 5 words**, never the full title or the caption. One line, or two short lines.
- **Digits for numbers**: "3", not "three".
- **One mark at most**: a `?` or a `!`. No hashtags, handles, links, emoji or "link in bio".
- **The brand's language** (`language` from `get_brand`), the same as the video's, unless the
  person asks for another. Letters with marks (ş, ğ, ü, é, ñ) and non-Latin scripts come out
  wrong more often: check each letter.
- **YouTube**: the thumbnail's words add to the title, never repeat it. A title "I tried the
  cheapest phone" pairs with the words "IT WORKS?", not "CHEAPEST PHONE".
- **The person's own words win.** Use them as given, in capitals, unless they break the claim
  floor (section 7). Cut them to 5 words only with a yes, and say so in the plan.

| Pattern | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Number and noun | "3 MISTAKES" | lists, tips, tutorials |
| The question | "WORTH IT?" | reviews, first looks |
| The warning | "DON'T DO THIS" | mistakes, how-tos |
| The contrast | "$10 VS $100" | comparisons |
| The reveal | "WHAT'S INSIDE?" | unboxings, reveals |
| Time or place | "6 AM ROUTINE" | routines, vlogs |
| The verdict | "FINALLY" | a moment the video lands on |

## 6. Faces

Write the expression as what the face does, not as a label. Use the wording below in the
brief's Person line.

| Feeling | Write | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Shock | eyes wide, eyebrows high, mouth open in a gasp | reveals, surprising facts |
| Delight | a huge open smile, eyes bright, leaning toward the lens | good news, a win, food |
| Curiosity | one eyebrow raised, a small half smile, head tilted | questions, mysteries |
| Doubt | lips pressed together, eyes narrowed, a sideways look | "worth it?", myths |
| Confidence | a calm, direct look into the lens with a slight knowing smile | advice, statements |
| Laughter | laughing, head tipped back, eyes creased | funny moments |
| Focus | concentrating, eyes on the product in their hands | tutorials, demos |
| Awe | jaw dropped, eyes wide, looking up at something | places, big reveals |
| Worry | teeth clenched in a grimace, eyes wide, pulling back | mistakes, warnings |

- Eyes on the lens make contact; eyes on the product send the viewer's eyes there.
- A brand whose voice is calm or premium (`brand_voice.tone`) gets confidence, delight or focus.
  Leave shock and worry for brands that talk that way.
- For several takes of one cover with different feelings, pick in this order and skip what the
  brand's tone rules out: shock, delight, curiosity, doubt, confidence, laughter.

## 7. Truth on a cover

Turn up the feeling, never the facts. A cover promises only what the video delivers, and it
follows the claim floor (house rules, section 7): the avatar has no past and no results.

| Asked for | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "I tried it for 30 days" with the avatar | "WORTH IT?" or "BEFORE YOU BUY" |
| "IT CLEARED MY SKIN" | "WHY IT SUITS OILY SKIN" |
| "I LOST 5 KG" | "WHAT'S IN IT?" |
| "#1 SERUM", "DOCTOR APPROVED" | only when it is on the approved claims list, word for word |
| A fake comment, review card, rating or news banner | the real one the person supplies, or none |
| Another brand's product or logo as the "bad" option | a plain, unbranded item |
| A celebrity or a real person's face | the avatar, or the person's own photo with their consent |

When you rewrite the person's words, say so in one line in the plan, with the reason: "Mia
cannot say she used it for 30 days, so I'd write WORTH IT? instead."

## 8. A series

A series is recognized by its covers before anyone reads them.

- Fix once: the frame, which side the face is on, the word style and colour, the background
  colour family, and the corner that carries the episode number ("EP 4", "DAY 12").
- Keep the series' brief in the conversation and reuse it word for word. Change only the words,
  the expression and the number.
- One colour per series, or one per content pillar, so a grid of covers reads as sorted.
- The same avatar in every cover is Creatorline's edge: no face photos, no drift between shoots.
