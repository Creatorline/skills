## 1. The spine of an episode

| Time | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 s | Hook | The host, mid-sentence, on the most specific line of the episode. Hook text on screen. |
| 3 to 8 s | The road | What the episode gives and how long the road is: "three phrases", "two fees and the one nobody checks". Series tag as text. |
| Parts | The content | One concrete takeaway per part: the actual phrase, the actual setting, the actual step. Each part opens on its own small hook. |
| Near 12 s, near 30 s | Re-hooks | Something changes: a new part, a cutaway, a label. Viewers decide again at these points. |
| 60 to 75 % in | Payoff | The strongest item, or the answer to the hook, with something not said yet. |
| Last 5 to 8 s | Verdict, then sign-off | A sentence about the topic, then the series' fixed closing words. The call to action is text and caption. |

Rules that hold at every length:

- The hook is never "hi", the show's name, or "in today's episode".
- Strongest item last in a list, or the answer at the payoff point: never spend the best part
  first and coast.
- Every promise the hook makes is kept in words: "three phrases" means three phrases said out
  loud, not "three tips" and a vague middle.
- No ad section. When a product belongs in the episode, it appears where it does the thing, in
  the same breath as the thing.

## 2. Formats that suit a host

Pick one from the topic and state it in the plan. Use the same format for every episode of a
series.

| Format | Fits | Shape | Parts at 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| Countdown | Tips, mistakes, tools, phrases | Hook names the count → one item per part, best last → verdict | 3 + sign-off |
| Explainer | How something works, why something happens | A surprising fact → how it works in two or three steps → what it means for the viewer | 3 + sign-off |
| Myth or fact | Common beliefs in the niche | The myth as people say it → the truth → why → what to do instead | 3 + sign-off |
| Question answered | An audience question | The question (a real comment card only if the person gave one) → the short answer → the why → one thing to try | 2 to 3 + sign-off |
| Weekly recap | What happened in the niche | The biggest item first → two quick items → the host's take | 3 to 4 + sign-off |
| This or that | Two options compared | The question → option A → option B → the verdict and who each suits | 3 + sign-off |
| Example story | A lesson told through a case | Open on the turn → the setup → what changed → the lesson | 3 + sign-off |

- Recaps and explainers with numbers need sources (section 8). Without them, choose another
  format.
- An example story is framed as an example ("Say you're in a meeting and...") or is about a real,
  checkable event. Never "I did this".
- A product walkthrough in steps belongs to creatorline-tutorial-video; a single review clip to
  creatorline-talking-video.

## 3. Hooks for hosted episodes

One line, 12 words or fewer, said inside 3 seconds, already mid-thought.

| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| The count | "Three phrases that make you sound sure of yourself in any meeting." |
| The mistake | "Stop starting your emails with 'just checking in'." |
| The contrarian line | "Your to-do list is the reason nothing gets done." |
| The viewer's own question | "Why does every meeting run fifteen minutes over?" |
| The stakes | "This one sentence decides how your raise conversation goes." |
| The swap | "Say 'my recommendation is' instead of 'I think maybe'." |

Weak hooks: a greeting, a topic label ("Let's talk about meetings"), a vague promise ("this will
change your life"), a statistic with no source.

## 4. Cutting the script into parts

1. Write the whole script first, in the brand's voice and language.
2. Count the words. About 2.5 spoken words per second of episode; video-briefs.md, section 7,
   has the budget table. Over budget: cut words, or add a part and tell the person.
3. Cut into parts of 12 to 25 s at complete thoughts, where the episode turns: the next item,
   the answer, the verdict. Never split a sentence.
4. Each part's `duration` = its words / 2.5, rounded up, + 1 second for the settle. Keep it
   between 5 and 30.
5. The sign-off is its own short part (about 5 s) so it can be reused (series-bible.md).
6. Mark the cutaway moments: one per 12 to 15 s of talk, on concrete nouns (the thing shown),
   never in the first 3 s or over the sign-off.

Why parts of 12 to 25 s: a flaw in one part costs one re-take, not the episode, and each part
has room for a few shots without rushing the words. Longer parts mean fewer seams; shorter parts
mean cheaper retries. Inside one part, the server's writer chooses the shots itself.

## 5. Hand-offs between parts

End every part but the last on a bridge that opens a small loop; open the next part by paying
it:

| End of part | Start of next part |
|---|---|
| "...and phrase two is the one most people get backwards." | "Phrase two." |
| "That's the cheap fix. The real one takes ten seconds." | "Here's the ten-second fix." |
| "But that's not even the strange part." | "The strange part:" |
| "So which one should you pick? Depends on one thing." | "The one thing is..." |

Keep bridges to one short sentence. The bridge is said with energy still up, not as an ending.

## 6. Endings and sign-offs

- **Verdict**: one sentence about what the episode showed ("Small words, and they change how
  the room hears you."). No new claim, no new number.
- **Sign-off**: the series' fixed words, 8 words or fewer, the same every episode ("That's Speak
  Up Sundays. See you next Sunday.").
- **Call to action**: on-screen text over the sign-off and in the post caption ("New episode
  every Sunday", "Follow for part 2"). Spoken calls to action are rewritten into verdicts by the
  server's writer anyway.

## 7. Writing the lines

- Speech, not prose: contractions, short sentences, one idea per sentence.
- Concrete over clever: the actual phrase, the actual step, the actual name.
- Numbers as people say them ("fifteen percent", "about two minutes").
- The brand's voice from `get_brand`: keep its do's, never write a don't.
- Skip filler the writer removes anyway ("literally", "game changer", "obsessed").
- Counting is said, never shown on fingers: write "Number one." and let the host nod.
- A line that shows how a word sounds is written as it is said, slowly ("con... fi... dent").
- Spoken lines in the brand's language (or the one the person asks for); the brief around them
  in English.

## 8. Facts and claims

- The host says only what the person gave, what the brand document holds, or what a source you
  checked and can name supports. Anything else stays out of the script and off the screen.
- A number on screen comes from that same source. Name the source to the person in the plan.
- Recaps: only items the person confirmed or a source you can show them. Never invent a
  headline, a quote, a figure or a date.
- Health, money, legal topics: general information only, no promises of results.
- The claim floor in house-rules.md, section 7, applies to every line: no personal results, no
  credentials, no invented reviews or comments.

## 9. Example: a 60 s episode in parts

Show "Speak Up Sundays", host Mia, format Countdown, brand voice: calm, direct, no jargon.

| Part | Words | `duration` | Lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, hook and phrase one | 47 | 20 | "Three phrases that make you sound sure of yourself in any meeting. Number one. Swap 'I think maybe we could' for 'My recommendation is.' Same idea, but now it sounds like a decision. Try it once this week. Phrase two is the one most people get backwards." |
| 2, phrase two | 40 | 17 | "Phrase two. Stop opening with 'Sorry, quick question.' Open with the question. 'What's the deadline for the launch?' No apology, no warm-up. You sound like someone who belongs in the room. And the last one ends meetings in your favor." |
| 3, phrase three and verdict | 46 | 20 | "Phrase three. When the room goes quiet, say 'So the next step is...' and name it. Whoever names the next step usually owns it. Three phrases: my recommendation is, the question itself, and the next step. Small words, and they change how the room hears you." |
| Sign-off | 8 | 5 | "That's Speak Up Sundays. See you next Sunday." |

About 62 s filmed, about 59 s after the seams are trimmed. Four cutaways: a hand closing a
notebook on a meeting table (over "sounds like a decision"), an empty meeting room in morning
light (over "belongs in the room"), a wall clock in a quiet office (over "When the room goes
quiet"), a hand pressing a blank sticky note onto a desk (over "usually owns it"). Texts:
the hook line, the series tag, the labels "1. My recommendation is", "2. Ask the question",
"3. The next step is...", and "New episode every Sunday" over the sign-off.

The briefs for these parts are in host-continuity.md, section 5.
